Question and source

matrix that ensure d_Q = d_C for its symmetric lifted-product CSS code.
Raveendran, Declercq and Vasic, On the Minimum Distances of Finite-Length
Lifted Product Quantum LDPC Codes, arXiv:2503.07567v1, 10 March 2025,

https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07567v1#S6

Answer contract
Source question -> a nonvacuous, checkable sufficient condition on an explicit
family of base exponent matrices -> a proof of both distance inequalities,
including nontrivial logical witnesses and exclusion of all lighter logicals.
A condition that merely restates d_Q >= d_C is not an answer. Nor is excluding
only the paper's displayed weight-(m+n) patterns, finding a heavy logical
representative, or observing no failure in sampled decoding.

Proposed first research block (not executed by this selection task)
Restrict initially to normalized 3-by-4 monomial bases over cyclic lifts.
Use lift lengths 6 through 24 as a bounded discovery window, with exhaustive
versus sampled scope frozen before running a search. No claim of exhaustive
enumeration of that window is intended. First reproduce small source examples
and audit the LP matrix convention. Then test the following OUR hypothesis:
absence of the source's RCPC patterns, together with girth at least eight in
the expanded classical base graph, suffices for d_Q = d_C in this family.
The hypothesis is a diagnostic restriction of the source question, not an
author-posed conjecture or an asserted theorem.

Stop this first block at a fully verified counterexample to that hypothesis,
or a structural sufficient-condition theorem for a clearly specified nonempty
subfamily, or a documented feasibility failure at the frozen search limit.
Counterexample success requires an exact classical-distance lower certificate
strictly above the weight of a quantum logical witness. The witness must have
zero opposite syndrome and lie outside the entire stabilizer row span.
It need not establish the exact quantum distance. A counterexample refutes our
hypothesis; the general sufficient-condition problem remains open.
Do not silently replace this with another decoder campaign or a girth theorem.

Precise objects and intended formal interface
Let L >= 2, R = F_2[x]/(x^L-1), and E : Fin 3 -> Fin 4 -> ZMod L.
Normalize E(0,j)=E(i,0)=0 and set B_ij=x^(E_ij) in R. These zeros denote
identity circulants, not absent edges. Set B*=transpose with x -> x^(-1).
Use the source's equation (9), with B1=B2=B:
  HX = expand([B tensor I_4 | I_3 tensor B*]),
  HZ = expand([I_4 tensor B | B* tensor I_3]).
Both have 12L rows and 25L columns. The classical matrix expand(B) has
3L rows and 4L columns. All weights and row spans are binary after expansion.
d_C minimizes weight over nonzero ker(expand(B));
d_X minimizes over ker(HZ) minus rowspan(HX), and d_Z swaps X and Z;
d_Q=min(d_X,d_Z). Require positive CSS dimension when discussing distance.

Lean interface sketch, NOT a compiled statement:
  distance_preserved_of_base_constraints
    (L : Nat) (hL : 2 <= L) (E : Fin 3 -> Fin 4 -> ZMod L)
    (normalized : Normalized E) (constraints : DesignCondition E)
    (nontrivial : PositiveCSSDimension (sourceLP E)) :
    quantumDistance (sourceLP E) = classicalDistance (expandBase E)
DesignCondition is deliberately not invented during selection. Discover and
state it explicitly before treating this signature as a mathematical theorem.
Boundary checks: repeated rows/columns, deficient ranks, even L, self-pairs in
odd RCPC partitions, vanishing logical quotient, and stabilizer cancellations.
Do not import a full-row-rank assumption without testing it: evaluating a fully
connected monomial base at x=1 gives an all-one matrix, with inherited row
dependencies. Ordinary binary HGP and ring LP also have different lengths.

Tractability is a relative judgment, with low confidence; no discovery-time
estimate or probability of success is assigned. A 3-by-4 base has six normalized
exponents, but exact distance still grows difficult. The decisive obstruction
is controlling logical vectors with support in both product sectors, modulo
all stabilizers. Searching for new support patterns can yield a small exact
counterexample before a general theorem. A positive theorem must supply a
genuine weight lower bound, not just characterize the known RCPC witness.

Formalization cost
Moderate for a fixed counterexample after external discovery: finite binary
matrices, exact syndromes, row-span nonmembership, and a classical-distance
exclusion certificate. Potentially substantial for a general theorem: source
matrix equivalence, quotient representatives, and a weight-preserving reduction
need fresh proofs. Existing CyclicLift/LiftedProduct.lean provides graph/matrix
bridges; the prior low-weight-logical and lift-label work supplies related
certificate patterns. These are reusable ingredients, not a distance theorem
for arbitrary 3-by-4 bases. No build or exact-target Lean proof is claimed here.

Literature comparison and search limits
F25 Section IV-D, Definitions 2–5 and Theorem 2 concerns the presence of RCPC,
and claims a distance formula under that condition. It does not settle the
Section VI design problem in the absence of RCPC. Its proof is presented as a
sketch with fuller treatment deferred. Neither its converse nor completeness
of the low-weight pattern classification is assumed in the proposed block.
Source Table I and equation (92) give finite construction/search examples;
reproducing those alone would not answer the open structural question.

F26's square binary HGP bases and constrained CPM lifts differ from F25's
rectangular monomial ring LP. Its connected rank-growth example settles the
bare existence version, not a general rank-design method or F25 distance.
F39's Frobenius-coupled bicycle subclass supplies upper bounds and explicit
logical cosets; it is not a distance-preservation theorem for rectangular LP.
F49's assisted-code/Steiner setting and its nondegeneracy theorem likewise do
not imply the chosen statement.
