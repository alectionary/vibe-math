THE (4,0) COUNT: DEFINITIONS, CLASSICAL ANSWER AND CYCLIC FORMULA

Target: the (4,0) half of the existing combined source task
L-F16-other-absorbing. This is a follow-up, not a new Beezer conjecture.
Source: Abiad–Castriota, arXiv:2607.13666v1, 15 July2026, Remark4.18
and the final bullet of Section6. The source proposes induced K4 counts
accessible through the same spectral data. The correction below uses actual
check incidence and full rectangular Fourier matrices; M_j alone is insufficient.

1. Objects and conventions

H is a finite binary check-by-variable incidence matrix. Write H(v) for the
set of checks incident with variable v. Assume every H(v) has size three and
|H(u) intersect H(v)|<=1 for distinct u,v. The second assumption is precisely
absence of Tanner four-cycles. No completeness, check regularity, connectedness,
prime lift size or quantum-distance assumption is used in the generic formula.

A support is an unordered four-element set of distinct variables. Its selected
check degree is the number of those variables incident with that check. It is
an elementary (4,0) support when every selected check degree is 0 or2. It is
automatically absorbing: every variable has three even checks and zero odd
checks. We count supports, not isomorphic shapes or ordered embeddings.

2. The decisive classical translation

A support is in ker_F2 H iff all its selected check degrees are even. With
four variables, the only possible obstruction to elementarity is a check c
of degree four. If such c exists, any other check d belonging to one selected
variable must also belong to another selected variable, by parity. That pair
would share c and d, contradicting the no-four-cycle assumption. Consequently
that selected variable could have only c, contradicting its degree three.
Thus even parity already forces elementarity. Conversely elementarity with
zero odd checks implies zero binary syndrome immediately.

Therefore N40(H)=A4(ker_F2 H), the ordinary binary weight-four coefficient.
This is classical codeword/even-configuration mathematics. Fujiwara2014,
Section3 p37, explicitly identifies the even four-triple configuration as
Pasch in Steiner designs. The argument above needs only a linear partial
triple system, not a complete design. The new artifact proves this implication
and the equality of actual matrix/support counts in Lean; no mathematical
priority for the elementary translation is claimed.

All six variable pairs have distinct check incidences. Each variable has
three degree-two checks, so its normal degree is three; hence the normal
simple graph is K4. Projected K4 alone is insufficient: four cubic columns
{0,1,2},{0,3,4},{0,5,6},{0,7,8} project to K4 through one degree-four check
and contain no (4,0) support. The artifact includes this boundary certificate.

A binary kernel word here can be a stabilizer in a quantum code. N40 is not
a count of nontrivial logical operators and does not by itself determine
quantum minimum distance or decoding performance.

3. MacWilliams already gives a full-incidence count

Let n be the number of variables and m the number of check rows. For each
binary check-message y in F2^m set w(y)=wt(y^T H). Then

  N40(H)=2^(-m) sum_y sum_{j=0}^4 (-1)^j C(w(y),j) C(n-w(y),4-j).       (MW)

Binomial coefficients outside their natural ranges are zero. This is the
weight-four coefficient of the classical MacWilliams transform, credited to
MacWilliams1963. The precise binary identity was checked in Lomonaco2006,
Theorem1; the original1963full proof was not retrieved. (MW) remains valid
with dependent check rows: y maps onto the row code with equally sized fibers,
so averaging over all messages equals averaging uniformly over the row code.

Here is a direct proof of the exact needed specialization. For a four-set S,
its zero-syndrome indicator is 2^(-m) sum_y (-1)^(y dot sum_{v in S}h_v),
by binary character orthogonality. Sum over S and interchange the finite sums.
For fixed y, choosing j variables having y dot h_v=1 and4-j having0 gives
the inner binomial expression in (MW). This proof identifies the actual input
needed; it does not assume that a dual weight distribution is free information.

Equivalently let s(y)=sum_v (-1)^(y dot h_v)=n-2w(y). Distinct columns imply

  24 N40(H)=2^(-m) sum_y s(y)^4 -3n^2+2n.                              (M4)

To check the diagonal corrections, expand the fourth power over ordered
four-tuples. All-distinct tuples contribute24N40. Four equal indices
contribute n, and two unequal pairs contribute6*C(n,2)=3n(n-1). Three-plus-
one and two-plus-one-plus-one tuples have nonzero binary sum because columns
are distinct. Hence the repeated-index contribution is3n^2-2n, as in(M4).

These are complete conventional counts from full binary incidence. They can
be exponential in m if implemented by all check messages. They are not low-
dimensional formulas from the physical cyclic matrices M_j. Binary syndrome-
space Fourier analysis and physical Z/N Fourier analysis use different groups.
The classical identity does not make M_j sufficient.

A standard meet-in-the-middle alternative is much cheaper. For each binary
syndrome s let m_s be the number of unordered distinct column pairs whose XOR
is s. Then

  N40(H)=(1/3) sum_s C(m_s,2).                                        (XOR)

Any two different pairs with equal XOR are disjoint: if they shared a variable,
the remaining two columns would be equal. Conversely a zero four-set has
exactly three perfect matchings, each producing one equal-XOR collision.
Cubic/no-four-cycle columns are distinct, so all hypotheses hold. The formula
uses O(n^2) pairs and exact syndrome hashing. This is a standard coding/counting
method, not a claim of a new counting algorithm or first enumeration.

4. Root-energy identity

Take an ordered adjacent root pair (u,v). Let W(u,v) consist of variables w
distinct from both roots that share a check with each root, but do not belong
to a common check of u and v. These are proper six-cycle third variables.
Let t=|W|, B=H(u) union H(v), and k_c=|{w in W:c in H(w)}|. Put

  E=sum_c k_c^2,        F=sum_{c in B} k_c^2.

Every w has one check shared with u and another shared with v. Those two checks
are distinct, because w shares no check of the root pair; there cannot be a
second check shared with either root, by no-four-cycles. Cubicity leaves exactly
one check outside B. Thus E-F counts ordered pairs (w,z) sharing an outside
check, including t diagonal pairs. Consequently

  E-F-t=number of ordered distinct outer pairs sharing an outside check. (L)

Such w,z cannot occupy the same socket at either root: they would share that
root socket and their outside check, producing a four-cycle. Their six pair
checks therefore form a Pasch with u,v. Conversely any ordered Pasch gives
this root/outer-pair structure. Every support has4!=24 such orderings. Hence

  24 N40(H)=sum_{ordered adjacent (u,v)} (E(u,v)-F(u,v)-t(u,v)).         (R)

The positive identity24N40+sum(F+t)=sum E avoids truncated natural subtraction.
It is important to remove F: counting arbitrary adjacent outer pairs also counts
ones sharing root checks and therefore does not count only genuine Pasches.
The generic Lean modules prove the local equivalence, all 24 orderings, the
bijections to actual four-supports, and the global positive energy identity.

5. Physical cyclic normalization and ordinary complex matrices

Let the incidence be a cyclic lift on variable types p and check types r over
G=Z/N, N>0. A socket (r,s) at type p means that (p,a) is incident with(r,a+s).
Normalize roots to (p,0),(q,d). Translating ordered roots is free, so the total
root sum is N times its normalized sum. No free-action hypothesis on unordered
four-supports is assumed or needed.

Define omega=exp(2*pi*i/N),

  D_j(r,p)=sum_{(r,s) in sockets(p)} omega^(-j*s),
  b_hat_W(j,p)=sum_a 1_W(p,a) omega^(-j*a).

The prior frozen standard-complex bridge proves that D_j b_hat_W is the actual
transformed incidence action, with the same negative-exponent convention and
the ordinary Euclidean norm. Unnormalized Parseval gives

  E(u,v)=(1/N) sum_j ||D_j b_hat_W(j)||^2.

Thus the explicit corrected formula is

  N40 = (N/24) sum_{normalized adjacent roots}
           [(1/N)sum_j ||D_j b_hat_W(j)||^2 -F(u,v)-t(u,v)].           (C40)

For comparison, the preserved (4,2) formula is

  N42 = (N/4) sum_{normalized adjacent roots}
           [t(u,v)^2+2t(u,v) -(1/N)sum_j ||D_j b_hat_W(j)||^2].        (C42)

They are two components of the same source question. Neither is an answer to
Beezer Conjecture4.1. Formula(C40) is proved in PaschComplexCount using the
preserved complex bridge and the new actual-support/root count. Its signature
allows every positive N, including1 and even/composite N. It uses actual full
rectangular D_j and incidence-defined W and F, not only D_jD_j*.

6. The source family and the M_j obstruction

Use the previously credited STS(13) Pasch trade (Jiang et al., arXiv2605.13690v1,
AppendixF.1, as recorded in our first block). Its two13-by26incidence matrices
A and A' are cubic, check-regular of degree6 and linear. Both integer point-Gram
matrices have diagonal6 and off-diagonal1, yet their Pasch counts are13 and8.
No new trade discovery is claimed.

Take B(x)=1+x+x^3 over the cyclic group of order7 and the actual source matrix

  HX=[A tensor I7 | I13 tensor B^T].

There are273 variables and91 checks. Its columns have degree3; its check degrees
are9; it has no Tanner four-cycles. The Fano transpose factor has7 Pasches.
Every Pasch in an ordinary HGP lies entirely in one factor fiber: any mixed
left/right root has no proper six-cycle third variable, and all pairs in a
Pasch must complete proper six-cycles. This gives the exact identity

  N40(HGP(A,B))=|D|N40(A)+|C|N40(B).

Therefore the two source counts are7*13+13*7=182 and7*8+13*7=147.
At every complex frequency, D_j=[A | beta_j I13] and
M_j=A A^T+|beta_j|^2 I13, with the same beta_j=1+omega^j+omega^(3j).
Consequently their full evaluated M_j matrices agree at every frequency;
not merely their eigenvalues or traces. A function of all M_j alone cannot
give both distinct counts. No quantitative claim of rarity or decoding effect
follows from this example.

7. Literature verdict and exact limits

Beezer2001 is now fully read in its final journal form. Theorem5.1 expresses
non-generators in terms of generator counts. Pasch is itself a generator at
both t=1 and t=2, so its count remains an input. The complete-STS four-line
basis of Grannell–Griggs–Mendelsohn1995 similarly does not determine that input.
MacWilliams, however, supplies the general full-incidence answer after the
classical even-configuration translation. The useful research contribution of
this block is the explicit source translation/correction and checked artifact,
not a claim that general weight-four counting was previously unknown.

Three earlier final/full-text comparisons remain inaccessible in the retained
ledger. Those limit priority conclusions, not the validity of the proofs above.
No claim of first-ever mathematical or formalization priority is made. The
newly inventoried Beezer questions have unassessed current-resolution and Lean
status; a historical conjecture label is not a certificate that it remains open.
