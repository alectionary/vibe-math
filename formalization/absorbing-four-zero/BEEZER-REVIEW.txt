USEFUL RESULTS AND EXACT LIMITS

Definitions 1.1-1.3, pp341-342 (PDF1-2): configurations are collections of
blocks in a t-design. The source permits repeated blocks and states how to
count them, though examples favor designs without repetition. A generating set
expresses every configuration count up to m blocks as a linear combination of
selected counts, with coefficients polynomial in v and lambda over the rationals.
This is about occurrence counts in a fixed design, not merely a shape catalog.

Theorem 2.1, pp344-347 (PDF4-7): removing a block with enough private points
and double-counting marked configurations gives a relation triangular in block
count and number of nontrivial points. Its proof uses exact t-design regularity
to say how many ambient blocks contain a marked t-set. The theorem is therefore
not automatically a theorem about arbitrary irregular partial triple systems.
A check-regular cubic incidence is a 1-design, so that applicable special case
must nevertheless be considered rather than dismissed as 'not an STS.'

Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.1, p348 (PDF8): a generator has more than t
repeated points on every block. All generators on at most m blocks generate
the remaining counts. Corollary 3.1, p349 (PDF9), specializes this to STS
configurations having no point of degree one. The empty configuration is included.

Theorem 5.1 and proof, pp352-354 (PDF12-14): the non-generator counts can be
solved in terms of the generator counts by a lower-triangular linear system.
The generator counts are inputs. In a Pasch configuration, all three points
of each triple have degree two. Thus Pasch is a generator both for t=1 and
t=2. This theorem does not compute its count from the design parameters alone.
For comparison, the (4,2)/C14 configuration is a generator at t=1 but not at t=2.

Section 5 credits the complete STS four-line basis (empty configuration and
Pasch) and its explicit formulas to Grannell-Griggs-Mendelsohn (1995). This
confirms that the classical (4,2) formula depends on an independently supplied
Pasch count; it does not turn that formula into a parameter-only (4,0) count.
The existing STS(13) trade already has Pasch counts 13 and 8 at identical
parameters and identical integer point-Gram matrices. It is a previously
credited example, not a new counterexample found in this block.

Theorem 4.1, p351 (PDF11), reports a finite computation proving minimality
for regular-graph generators through eight edges. The proof uses 46 simple
5-regular graphs on 16 vertices and a nonzero determinant computed in
Mathematica. This is an author-reported computation, not a Lean proof. The
initial repeated-block convention should be retained when formulating any
formal version; the listed simple-graph computation is the stated proof scope.

SOURCE-STATED QUESTIONS AND DIRECTIONS

1. Conjecture 4.1, p351 (PDF11): for fixed t and k, are the generators on at
most m blocks a minimal generating set for m-line configurations in general
t-(v,k,lambda) designs? This is an explicit precise conjecture, suitable for
one canonical target. The following paragraph proposes regular graphs
(t=1,k=2), for all m, as a first special case. We record that paragraph as a
special-case occurrence of the same conjecture, not a second equivalent target.
The final theorem verifies only m<=8 in that regular-graph case. A bounded
exact-title/conjecture search did not identify a later full solution; the current
resolution and exact Lean discovery remain unassessed, rather than certified open.

2. Section 7, pp356-357 (PDF16-17): determine the smallest designs of fixed
girth, in analogy with graph cages, and examine their structure. This is a
source-posed research direction. Fixing the admissible design parameters and
existence range is still needed for a precise extremal theorem. It is indexed
outside the eligible theorem denominator. Beezer points to his own 'The girth
of a design' for a fuller exploration; that follow-up must be read before
choosing an instance as a new research problem.

3. Section 6, p355 (PDF15): extend the tabulated equations to identify which
larger configurations have counts determined by the design parameters.
This is a broad source-proposed computational/classification direction, not
a separately numbered conjecture or a completed finite classification. It is
indexed outside the eligible theorem denominator; no effort estimate is invented.

HISTORICAL QUESTION, NOT A NEW BEEZER CONJECTURE

The introduction and Section 7 recall the Erdos high-girth STS existence
question. Kwan-Sah-Sawhney-Simkin's Annals 2024 theorem answers that historical
question. Its girth excludes small dense configurations on j points with j-2
blocks. Beezer's Definition 7.1 instead excludes all small nonempty generators.
These definitions must not be merged: a six-block, nine-point grid is a generator
but is not a six-block, eight-point forbidden configuration. The later high-girth
existence theorem does not by itself solve Beezer's minimum-order design problem.
This historical citation is recorded in the review, not counted as a new
currently open canonical question from Beezer's paper.

FOLLOW-UP FOUND DURING THE FINAL AUDIT

Heinlein–Ostergard2022, Algorithms and complexity for counting configurations
in Steiner triple systems (DOI10.1002/jcd.21839), Section2.2, Theorem7, verifies
the minimality conjecture for complete Steiner triple systems through eight
lines by a full-rank computation on623 STS(25)s. This is a later restricted
case of Beezer's general minimal-basis conjecture, not a solution for arbitrary
t,k,m. Their introduction still describes the all-n case as a conjecture.
This was read in the final publisher HTML. No new Lean audit of their rank
computation is claimed. The authors also describe constant-configuration
classification as incomplete; the full general current status remains unassessed.
