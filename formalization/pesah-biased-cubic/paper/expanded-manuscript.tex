\begin{abstract}
The dissertation's principal pure-Z advantage remains only partly reconstructed. Pesah reports cubic BP--OSD thresholds of 21.7\% undeformed and greater than 36\% deformed; we obtain 21.908\% for the former, but cannot identify a reliable asymptotic threshold for the latter. Rhombic pure-Z values are 28.4\% and 6.5\% in the source versus 27.407\% and 6.490\% here, retaining the strong disadvantage of that deformation. The preserved cubic pure-X comparison is 2.9\%/7.4\% versus 2.388\%/7.570\%. These are limited reconstructions with unknown original Monte Carlo error and production settings. A consequential point-estimate reversal occurs for cubic Sweep--Match depolarizing noise: the source deformation contrast is $+0.300$ percentage points, ours $-0.134$; our matching engine differs from the historical one. A cross-code ranking also reverses at Z bias 100, but the responsible cubic fit is unstable and does not establish a new ranking. A further 112,000 fresh trials expose size-dependent crossings; both logistic and source-family quadratic scaling fail the tested local windows. Lean proofs now classify cubic closed cochains by three cycle bits and establish minimum nontrivial cocycle weight $L^2$, with a finite-coordinate exactness bridge. Written analytical checks additionally recover the rhombic parameters and distances, the ideal-majority 50\% limit and syndrome-consistent OSD guarantee. Actual BP--OSD asymptotic thresholds and historical-production/frontend equivalence remain unestablished. Every substantive result is accounted for, with limited or blocked outcomes retained; this is not a claim that every result was successfully reproduced.
\end{abstract}

\section{Experimental reconstruction}
We run periodic sizes $L=4,6,8,10$, both deformation arms, perfect syndrome measurements and independent physical-qubit errors. Categorical I/X/Y/Z draws preserve the simultaneous X and Z components of Y. For bias $\eta$ along one Pauli axis, its probability is $p\eta/(\eta+1)$ and each other axis receives $p/[2(\eta+1)]$. Depolarizing noise is $\eta=1/2$; the pure channels are the limiting cases. Hadamard deformation swaps X and Z on the selected axis.

The primary decoder reconstructs the historical ten-iteration BP and order-zero OSD implementation in \citet{bn3d}; its algorithmic context is \citet{roffe2020}. The source does not identify production seeds, counts, iteration settings, package versions, tie handling or fitting windows. Current NumPy quicksort, the historical prior regularizer and independent PCG64/MT19937 trials are explicit supplied assumptions. Full decoder equivalence to the undisclosed source production run is not established.

An original C++ GF(2) elimination routine accelerates OSD while preserving reliability ordering, pivot selection and zero free variables. It matched the preserved Python routine in 196 correction cases through $L=10$. The generalized cubic decoder matched 480 historical dense-reference cases; the rhombic implementation matched 320, after an explicit global Pauli conversion. These checks share graphs, priors and the stated algorithm. They independently check arithmetic/correction implementations, not an entire independent scientific replication.

\subsection{Rhombic homology and distance}
The odd-parity cubes define vertices of a face-centred cubic complex. Each physical edge touches exactly two such cubes and becomes their connecting edge. The four odd cubes around an original vertex form a tetrahedron: its six edges are the six incident physical edges, and its four faces are the source triangle checks. Cube and triangle incidence therefore commute. Subdivide each unit cube into its odd-corner tetrahedron and four even-corner pieces. The latter join into octahedra. This fills space; even periodic identification gives a three-torus, including small quotients with identified edge endpoints.

There are $L^3/2$ vertices and $3L^3$ edges. Connectivity gives cube rank $L^3/2-1$; three-dimensional first homology gives triangle rank $5L^3/2-2$, hence $k=3$. A nontrivial cycle has a trail with displacement at least L in one coordinate, while each edge changes that coordinate by at most one. A length-L zigzag attains the resulting X-distance. For each winding direction there are $2L^2$ edge-disjoint homologous zigzags, one for each transverse position and each of two physical edge orientations. A nontrivial cocycle intersects all of them oddly, so its weight is at least $2L^2$. A transverse cut with both orientations attains this Z-distance. The source's prose and figure/historical Pauli labels conflict; these formulas follow the prose convention.

\repo{followup/check_rhombic_cellulation.py}{check_rhombic_cellulation.py} reconstructs endpoints and tetrahedron faces independently of the source corner-triple implementation. At $L=2,4,6,8,10$, every cube matrix and triangle multiset matches, and all counted zigzags have connected endpoints, correct winding and disjoint supports. The check takes 0.289 seconds and under 84 MiB RSS. Its shared periodic-coordinate convention limits independence; finite cases support the mapping, while the general result uses the written cellulation proof.

\subsection{Ideal majority and actual decoding}
For a component of m independent Bernoulli(p) errors, let $q_m=\Pr[B>m/2]+\tfrac12\Pr[B=m/2]$, where $B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)$ and ties use independent fair coins. Parallel components represent the same logical class, so the failure probability for N components is
\[P_{\rm fail}=\frac{1-(1-2q_m)^N}{2}.\]
The restricted cubic sectors have $(m,N)=(L,L^2)$ for chains and $(L^2,L)$ for planes. For fixed $p<1/2$, $q_m\le\exp[-2m(1/2-p)^2]$ and $P_{\rm fail}\le Nq_m\to0$. One direct derivation uses exponential Markov inequality: for $0<p<1/2$, choose $t=\log[(1-p)/p]$ to bound $\Pr[B\ge m/2]$ by $[2\sqrt{p(1-p)}]^m\le\exp[-2m(1/2-p)^2]$; $p=0$ is immediate. At $p=1/2$ the logical parity is uniform. This proves the ideal-majority endpoint on $0\le p\le1/2$, not that ten-iteration BP--OSD realizes it. The remaining point equations at fixed first coordinate are exactly square-lattice vertex incidence; \repo{followup/check_cubic_layers.py}{check_cubic_layers.py} independently verifies the finite mapping.

\repo{followup/check_majority_exact.py}{check_majority_exact.py} independently evaluates Bernoulli polynomial convolution and parity recursion using rational arithmetic. All 48 saved predictions at four sizes and six probabilities agree within $1.43\times10^{-15}$; the $2\times10^{-14}$ check tolerance concerns floating-point evaluation, not sampling uncertainty. The argument requires independent errors and independent tie coins. Further finite-size fits cannot turn this ideal-decoder proof into a BP threshold theorem.

In the deformed rhombic loop sector, each first-axis error is exposed by a triangle, proving unique recovery restricted to that support. An unrestricted regularized decoder can nevertheless leave it. At $L=2$, exhaustive tests of 256 patterns at prior $10^{-12}$ find four valid-syndrome logical failures; the external historical dense implementation reproduces all four sparse-decoder corrections. At priors $.001,.1,.49,.5$ every pattern succeeds. \repo{followup/check_loop_reference.py}{check_loop_reference.py} and \repo{followup/rhombic-loop-exhaustive.json}{rhombic-loop-exhaustive.json} retain the witnesses. The source wording is preserved in \repo{evidence/rhombic_loop_argument.png}{rhombic_loop_argument.png} and \repo{evidence/rhombic_loop_continuation.png}{rhombic_loop_continuation.png}. These stress cases lie outside the dissertation's sizes and probability grids: they qualify a universal implementation guarantee, not its reported zero-loop-error observations or the restricted uniqueness theorem.

\subsection{OSD and the quantum bridge}
Column permutation and invertible row operations preserve the system $Hc=s$. If $s\in\operatorname{im}H$, the zero rows after elimination have zero right-hand side. Setting free variables to zero and pivot variables to their reduced right-hand side solves every equation; undoing the permutation yields a valid correction. The native solver's skipped prefix words are zero by its active-row invariant. The source/Python solvers omit an inconsistency guard, so the consistent-syndrome premise is essential; all production syndromes here satisfy it.

An independent column-span enumeration in \repo{followup/check_osd_exhaustive.py}{check_osd_exhaustive.py} checks all 682 binary matrices of shapes 1--3 by 1--3, every consistent syndrome, all strict reliability orders and a tied order. All 20,748 cases match Python, native and historical solvers: 62,244 correction comparisons. In 455 inconsistent examples native rejects while source/Python return invalid corrections. The complete check takes 3.34 seconds and under 48 MiB RSS. Shared matrices and tie ordering are disclosed. This verifies consistency, not optimality or convergence of approximate BP.

For r independent commuting Hermitian Pauli checks generating no $-I$, the common-eigenspace projector is $2^{-r}\prod_i(I+g_i)$. Only its identity term has nonzero trace, giving dimension $2^{n-r}$. Independent CSS bases satisfy the no-$-I$ premise because a scalar product requires both binary supports to vanish. Tensor-product Hadamard conjugation is unitary and preserves Pauli support, including the irrelevant sign on Y. This supplies the written Hilbert-space bridge to the earlier binary transport proof; it does not assert unchanged CSS sector distances after mixing.

\section{Software contributions and attempted blockers}
The source claims a tenfold OSD speed improvement from reusing elimination rather than recomputing ranks. Our reconstructed repeated-rank baseline and reused-elimination implementation agree on 15 small cases and show speedups roughly 12--145 times. The original benchmark inputs, baseline implementation and hardware are absent; concurrent workload also affects our timings. This supports the mechanism, not an exact tenfold benchmark reproduction. The native optimization's separate speedup is not credited as a reproduction of that source claim.

On shared diagnostic errors, total failures are 1,375 at ten iterations, 1,476 at 100, and 1,353 with stable sorting, out of 5,600 each. These heterogeneous exploratory counts demonstrate setting sensitivity; they are not pooled threshold estimates. No invalid correction occurred in these checks.

The historical Flask backend passes 28 response checks: four code/size geometry checks and 24 zero-syndrome BP decoding requests. Browser loading of the original frontend encounters a denied external Three.js module host and produces no canvas. Frontend interaction/rendering remains blocked; no alternate CDN was used. Backend success is not full GUI reproduction. Claimed usefulness for visualization is qualitative and lacks a source evaluation protocol.

Chapter 4 proposes measurement/circuit noise, coprime dimensions, boundaries, higher-order OSD, a statistical-physics model and future resource comparisons. They are future work, not dissertation experiments that we failed to run. We do not invent circuit-level thresholds or Y-bias results merely because the setup mentions general Pauli channels.
