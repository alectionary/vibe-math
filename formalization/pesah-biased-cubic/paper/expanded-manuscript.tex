\begin{abstract}
We did not replicate the cubic pure-Z improvement reported by \citet{pesah2021} in the UCL Master of Research dissertation \emph{Tailoring 3D topological codes and decoders to biased noise}: the source reports that Hadamard deformation with BP--OSD increases the threshold from 21.7\% to greater than 36\% (Table 3.1), whereas we estimate 21.908\% undeformed and cannot identify a reliable deformed threshold. Unknown original Monte Carlo error, missing production settings and inadequate finite-size scaling fits prevent an exact comparison; this failure to replicate does not refute the reported improvement. For cubic Sweep--Match under depolarizing noise, the deformation contrast reverses from $+0.300$ to $-0.134$ percentage points, with a different matching engine; an unstable fit also reverses a cross-code point ranking at Z bias 100. Supporting results recover the direction of cubic pure-X improvement (2.9\%/7.4\% published versus 2.388\%/7.570\%) and rhombic pure-Z deterioration (28.4\%/6.5\% versus 27.407\%/6.490\%). Independent numerical checks and analytical arguments verify code parameters, ideal-majority limits and conditional decoder guarantees. These findings support channel-dependent benefits of deformation, but do not establish the principal cubic pure-Z improvement or the actual BP--OSD asymptotic endpoint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We did not replicate the reported cubic pure-Z improvement from 21.7\% to greater than 36\%: our undeformed estimate is 21.908\%, but the deformed threshold is not reliably identified. Missing production information and failed scaling diagnostics limit this comparison. The Sweep--Match depolarizing contrast changes from $+0.300$ to $-0.134$ percentage points, and an unstable cubic fit reverses a cross-code point ordering at Z bias 100. These are point-estimate discrepancies, not evidence of a statistically established opposite ordering. Numerical agreement uses the 0.1-percentage-point rule stated above, separately from uncertainty and reconstruction scope.

The scientific question is whether local Hadamard deformation improves a code's tolerance of biased Pauli noise without changing its physical-qubit count. We investigate the numerical, analytical and software results in Arthur Pesah's \emph{Tailoring 3D topological codes and decoders to biased noise}, a University College London Master of Research dissertation submitted on 1 September 2021 under Dan Browne \citep{pesah2021}. Its contributions are the code deformations, decoder implementation and published comparisons. Our contributions are reconstructed experiments, independent implementation checks and analytical verification of the stated reductions and code parameters.

The evidence supports different effects in different channels. Cubic pure-X improves in the reconstruction, whereas rhombic pure-Z worsens strongly. Agreement in these cases, or equality of code resources, does not establish the unverified cubic pure-Z improvement. The study covers endpoint thresholds, finite-bias comparisons, total/point/loop curves, code constructions, the OSD optimization and the graphical interface. The 87-item \repo{coverage.json}{coverage.json} distinguishes assessed findings and access limits from inherited background and proposed future experiments.

\section{Experimental reconstruction}
We run periodic sizes $L=4,6,8,10$, both deformation arms, perfect syndrome measurements and independent physical-qubit errors. Categorical I/X/Y/Z draws preserve the simultaneous X and Z components of Y. For bias $\eta$ along one Pauli axis, its probability is $p\eta/(\eta+1)$ and each other axis receives $p/[2(\eta+1)]$. Depolarizing noise is $\eta=1/2$; the pure channels are the limiting cases. Hadamard deformation swaps X and Z on the selected axis.

The primary decoder combines ten iterations of belief propagation (BP) with order-zero ordered-statistics decoding (OSD), reconstructing the implementation in \citet{bn3d}; its algorithmic context is \citet{roffe2020}. The source does not identify production seeds, counts, iteration settings, package versions, tie handling or fitting windows. Current NumPy quicksort, the historical prior regularizer and independent PCG64/MT19937 trials are explicit supplied assumptions. Full decoder equivalence to the undisclosed source production run is not established.

An original C++ GF(2) elimination routine accelerates OSD while preserving reliability ordering, pivot selection and zero free variables. It matched the Python reference routine in 196 correction cases through $L=10$. The generalized cubic decoder matched 480 historical dense-reference cases; the rhombic implementation matched 320, after an explicit global Pauli conversion. These checks share graphs, priors and the stated algorithm. They independently check arithmetic/correction implementations, not an entire independent scientific replication.

The rhombic convention is consequential: the thesis prose uses cube Z checks and triangle X checks; the available historical code globally interchanges them. We use the prose convention and verify the exact global-Hadamard mapping. A nearby figure caption also conflicts with the prose. \subsection{Rhombic homology and distance}
The odd-parity cubes define vertices of a face-centred cubic complex. Each physical edge touches exactly two such cubes and becomes their connecting edge. The four odd cubes around an original vertex form a tetrahedron: its six edges are the six incident physical edges, and its four faces are the source triangle checks. Cube and triangle incidence therefore commute. Subdivide each unit cube into its odd-corner tetrahedron and four even-corner pieces. The latter join into octahedra. This fills space; even periodic identification gives a three-torus, including small quotients with identified edge endpoints.

There are $L^3/2$ vertices and $3L^3$ edges. Connectivity gives cube rank $L^3/2-1$; three-dimensional first homology gives triangle rank $5L^3/2-2$, hence $k=3$. A nontrivial cycle has a trail with displacement at least L in one coordinate, while each edge changes that coordinate by at most one. A length-L zigzag attains the resulting X-distance. For each winding direction there are $2L^2$ edge-disjoint homologous zigzags, one for each transverse position and each of two physical edge orientations. A nontrivial cocycle intersects all of them oddly, so its weight is at least $2L^2$. A transverse cut with both orientations attains this Z-distance. The source's prose and figure/historical Pauli labels conflict; these formulas follow the prose convention.

\repo{followup/check_rhombic_cellulation.py}{check_rhombic_cellulation.py} reconstructs endpoints and tetrahedron faces independently of the source corner-triple implementation. At $L=2,4,6,8,10$, every cube matrix and triangle multiset matches, and all counted zigzags have connected endpoints, correct winding and disjoint supports. The check takes 0.289 seconds and under 84 MiB RSS. Its shared periodic-coordinate convention limits independence; finite cases support the mapping, while the general result uses the written cellulation proof.

\subsection{Ideal majority and actual decoding}
For a component of $m$ independent Bernoulli($p$) errors, let $q_m=\Pr[B>m/2]+\tfrac12\Pr[B=m/2]$, where $B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)$ and ties use independent fair coins. Parallel components represent the same logical class, so the failure probability for $N$ components is
\[P_{\rm fail}=\frac{1-(1-2q_m)^N}{2}.\]
The restricted cubic sectors have $(m,N)=(L,L^2)$ for chains and $(L^2,L)$ for planes. For fixed $p<1/2$, $q_m\le\exp[-2m(1/2-p)^2]$ and $P_{\rm fail}\le Nq_m\to0$. One direct derivation uses exponential Markov inequality: for $0<p<1/2$, choose $t=\log[(1-p)/p]$ to bound $\Pr[B\ge m/2]$ by $[2\sqrt{p(1-p)}]^m\le\exp[-2m(1/2-p)^2]$; $p=0$ is immediate. At $p=1/2$ the logical parity is uniform. This proves the ideal-majority endpoint on $0\le p\le1/2$, not that ten-iteration BP--OSD realizes it. The remaining point equations at fixed first coordinate are exactly square-lattice vertex incidence; \repo{followup/check_cubic_layers.py}{check_cubic_layers.py} independently verifies the finite mapping.

\repo{followup/check_majority_exact.py}{check_majority_exact.py} independently evaluates Bernoulli polynomial convolution and parity recursion using rational arithmetic. All 48 saved predictions at four sizes and six probabilities agree within $1.43\times10^{-15}$; the $2\times10^{-14}$ check tolerance concerns floating-point evaluation, not sampling uncertainty. The argument requires independent errors and independent tie coins. Further finite-size fits cannot turn this ideal-decoder proof into a BP threshold theorem.

In the deformed rhombic loop sector, each first-axis error is exposed by a triangle, proving unique recovery restricted to that support. An unrestricted regularized decoder can nevertheless leave it. At $L=2$, exhaustive tests of 256 patterns at prior $10^{-12}$ find four valid-syndrome logical failures; the external historical dense implementation reproduces all four sparse-decoder corrections. At priors $.001,.1,.49,.5$ every pattern succeeds. \repo{followup/check_loop_reference.py}{check_loop_reference.py} and \repo{followup/rhombic-loop-exhaustive.json}{rhombic-loop-exhaustive.json} retain the witnesses. The source wording is preserved in \repo{evidence/rhombic_loop_argument.png}{rhombic_loop_argument.png} and \repo{evidence/rhombic_loop_continuation.png}{rhombic_loop_continuation.png}. These stress cases lie outside the dissertation's sizes and probability grids: they qualify a universal implementation guarantee, not its reported zero-loop-error observations or the restricted uniqueness theorem.

\subsection{OSD and the quantum bridge}
Column permutation and invertible row operations preserve the system $Hc=s$. If $s\in\operatorname{im}H$, the zero rows after elimination have zero right-hand side. Setting free variables to zero and pivot variables to their reduced right-hand side solves every equation; undoing the permutation yields a valid correction. The native solver's skipped prefix words are zero by its active-row invariant. The source/Python solvers omit an inconsistency guard, so the consistent-syndrome premise is essential; all production syndromes here satisfy it.

An independent column-span enumeration in \repo{followup/check_osd_exhaustive.py}{check_osd_exhaustive.py} checks all 682 binary matrices of shapes 1--3 by 1--3, every consistent syndrome, all strict reliability orders and a tied order. All 20,748 cases match Python, native and historical solvers: 62,244 correction comparisons. In 455 inconsistent examples native rejects while source/Python return invalid corrections. The complete check takes 3.34 seconds and under 48 MiB RSS. Shared matrices and tie ordering are disclosed. This verifies consistency, not optimality or convergence of approximate BP.

For $r$ independent commuting Hermitian Pauli checks generating no $-I$, the common-eigenspace projector is $2^{-r}\prod_i(I+g_i)$. Only its identity term has nonzero trace, giving dimension $2^{n-r}$. Independent CSS bases satisfy the no-$-I$ premise because a scalar product requires both binary supports to vanish. Tensor-product Hadamard conjugation is unitary and preserves Pauli support, including the irrelevant sign on Y. This supplies the Hilbert-space interpretation of the binary transport theorem; it does not assert unchanged CSS sector distances after mixing.

\subsection{Limits of the analytical guarantees}
The ideal-majority 50\% endpoint does not prove an asymptotic threshold for BP--OSD. General cubic rank/X-distance, rhombic homology/distances and quantum interpretation are established by written arguments; they are not presented as Lean theorems. The frozen numerical diagnostics reject the tested local scaling models; changing windows to obtain a preferred threshold is not justified. The unavailable inputs are original production counts/configuration, benchmark inputs and the frontend dependency. Circuit-noise and other Chapter 4 proposals are future work rather than completed dissertation experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
We did not establish the reported cubic pure-Z improvement from 21.7\% to greater than 36\%. Our undeformed estimate is 21.908\%, but the deformed threshold is not identifiable reliably from these finite-size curves. In contrast, the reconstructed cubic pure-X and rhombic pure-Z comparisons recover the published directions: deformation improves the former and worsens the latter. The Sweep--Match depolarizing contrast reverses sign, and the cross-code ranking at Z bias 100 is sensitive to an unstable cubic fit. Unknown source uncertainty and production settings prevent these discrepancies from establishing a statistically supported opposite conclusion.

Analytical verification explains several structural properties without resolving the missing performance result. The cubic and rhombic parameters, ideal-majority limits and conditional OSD guarantee follow from the stated proofs and assumptions. Actual BP--OSD asymptotic thresholds require additional justification. Reliable numerical threshold estimates require an adequate scaling model and a prospective size/grid design; exact production and benchmark reproduction require the original inputs and configuration. The unavailable matching dependency and frontend module also prevent historical-engine and full-interface equivalence. The replication verdict therefore distinguishes reported values, reconstructed estimates and verified theorems.

\section{Constructive periodic classification and distance}
The constructive proof classifies closed periodic cochains by their three cycle labels and establishes their minimum nontrivial weight. Its statements are in \repo{followup/lean/CubicPotential.lean}{CubicPotential.lean}, \repo{followup/lean/CubicDistance.lean}{CubicDistance.lean} and \repo{followup/lean/CubicLift.lean}{CubicLift.lean}; \repo{followup/cubic-homology.md}{cubic-homology.md} gives the written argument and implementation correspondence. The Lean build and standard axiom dependencies are specified in Appendix B.

For a closed binary edge field on the unwrapped lattice, integrate along x at $y=z=0$, then y at $z=0$, then z. A proved XOR telescoping identity and the three plaquette equations show that the resulting potential has the original field as its gradient. This potential is not assumed periodic. Period shifts change it by position-independent bits, precisely the three cycle sums. A periodic potential exists if and only if those bits vanish. Two closed fields therefore differ by a periodic gradient if and only if their labels agree. Seam representatives realize every label; constant fields would fail to realize nonzero labels at even L. The finite adapter proves the analogous zero-holonomy criterion for the actual finite-coordinate face and vertex formulas, including the periodic boundary.

A nonzero axis cycle sum is the same on all $L^2$ parallel lines. Each must contain an occupied edge, giving a kernel-checked lower bound $L^2$. A seam representative attains it. This is the exact minimum nontrivial cocycle weight in the coordinate model. The independently executable consumer uses actual Python vertex/face matrices and logical strings to check Lean-computed potentials, labels, weights and period jumps; all 80 constructed fields pass. General claims rely on proofs, not the number of examples.

For the written rank consequence, the constant vertex-gradient kernel gives rank $L^3-1$. The linear cycle-label map is onto three bits and its kernel consists exactly of gradients, so the closed-field dimension is $L^3+2$. Rank-nullity gives face rank $2L^3-2$, hence the CSS count $3L^3-(L^3-1)-(2L^3-2)=3$. Finite-dimensional rank-nullity/cardinality conversion, array-index rank correspondence and Hilbert-space interpretation are not all formalized; this is a written consequence of the checked structural results, not a Lean matrix-rank theorem.

For the written X-distance argument, finite binary annihilator duality pairs a non-boundary cycle with a nonzero seam class. Translated parallel seams differ by a vertex gradient, so a zero-syndrome cycle pairs identically with all L disjoint seams. Its weight is at least L; a straight periodic string attains L. This is a written analytical check, not a Lean duality theorem. The main analytical section supplies separate rhombic, probability, algorithmic and quantum arguments; no cubic result is transferred to the rhombic code without its own geometry.
