Dolecek et al., IEEE TIT 56 (2010), 181–201, Section III-C, Lemma 4, printed pp. 187–188: classical array code Cp,3 has p^2(p−1) (3,3) sets and 3p^2(p−1)/2 (4,2) fully absorbing sets. Their proof counts each (4,2) twice through its constituent (3,3) sets, using a special extension property. Their matrix is a fully populated array of permutation blocks with exponent r*c modulo an odd prime. QC-GHP has a different sparse/two-block exponent pattern and polynomial-valued diagonal blocks. No full-target transfer established. The five-pair-check topology and two-triangle counting idea are classical ingredients, not new contributions.

Wang–Dolecek–Wesel, arXiv:1208.6094v2 (2012, journal 2013), Theorem 1 and Section VI: cycle-consistency, check-consistency and bit-consistency machinery for separable circulant-based mother/daughter codes. Sufficiency includes a non-extensibility condition. Section VI gives an explicit (4,2) CCM and asymptotic multiplicity. This is applicable background and a possible route to specializations, not an inspected universal corrected QC-GHP Fourier formula.

Karimi–Banihashemi 1308.1259v1 (2013), Section III and Section V-B1: normal graphs remove degree-one checks and replace degree-two checks with edges. This encodes already elementary sets; it does not justify counting all induced diamonds in the global variable-node projection without excluding high-multiplicity checks. Their LSS search, and Hashemi–Banihashemi 1510.04954v1 (2015) dpl expansion methods, already supply exhaustive enumeration algorithms. Thus merely enumerating the target in an expanded graph is not a new algorithmic answer.

Full-target translation check for SCB: a column-weight-three SCB mother/daughter retaining three row groups has exactly one neighbor per variable in each group. Any set of checks pairwise co-occurring with a variable therefore has size at most three. Our fixed 7x7 B^T Fano block has a seven-check co-occurrence clique: every check pair occurs in one column. Hence the reduced QC-GHP witness cannot be converted into that three-row-group SCB family by relabeling. This explains concretely why its published special-family CCM/multiplicity formulas do not by themselves settle the full target. The generic ETS algorithms still enumerate our matrices, and no first-enumeration claim is made.
