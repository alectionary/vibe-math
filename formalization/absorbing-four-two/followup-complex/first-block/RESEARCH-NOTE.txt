Scope and notation
------------------
H is a finite binary parity-check matrix. Its columns are variables V and rows
are checks C. N(v) is the set of checks incident to v. Every column has weight
three. Distinct columns share at most one check (equivalently, the simple Tanner
graph has no four-cycle). Products used for counting below are ordinary integer
or complex products. CSS commutation is checked separately over F_2.

For a selected variable set S, d_S(c)=|N(c) intersect S|. S is elementary when
every incident check has induced degree one or two. It is absorbing when each
selected variable has more even than odd induced-degree check neighbors. An
elementary (4,2) set has |S|=4 and exactly two odd induced-degree checks. Count
variable subsets once, without choosing an ordering or checking nodes outside S.
This is not the stronger 'fully absorbing' condition.

Classical ingredient
--------------------
The elementary (4,2) normal graph is a diamond, K4 minus an edge. The two central
vertices have normal degree three; the two leaves have degree two. This topology,
and counting through the two constituent six-cycles, are classical. See Dolecek
et al. (2010), Section III-C / Lemma 4, and Wang–Dolecek–Wesel (2012/2013), Section
VI. The count below makes the distinction between a normal graph of an already
elementary set and the global variable-node projection explicit.

Lemma 1: classification from actual incidences
---------------------------------------------
Let S be four selected variables. If it is elementary with two odd checks, the
12 variable-check incidences comprise two degree-one checks and five degree-two
checks. Replacing degree-two checks by edges gives a simple graph with four
vertices and five edges: no parallel edge is possible because H has no four-
cycle. Thus it is K4 minus an edge. Its degrees are 3,3,2,2, proving absorption
automatically, and giving a unique unordered central pair.

Conversely, an incidence-realized diamond with five distinct degree-two checks
and two distinct remaining degree-one checks is elementary (4,2). An induced
diamond in the global projection alone does not guarantee these incidences.

Lemma 2: proper common neighbors
--------------------------------
For an ordered pair u != v sharing a check c, define

  W_uv = {w not in {u,v}: w shares a check with u and with v,
                             and w is not incident to c}.
  t_uv = |W_uv|.

The shared check c is unique. Equivalently, the triple intersection
N(u) intersect N(v) intersect N(w) must be empty. Each w in W_uv completes a
proper six-cycle on u,v,w, through three distinct checks. The other two checks
of u and the other two checks of v give four possible pairs of checks; at most
one variable realizes each such pair. Consequently t_uv <= 4.

An ordered choice w,z in W_uv, w != z, with N(w) intersect N(z) empty, determines
an elementary (4,2) set {u,v,w,z}. To see this, consider the five pair-checks uv,
uw,vw,uz,vz. The proper-triangle conditions make each of the two triangles use
three distinct checks. Any further equality between these five check labels
would force w and z to share a check. Hence all five are distinct. Degree three
exhausts the check neighborhoods of u and v. Each leaf has its two indicated
even checks and one remaining check. Neither remaining check meets u or v,
since this would repeat a shared check for a variable pair; the two remaining
checks cannot coincide since w,z have no shared check. Thus they are precisely
the two odd degree-one checks. Conversely, the two leaves of every elementary
(4,2) set satisfy these requirements at its central pair.

There are exactly four ordered realizations of each set: two central orders and
two leaf orders. No division by the number of cycles or by a lift orbit is used.

Theorem 1: exact incidence formula
----------------------------------
Let E^or denote the ordered distinct variable pairs sharing a check. Let A be
the adjacency matrix of the simple global variable-node projection, with zero
diagonal; A = H^T H - 3I. Let b_uv be the 0/1 indicator vector of W_uv. Then

  4 N_(4,2)(H)
    = sum_{(u,v) in E^or} [t_uv(t_uv-1) - b_uv^T A b_uv]
    = sum_{(u,v) in E^or} [t_uv^2 + 2t_uv - ||H b_uv||_2^2].       (1)

Proof: t(t-1) counts ordered distinct leaf choices. The quadratic form b^T A b
counts precisely the ordered choices whose leaves share a check. Lemma 2 gives
the four-to-one correspondence. Finally, H^T H = A+3I and b^T b=t.
Every summand is nonnegative and even. The individual energy identity can be
written without subtraction:

  #ordered distinct nonadjacent pairs in W + ||H 1_W||_2^2 = |W|^2+2|W|.

This identity holds for every W in a cubic Tanner graph without four-cycles.
Thus there is no unknown (4,0) count left as a correction term in (1).

Computing the incidence correction
----------------------------------
For a central edge uv with unique check c, all common projected neighbors at c
must be removed. If h_c is the indicator of the whole check neighborhood, then

  b_uv = (A e_u) elementwise-multiplied by (A e_v) - h_c + e_u + e_v,
  t_uv = (A^2)_(uv) - deg(c) + 2.

The endpoint additions restore zero at u and v. All other entries are zero or
one by the no-four-cycle hypothesis. These expressions exhibit the additional
incidence information used by (1).

Theorem 2: cyclic / Fourier formula
-----------------------------------
Describe a cyclic lift of degree ell by three sockets (r,e) for every variable
type p. Its variable (p,a) has check neighbors (r,a+e), with arithmetic modulo
ell. The sockets are distinct, and the expanded Tanner graph satisfies the
preceding hypotheses. This includes the stated column-weight-three QC-GHP
matrix [A_tilde | I tensor B^T], after the transpose sign on B is included.

Let R be the ordered central-pair representatives ((p,0),(q,d)). Each actual
ordered central pair has a unique such representative and a unique translate.
This action is free on ordered pairs, even for composite or even ell. For a
root alpha in R compute W_alpha and t_alpha by its sockets. For each pair of
remaining check sockets x=(r,a), y=(s,b), a completing variable (p,t) exists
exactly when it has distinct sockets (r,e),(s,f) satisfying

  t = a-e;        a-e+f = b    (mod ell).

No four-cycle makes this completion unique when it exists. Only four pairs of
remaining sockets must be inspected to construct W_alpha; no expanded graph is
needed for the integer implementation.

Define omega=exp(2 pi i/ell),
  D_j[r,p] = sum_{(r,e) in sockets(p)} omega^(-je),
  b_hat_alpha,j[p] = sum_{(p,a) in W_alpha} omega^(-ja).
With this unnormalized Fourier convention, Parseval gives

  ||H b_alpha||^2 = (1/ell) sum_{j=0}^{ell-1} ||D_j b_hat_alpha,j||^2.

Applying (1) and the free ordered-root translation yields the explicit formula

  N_(4,2)(H) = (ell/4) sum_{alpha in R}
    [t_alpha^2 + 2t_alpha
        - (1/ell) sum_j ||D_j b_hat_alpha,j||^2].                 (2)

This is a finite formula in the polynomial/socket data. Exact modular counting
implements it without rounding; numerical complex evaluation is only a check.
For QC-GHP, D_j is the full rectangular evaluated check matrix. Its product
M_j=D_j D_j^* loses information needed to recover the corrected vectors/count.

Theorem 3: M_j alone does not determine the count, even at fixed parameters
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Here are two 12x12 binary matrices, specified by column supports in rows 0..11.
They have the following eight common columns:

  {0,10,11}, {1,6,9}, {2,9,10}, {3,6,7},
  {4,7,8}, {5,7,11}, {6,8,10}, {8,9,11}.

Add to A the four columns
  {0,1,2}, {0,3,4}, {1,3,5}, {2,4,5}.
Add to A' instead
  {0,1,3}, {0,2,4}, {1,2,5}, {3,4,5}.

This is a Pasch trade: the two four-column families cover exactly the same
check pairs. Each point occurs twice on each side. Thus AA^T=A'A'^T over the
integers. Both complete matrices have row and column weight three, no repeated
check pair, and binary rank eleven. Their (4,2) counts are seven and five.
These small base facts are suitable for direct kernel-evaluated certificates.

Let B be the 7x7 circulant of b(x)=1+x+x^3. Define genuine QC-GHP CSS matrices

  H_X(A) = [A tensor I_7 | I_12 tensor B^T],
  H_Z(A) = [I_12 tensor B | A^T tensor I_7],

and similarly for A'. The two products commute over F_2. Both are connected
[[168,6]] codes (distance unspecified), with rank(H_X)=rank(H_Z)=81. Their X
Tanner graphs are (3,6)-biregular with girth six. Their entire integer X-side
Gram matrices agree:

  H_X H_X^T = (AA^T) tensor I_7 + I_12 tensor (B^T B).

Consequently every M_j agrees, not merely its eigenvalues. Nevertheless,

              N_(4,2)(H_X(A)) = 49,
              N_(4,2)(H_X(A')) = 35.

The additional (4,0) counts are both 91, so the difference is not obtained by
simply changing that companion count. Code length, logical dimension, degree
parameters and the X-side spectral data all match. No decoder comparison or
minimum-distance equality is asserted.

Structural proof of the lift counts: with constant base labels, any proper
mixed six-cycle would have either two left variables sharing a check (forcing
their fiber coordinates to agree), or two right variables sharing a check
(forcing their row types to agree). In either case the left/right cross
incidences would all use one check, contradicting properness. Thus every proper
six-cycle is wholly left or wholly right. The two triangles of a (4,2) diamond
share the central pair, so the whole set is of one type. Left-only counts are
seven copies of the base counts. Right-only sets lie in one row group, and the
circulant B^T is the Fano incidence matrix, which has no (4,2) set. Hence 49 and
35 follow. Independent full-matrix direct enumeration also checks both values.

The obstruction is specifically to formulas using only the M_j. Full D_j,
polynomial supports, or equivalent incidence information can support (2).

A subtraction-free modular alternative
--------------------------------------
For an ordered central representative alpha=((p,0),(q,delta)), order each
endpoint's two remaining checks as x0,x1 and y0,y1. Let C(x,y) be the unique
variable incident to both checks, or undefined if none exists. No four-cycle
ensures uniqueness. For each permutation sigma of {0,1}, put

  w = C(x0,y_sigma(0)),       z = C(x1,y_sigma(1)).

Let e_alpha be the number of these two permutations for which w,z exist and
N(w) intersect N(z) is empty. This also excludes w=z. The matching covers the
two exclusive checks at each central endpoint. Each elementary diamond has one
such matching at each of its two central orders, so

  N42 = (ell/2) sum_alpha e_alpha.                              (3)

The modular completion test in Theorem 2 makes (3) explicit in the occupied
polynomial sockets. It uses only four potential completing variables per root,
and two matching tests. This is an alternative written proof/implementation
route; it is not claimed as the first exhaustive enumeration method. For the
reduced witnesses, the normalized matching sums are 14 and 10, agreeing with
the ordered-leaf sums 28 and 20 in (1)/(2).

Details of the Fourier bridge
-----------------------------
For a check row type r and fiber t, let y_r(t)=(H b)_(r,t). Socket incidence gives

  y_r(t) = sum_p sum_{(r,e) in sockets(p)} b_p(t-e).

Changing variables a=t-e in the finite DFT shows

  y_hat_r(j) = sum_p [sum_{(r,e) in sockets(p)} omega^(-je)] b_hat_p(j),

which is exactly (D_j b_hat_j)_r. Orthogonality is the finite geometric-series
identity sum_j omega^(j(a-b))=ell when a=b modulo ell, and zero otherwise.
Expanding sum_j conjugate(y_hat_r(j))*y_hat_r(j), interchanging finite sums, and
applying this identity leaves ell*sum_t |y_r(t)|^2. Summing over r proves (2).
This proof works for every positive ell, including composite/even ell. It never
assumes that a four-variable set has a free translation orbit. Only its ordered
central roots are normalized, and that action is free. The paper's opposite
polynomial/DFT sign convention only relabels j by -j in the full frequency sum.

Why the normal-graph qualification matters
------------------------------------------
Here is a four-variable, eight-check cubic/no-four-cycle example:

  N(0)={0,1,3}, N(1)={0,2,4}, N(2)={0,5,6}, N(3)={1,2,7}.

Its global variable projection has five edges, hence is a diamond. But check 0
meets three selected variables, so the configuration is not elementary and its
N42 count is zero. Thus the classical normal graph of an already elementary
set cannot be confused with every induced diamond in the global projection.
The proper-neighbor exclusion in (1) removes this obstruction.

The three-row-group column-weight-three SCB mother/daughter family in the

all seven would require different groups. The witness cannot fit that

For an exact numerical view of the trace obstruction, the two full global
variable projections have the same traces tr(A_VN^2)=2520,
tr(A_VN^3)=12810 and tr(A_VN^4)=157136. These were independently evaluated with
ordinary integers. More generally, equal check Grams and equal dimensions imply
the same singular values and hence equal traces of every power of
A_VN=H_X^T H_X-3I, even though the elementary (4,2) counts differ.

Two further predecessor checks strengthen these limits. Abu-Surra et al.

Dehghan–Banihashemi (2019), Sections III/VI, already gives classical

trapping-set distributions under switching. General spectral information loss
is therefore not a new idea. The inspected text does not prove the concrete

A smaller scalar-frequency obstruction found late in the block
--------------------------------------------------------------
Take ell=24 and use the generalized-bicycle construction (the r1=n1=1
subfamily of QC-GHP). In F2[x]/(x^24-1), put

  a(x)=1+x+x^5,
  b(x)=1+x^2+x^10,        b'(x)=1+x^2+x^16=x^2 b(x^-1).

For each choice of b or b', set HX=[A | B^T], HZ=[B | A^T], where A,B
are the corresponding 24x24 circulants. The two Tanner graphs are cubic,
check-degree six, connected and have no four-cycles. Exact binary elimination
gives rankX=rankZ=22, hence both constructions are [[48,4]] (distance
unspecified). At every frequency, |b'(omega^j)|=|b(omega^j)|, so the scalar
M_j=|a(omega^j)|^2+|b(omega^j)|^2 agrees. Their full integer check Grams agree too.

Nevertheless N42 is 192 for b and 72 for b'. Their normalized ordered-root
sums are 32 and 12, giving (24/4)*32 and (24/4)*12. A separate C++ program
checks every one of the 194,580 four-subsets in each graph, agreeing with
independent Python definition-based enumeration and the exact socket formula.
Their N40 values are 0 and 24. Thus the 168-qubit example remains useful for
its stronger matched-N40 property, while this 48-qubit pair gives a simpler
obstruction already in the scalar generalized-bicycle subfamily.

Run reproduce_gb48.py for independent expansion, commutation, ranks,
