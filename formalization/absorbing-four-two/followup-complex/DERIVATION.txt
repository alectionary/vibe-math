WHY THE COMPLEX FORMULA COUNTS THE REQUIRED SUPPORTS

This is an explanatory companion to the checked theorem interfaces in
SOURCE-MAP.txt. All graphs below are finite binary incidences: a variable v
has three distinct check neighbors H(v), and two distinct variables share at
most one check. The latter condition is precisely absence of Tanner four-cycles.

1. THE LOCAL COUNT

Fix an ordered adjacent pair (u,v). Let W consist of the other variables w
that share a check with each of u,v, while no check belongs to all of u,v,w.
With the hypotheses above, the three pairwise shared checks are distinct.
Thus u,v,w form a genuine six-cycle. Ordinary common neighbors in the
variable projection would also include triples meeting at one check; W
explicitly removes those false completions.

Put t=|W| and k_c=|{w in W : c in H(w)}|. The incidence energy is

  E(W) = sum_c k_c^2.

Expanding the squares counts ordered pairs (w,z), weighted by the number of
their shared checks. Diagonal pairs contribute 3t. Every adjacent off-diagonal
pair contributes one, and every nonadjacent pair contributes zero. Consequently

  # ordered distinct nonadjacent pairs in W = t^2+2t-E(W).

This is PairEnergy.missing_pair_energy_identity, written over natural numbers
as a positive equality to avoid subtraction truncation.

2. WHY THE DIVISOR IS FOUR

An ordered nonadjacent pair w,z in W gives an elementary (4,2) support
{u,v,w,z}. Its variable projection is a diamond: the central vertices u,v
have degree three, and w,z have degree two. The proper-completion and
nonadjacency requirements prevent check identifications that would destroy
elementarity. Conversely every elementary (4,2) support has exactly this
shape under cubicity and absence of four-cycles, and is absorbing.

The central edge is unique. There are two orders of its endpoints and two
orders of the remaining vertices. Summing the preceding local count over
all ordered central pairs therefore counts each support exactly four times.
This is PairRootBridge.rootPairSum_eq_four_count42, using the checked
four-column classification and root equivalences from the first block.

3. WHY CYCLIC NORMALIZATION CONTRIBUTES N

For socket data S_p subset R x Z/N, variables are (p,a), checks are (r,b),
and (p,a) has check (r,a+e) for each (r,e) in S_p. Simultaneous translation
is an incidence automorphism. Every ordered central pair is uniquely a
translate of ((p,0),(q,d)); the translating element is the first variable's
coordinate. Its orbit therefore has exactly N elements.

This argument concerns ordered roots. It does not assume that the translation
action on unordered four-variable supports is free. Supports with a nontrivial
period do not invalidate the N factor, including for composite N. The
normalization theorem in CyclicNormalization.lean counts these roots directly.

4. WHY THE FOURIER ENERGY HAS A 1/N FACTOR

Let f_p(a) be the indicator that (p,a) belongs to W. Then k_r(b), the number
of its incident W variables, is the finite convolution

  k_r(b) = sum_p sum_(r,e in S_p) f_p(b-e).

Use psi(a)=exp(2*pi*i*a.val/N), omega=exp(2*pi*i/N), and the unnormalized
negative-exponent transform f_hat_p(j)=sum_a psi(-j*a) f_p(a). The shift
convention above gives

  k_hat_r(j) = sum_p D_j[r,p] f_hat_p(j),
  D_j[r,p]  = sum_(r,e in S_p) psi(-j*e).

The standard character is faithful for every positive N. Its exact finite
orthogonality identity gives unnormalized Parseval:

  sum_j ||D_j f_hat(j)||_2^2 = N sum_(r,b) k_r(b)^2 = N E(W).

No primality assumption, numerical approximation, or formal substitute for
the complex exponential is used. ComplexDFT.lean also checks the ordinary
integer-power expression psi(-j*a)=omega^(-j.val*a.val), including modular
wraparound; ComplexMatrixCount.lean checks the ordinary Euclidean norm.

5. THE RESULT AND ITS INFORMATION REQUIREMENT

Combining the four-root multiplicity, N translates per normalized root, and
Parseval gives

  N42(H) = (N/4) sum_(normalized central roots)
             [t^2+2t - (1/N) sum_j ||D_j f_hat(j)||_2^2].

The sum is zero for nonadjacent or identical proposed central endpoints.
The theorem is AbsorbingFourier.complex_root_count_formula. The indicator
and the full rectangular matrix both remain in the statement: f_hat depends
on the proper common-neighbor set determined by the actual incidence.

For the actual 168-column witnesses, the same sockets give D_j=[A | beta_j I]
with beta_j=1+omega^j+omega^(3j). Positive exponents here come from the
transposed right binary block. The source/literal incidence equivalences,
counts 49 and 35, and equality of all their full complex D_j D_j* are checked
in the new modules. Thus even the full evaluated Gram matrices cannot by
themselves replace the incidence information in this formula.

This explanation makes no efficiency or historical-priority claim. Classical
STS counts, shared-cycle detection, and expanded-graph enumeration have
the predecessors and access limits recorded in literature-followup.json.

6. AN INDEPENDENT CONSISTENCY REDUCTION TO THE CLASSICAL STS COUNT

Here is a written specialization of the root argument, not an additional Lean
theorem. Its resulting formula is attributed to Grannell-Griggs-Mendelsohn
(1995), https://grannell.net/Papers/smallbasis.pdf, Section 2, C14. The purpose
is to check that the general incidence correction agrees with that prior result.

Let points be checks and the blocks of a complete STS(v) be variables. For an
ordered intersecting block pair write u={c,a,b}, v={c,d,e}. The five displayed
points are distinct. Each of the four cross pairs (a,d), (a,e), (b,d), (b,e)
lies in exactly one block. Those four blocks are exactly W. They are distinct,
and their third points lie outside {c,a,b,d,e}, by uniqueness of pair coverage.
Write them as

  w11={a,d,x11}, w12={a,e,x12},
  w21={b,d,x21}, w22={b,e,x22}.

Four unordered pairs among these blocks already share a displayed point.
The only possible missing pairs are the two diagonals. Define delta1 to be
one when x11=x22, and delta2 to be one when x12=x21, zero otherwise. Each
diagonal equality makes a Pasch configuration together with u,v. Hence

  t=4, E(W)=20+2(delta1+delta2),
  # ordered missing pairs = 4-2(delta1+delta2).

Each point belongs to r=(v-1)/2 blocks. Thus there are v*r*(r-1) ordered
intersecting block pairs. Every Pasch has four blocks, any ordered pair of
which can serve as u,v; the remaining pair contributes exactly one of the
two deltas. Summing delta1+delta2 therefore gives 12P, where P is the number
of unordered Pasch supports. Summing the local identity and dividing by four
gives

  N42 = v*r*(r-1)-6P = v(v-1)(v-3)/4-6P.

The crucial extra premise is complete pair coverage, used to produce all four
cross-pair blocks. A partial triple system need not contain them; W can have
different sizes. Thus this specialization verifies agreement with the classical
answer without replacing the general socket theorem by an STS-only formula.
The finite consumer report also checks the intermediate root counts for the
four inspected classical systems. None of this establishes new priority.
