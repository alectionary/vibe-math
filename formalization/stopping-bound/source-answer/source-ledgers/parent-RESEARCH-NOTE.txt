L-F16-stopping-bound: a finite obstruction for the scalar-factor QC-GHP bound
24 October 2026

ANSWER

For the positive-degree, binary biregular scalar-factor QC-GHP construction
actually used in Proposition 5.1, the proposed regime cannot give a new stopping-
distance guarantee. With two or more row blocks it is impossible. With one row
block it forces a dense matrix and yields exactly the elementary integer lower
bound two. The raw spectral inequality can hold for dense finite CSS matrices;
that is different from a useful LDPC stopping-distance guarantee.

This answers the bounded parameter-feasibility part in the source's precise
scalar-factor setting. It does not supply a stronger spectral bound, classify all
dense parameters satisfying the raw inequality, or settle arbitrary matrix-factor
or nonabelian lifted products. The canonical inventory target remains partially
addressed because its stronger-bound direction is still open.

SOURCE STATEMENT AND HYPOTHESES

Let A be the real binary expansion of an r-by-n1 group-ring matrix, let B be the
ell-by-ell binary circulant of b, and let

    H = [ A | I_r tensor B^T ].

The source's specialization has r2=n2=1. Assume r,n1,ell are positive; H is binary
and (gamma,k)-biregular; gamma is a positive integer; m=r*ell is the check count,
and N=(n1+r)*ell is the variable count. We require m>=2 so that the second
singular value exists. The singleton-check case r=ell=1 does not define that
quantity. Gamma=0 is also outside the divisions and the intended statement.
There is no additional girth assumption in Proposition 5.1. Connectedness is not
postulated by our theorems. Repeated eigenvalues are counted with multiplicity.

All spectra here are over the REAL expanded incidence matrix, not over F2 and
not normalized by degrees. Write s=sigma_2(H)^2. The Fourier identity is

    M_j = A(omega^j) A(omega^j)^* + |b(omega^j)|^2 I_r.

At frequency zero, b(1) means the ordinary integer support weight, not its parity.
Column regularity in the second block forces wt(b)=gamma. In the r=1 case the
source's lambda_2(M_0) term has no index and is omitted; the full Gram matrix
still has a second eigenvalue when ell>=2.

Proposition 5.1 states, for each nonempty stopping set of size a,

    a (m-a*gamma/2) <= C,       C=s*m^2/gamma^2.

When s<gamma/2, set

    a_- = (m/gamma)(1-sqrt(1-2s/gamma)),
    a_+ = (m/gamma)(1+sqrt(1-2s/gamma)).

These are precisely the source roots (m +/- sqrt(m^2-2*gamma*C))/gamma;
sourceLargeRoot_eq and sourceSmallRoot_eq check both algebraic translations
in Lean. The conclusion is
a DISJUNCTION: a<=a_- OR a>=a_+. A stopping-distance bound a_+ additionally
requires exclusion of every nonempty stopping support in (0,a_-]. Merely finding
s<gamma/2 does not establish that exclusion or a useful lower bound.

The proof's division by a*|T| addresses |T|>0. If T is empty, the displayed
inequality holds because its left side is nonpositive; the omitted division case
is harmless. Tanner graphs are simple because the expanded matrices are 0-1.
Quantum logical distance is a different invariant: stabilizer supports can be
stopping sets and are not excluded here.

THEOREMS AND PROOFS

1. Two or more row blocks: s >= gamma^2, for every finite lift.

The zero-frequency shift already explains the obstruction. M_0 is a positive
semidefinite Gram matrix plus gamma^2 I_r, so all r of its eigenvalues are at
least gamma^2. With r>=2, removal of a single Perron eigenvalue leaves one of
these values in the full nonprincipal spectrum. Thus s>=gamma^2>=gamma/2 for
positive integral gamma. Increasing gamma cannot rescue this regime.

The Lean proof establishes the same result directly for the expanded real
matrix, without taking the Fourier identity as a premise. On the r-dimensional
space of vectors constant within each fiber, the second block contributes
exactly gamma^2 times the squared norm. The first block contributes a sum of
squares. When r>=2, the linear functional giving the coefficient of a top Gram
eigenvector has a nonzero kernel on that space. For a nonzero vector in this
kernel, the ordered spectral expansion bounds its energy above by s times its
squared norm. Comparison proves s>=gamma^2. This argument also covers repeated
top eigenvalues and disconnected graphs.

In fact the first block may be any real matrix and B need only have constant row
sum gamma. The source's cyclic/binary construction is a subclass. The theorem
qc_scalar_factor_no_source_regime derives the needed row sum of B directly from
H's column regularity; it is not an extra spectral assumption.

As a checked consumer, the running 882-variable code's scalar support {0,1,6}
in Z/63Z already forces s>=9 for every first block of the appropriate size.
The paper requires s<1.5 for its column weight three. This is a finite
obstruction, stronger for this purpose than the asymptotic comparison in
Remark 5.2.

2. One row block: necessary density greater than three quarters.

Put r=1, q=n1+1>=2 and m=ell. Edge counting gives k=q*gamma. Binary column
regularity gives gamma<=ell. For any binary (gamma,k)-biregular matrix with m>=2,

    k(m-gamma) <= (m-1)s.                              (T)

Indeed, tr(HH^T)=m*k, every eigenvalue except the largest is at most s, and the
largest is at most gamma*k. The last bound follows directly by binary weighted
Cauchy-Schwarz and the row and column sums. Our proof checks these statements for
the actual sorted Gram spectrum, not an assumed list of eigenvalues.

Consequently, in the one-row case,

    q*gamma*(ell-gamma) <= (ell-1)*s.

If s<gamma/2, positivity permits cancellation and gives

    gamma > ell-(ell-1)/(2q) >= (3ell+1)/4.

Thus each column occupies more than 3/4 of the checks, and each row more than
3/4 of the variables. These matrices cannot form a sparse parity-check family
of unbounded length. This includes polynomial entries in the first block;
restricting those entries to monomials does not create an escape.

3. The remaining source bound is exactly the trivial integer bound two.

Under the same one-row hypotheses and s<gamma/2, Lean proves

    1 < a_+ <= 2,             0 <= a_- < 2.

For the upper bound, (T), q>=2 and gamma<=ell imply

    s*ell^2 >= 2*gamma^2*(ell-gamma).

To see this without a division assumption, multiply (T) by gamma and use
ell^2>=gamma(ell-1) and s>=0. Density gives 2gamma/ell-1>=0. The displayed
inequality is equivalent to

    1-2s/gamma <= (2gamma/ell-1)^2.

Taking nonnegative square roots yields a_+<=2. Strict positivity of the
discriminant and ell/gamma>=1 give a_+>1. The same discriminant and density
bounds give 0<=a_-<2.

Every positive-column-weight binary Tanner graph already has no singleton
stopping set: a nonzero column has a neighboring check of induced degree one.
So every nonempty stopping set has at least two variables. In the surviving
one-row regime this automatically excludes the small branch, and also already
implies a>=a_+. The ceiling of a_+ is exactly two. The source spectral stopping
inequality contributes no additional guarantee.

The final source theorem uses scalarFactorMatrix with the unflattened indices
(Fin 1 x F) and ((C x F) + (Fin 1 x F)). It derives the cardinality equation and
edge-count relation internally. No relabeling, Fourier decomposition, auxiliary
expansion hypothesis, or desired spectral lower bound is added as a premise.

4. A checked dense illustration separates the raw inequality from usefulness.

Take H_X=H_Z to be the all-ones 2-by-4 matrix. Its real Gram matrix is
[[4,4],[4,4]], with eigenvalues 8 and 0. Its column degree is two, so s=0<1.
The roots are a_-=0 and a_+=2. Its nonempty stopping supports are exactly the
subsets of size at least two. H_X H_Z^T=0 over F2, so these are a valid CSS
matrix pair. This illustration is dense and is not a new LDPC family. No
quantum logical-distance or dimension calculation is claimed for it.

PRIOR ART AND CURRENT-STATUS AUDIT

The full ledger is prior-art.json; it records queries, versions, inspected
statements, public code/social matches and failures.

- Existing repository results: the completed cyclic-girth and four-variable
  absorbing-count theorems address different conditions. Their degree/girth
  stopping bounds do not verify s<gamma/2 for this construction.
- Tanner's inspected manuscript is dated 26 July 2000 (journal publication
  2001). Theorem 3.1 gives the classical bit-oriented distance bound. Its proof
  uses connected biregularity and at least two active bits per active check.
- Zhu and Chugg, December 2007, Corollary 6 explicitly extend the classical
  bit-oriented bound to stopping distance:

      d_stop >= N(2gamma-s)/(gamma*k-s).

  It becomes positive at s<2gamma, not at gamma/2. It is a genuine lower bound
  without the source's small/large-branch issue. This older result makes a
  Tanner-style replacement of the weak mixing step available, but does not
  identify a source-regime QC-GHP family. We do not claim that bound as new.
- Remark 5.2's Feng-Li and Li-Sole references concern asymptotic or girth-
  dependent spectral restrictions. Those asymptotic/girth comparisons do not by themselves supply the finite
  dense exception or the actual scalar-factor obstruction. Full original
  retrieval remained incomplete for these two classical sources; those gaps
  are explicitly retained rather than reported as zero-question screens.
- Panteleev-Kalachev, arXiv:1904.02703v3 (12 November 2021), supplies the
  construction. Its general quantum-distance agenda is distinct from graph
  stopping distance, and its footnote 19 already acknowledges special-case
  bounds for C(A,1+x).
- Leverrier-Zemor, arXiv:2202.13641v3 (16 September 2022), Theorems 1-2,
  uses constituent Ramanujan Cayley graphs AND local-code distance, robust
  dual tensors, puncturing resistance and total no-conjugacy. It does not
  assert that the expanded CSS check matrix satisfies s<gamma/2. A good
  constituent graph is not a certificate for the source singular-value test.

Question answered: For the source's scalar-factor QC-GHP form, can Proposition
5.1's raw regime yield a stopping-distance improvement beyond the elementary
bound two? No: multi-row blocks make the inequality impossible; one-row blocks
force density above 3/4 and a source root in (1,2], with the small branch already
excluded by positive degree. All new mathematical proofs and the actual-source
bridges are Lean checked.

Question still open: A genuinely useful replacement/tightening of the spectral
stopping bound; and any corresponding parameter question for more general
matrix-factor or nonabelian lifted products. The work is not a classification of
all dense matrices satisfying the raw inequality.

Next decisive step — FOLLOW-UP: freeze a stronger bound using actual incidence
or stabilizer structure, compare it first with Zhu-Chugg's classical formula,
and test whether it proves a lower bound greater than two on a fixed sparse
scalar-factor QC-GHP family with independently known small stopping supports.
Simply searching higher column weights or improving the constituent Ramanujan
graph does not resolve the finite obstruction proved here.
