\section{Introduction}
We partly replicated Schwabe's (2016) conclusions about psychometric measurement and missing-covariate analysis, with the strongest numerical evidence supporting the Chapter 6 precision ordering rather than the full dissertation's empirical and power conclusions \citep{schwabe2016}. All 108 deletion-minus-full variance-standard-error contrasts were positive with positive multiplicity-adjusted intervals. The illustrative heritability bias was positive in the source (0.03315402) and our reconstruction (0.03118039), and smaller by the geometric tolerance 0.00027219389842495325. Empirical refits, source-repetition Bayesian recovery and the reported full-information power advantage are not established. Eleven full-information bias point estimates reverse the source sign, but none excludes zero after multiplicity adjustment. A blocked comparison is not evidence of nonreplication.

The dissertation asks whether measurement properties can distort genetic and environmental inference, and whether joint modeling of incomplete covariates improves twin analyses. Its 2016 University of Twente version integrates item response theory (IRT) with additive-genetic, shared-environmental and unique-environmental components, denoted A, C and E. It studies latent genetic moderation, observed socioeconomic moderation and incomplete covariates. Chapter 7 qualifies the measurement argument: a common identified scale matters, and item modeling cannot repair poorly informative tails or systematic self-report bias (pp.107--108). We preserve those qualifications.

Our comparison covers seven chapters and Appendices A--G. The 996 linked source records include 26 principal claims and overlapping supporting quantities; they are not 996 independent studies. The first-page table retains all principal claims plus two preselected signed illustrations. Qualitative or compound claims have no invented scalar average. A separate scientific verdict can be partial even when a nonscalar magnitude is not established.

\section{What we did ourselves, and what we took from others}
Our contribution is an independent reconstruction and analytical audit, not a new empirical twin study. Schwabe supplied the model ideas, published designs, numerical summaries, figures and original program specifications. We computed conditional algebraic arguments, generated new fixed-seed data, implemented fits, evaluated convergence, and compared results with source quantities. Printed empirical estimates and digitized figure values remain attributed source evidence.

The Chapter 6 reconstruction comprises 48,000 datasets and 96,000 method fits, paired within datasets: 36 primary conditions and 12 separate sensitivity conditions, each with 1,000 datasets. All 96,000 fits passed the recorded numerical guards; none was discarded. Guard checks do not certify a global likelihood optimum. Response-generation checks for Chapters 2 and 5 comprise 2,650 datasets in 19 conditions and no posterior fits. Recomputing standardized reliability on those same datasets adds no independent datasets.

We used R 4.5.0 \citep{r2025}, OpenMx 2.21.13 for the Gaussian likelihood \citep{neale2016}, and lavaan 0.6.19 for independent baseline comparisons \citep{rosseel2012}. JAGS 4.3.2 with rjags 4-16 retains the non-Gaussian item-level likelihood \citep{rjags2024}; a covariance-only substitute would change it. CODA 0.19.4.1 computes descriptive posterior summaries \citep{coda2006}, and posterior 1.6.1 supplies rank-normalized convergence and effective-sample-size diagnostics \citep{vehtari2021}. Package versions and available execution identities are linked in the artifacts appendix. Ordinary mathematical checks were performed without a Lean certificate.

\section{Does common measurement imply invariant biometric inference?}
A common identified scale supports a restricted population-target result, but it does not guarantee identical finite-data evidence from different item subsets. Under calibrated conditionally independent binary items with positive logistic slopes, two distinct item observations identify the Gaussian latent mean and variance from population marginal and joint probabilities. Cross-twin joint probabilities then identify the covariance through a strictly positive Gaussian covariance derivative \citep{voigtlaender2020}. At each supported moderator value,
\[
 A=2(K_{\rm MZ}-K_{\rm DZ}),\qquad C=2K_{\rm DZ}-K_{\rm MZ},\qquad E=T-K_{\rm MZ}.
\]
Both zygosities must occur at each of two distinct observed moderator levels to identify the stated log-linear coefficients. These are sufficient mathematical hypotheses, not verified properties of the unavailable applications.

Item subsets can give opposite finite likelihood orderings despite identifying the same population parameter. Consider positive ACE points $(1/4,1/4,1/2)$ and $(1/2,1/8,3/8)$, both with total variance one. DZ covariance is $3/8$ at both points, whereas MZ covariance increases from $1/2$ to $5/8$. With known Rasch items, one subset containing concordant successes favors the higher MZ covariance; another with success/failure patterns favors the lower covariance. Positive and negative derivatives of their pattern probabilities establish strict opposite orderings. This is not a claim about opposite global fitted optima or significance decisions, nor an assertion that the dissertation intended exact finite-sample equality.

The scope is narrower than universal IRT invariance. Chapter 2's latent-genetic moderation produces a non-Gaussian marginal model not covered by this conditional Gaussian argument. Ordinal and multidimensional observation kernels require their own identification work. Coherent affine changes of latent scale multiply variances by the squared scale factor; heritability is unchanged, but raw variances are not. Nonlinear transformations can change the model family. The complete argument and assumptions are in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/scale-invariance-evidence.json}{\nolinkurl{handoff/scale-invariance-evidence.json}}.

\section{Empirical interactions and response scales}
The original empirical interaction directions cannot be independently confirmed with the available inputs. Mathematics AxE was reported positive at 2.0837 with its 95\% highest-posterior-density (HPD) interval excluding zero; AxC was 2.6159 with an interval containing zero (Table 3.2). Liberalism AxE was negative at $-2.81$ and AxC positive at $0.54$, both with source HPD exclusions (Table 4.3). The socioeconomic shared-environment slope was $-3.0777$ (Table 5.4). These are source estimates, not our fitted effects. Original NTR/Cito linkage and calibration, Virginia responses, and relevant joint posterior inputs were not recovered by the documented public-source search.

The Chapter 4 dimensionality, nine-item subscale, and IRT-versus-sum-score sign reversals remain blocked empirical targets. The related published conservatism article and supplement provide model and response-category context \citep{schwabeConservatism}; they do not supply the missing original responses. Source-only transformations of reported slopes or intervals cannot replace a joint posterior refit. Chapter 6's thirteen covariate nondetections at the reported 99.6\% HPD criterion likewise cannot be reconstructed from the simulation grid. Nondetection is not a zero effect or equivalence claim.

Software behavior has a narrower evidential role. BayesTwin's pre-defense revision and version 1.0 were inspected separately \citep{bayestwin}. Its public example is simulated, not dissertation empirical data. Agreement of program semantics or replayed examples does not validate the empirical interaction findings. \section{Identification, variance functions and likelihood maxima}
Log-variance moderation removes particular path-sign ambiguities and is conditionally identifiable, but finite-sample likelihood uniqueness is unresolved. With a shared binary moderator $m\in\{0,1\}$, the squared path $(a+bm)^2$ has the same endpoint values after $b$ is replaced by $-2a-b$. Even $a>0$ leaves this reflection unless the endpoint is zero or additional positivity constraints are imposed. For three distinct moderator values, equality of squared affine paths implies equality up to global sign; a strictly positive intercept removes that global ambiguity. The binary and continuous statements therefore need different qualifications (Appendix C).

The exponential variance $V(m)=\exp(\beta_0+\beta_1m)$ is positive and has derivative $\beta_1V(m)$. This proves monotonicity, including the constant zero-slope case, but not concavity of a likelihood. The Chapter 5 hierarchy yields $T=A+C+E$, $K_{\rm MZ}=A+C$ and $K_{\rm DZ}=A/2+C$ under the stated independent innovations; the square-root scaling in Equation 5.14 is essential. Combining these moments with the calibrated observation assumptions establishes population identification and a unique population expected-log-likelihood maximizer.

A finite maximum need not exist for every possible dataset with a free mean. For all-zero binary responses, decreasing the fitted mean improves the likelihood toward an unattained supremum. This defeats a universal existence interpretation, but supplies no example of two finite maxima. The source's statement of ``only one maximum'' (p.72) is thus not established as an at-most-one finite-maximum result. A proper prior changes the objective; no posterior-unimodality or convergence conclusion follows. The full argument is in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/chapter5-analytical-evidence.json}{\nolinkurl{handoff/chapter5-analytical-evidence.json}}.

\section{Missing covariates: precision, bias and power}
Full information reproduced the qualitative precision ordering throughout the reconstructed Chapter 6 grid. Three missingness mechanisms, four covariate counts ($q=2,3,4,5$) and three primary rates (2\%, 6\%, 10\%) supply 36 conditions; all 108 component-specific deletion-minus-full variance-SE contrasts were positive. Paired Student-$t$ intervals with Bonferroni family size 108 excluded zero in every case \citep{dunn1961}. For MCAR with $q=2$ and 2\% missingness, the A contrast was 0.00150235, MCSE 0.00002231, and adjusted interval [0.00142398,0.00158072], versus the source-derived 0.00106356.

The literal plotted variance-SE magnitudes were not reproduced within the conditional source-comparison bands. All 216 points lay outside bands combining our Monte Carlo uncertainty with geometric extraction bounds. Original Monte Carlo uncertainty and the source SE implementation are unavailable. Exploratory path-SE comparisons have only 20 points outside their corresponding bands, but path SE is a different estimand and cannot replace variance SE. Accordingly, the headline precision verdict is partial and its magnitude comparison unresolved. The figure's 3\% label versus the 2\% methods description is retained; 12 additional 3\% conditions are a separate sensitivity family.

MAR deletion reproduced the reported bias directions across all 36 component-condition cells. A bias ranged from $-0.072831$ to $-0.006218$, C from $-0.114958$ to $-0.007669$, and heritability from $0.001416$ to $0.031180$. Adjusted intervals excluded zero in the expected direction in 10 of 12 A cells, all 12 C cells, and 9 of 12 heritability cells. Full-information biases were much smaller descriptively: A [$-0.003192$,0.001336], C [$-0.002616$,0.001017], and heritability [$-0.001390$,0.002654]. None excluded zero after adjustment, which does not establish unbiasedness or equivalence.

The heritability illustration preserves the contrast and its uncertainty. With MAR, five covariates and 10\% missingness, deletion's mean estimate was 0.53118039 against truth 0.5, yielding bias 0.03118039; the source-derived estimate 0.53315402 yielded bias 0.03315402. Our MCSE was 0.00304897 and the exploratory 72-comparison adjusted interval [0.02080654,0.04155423]. The descriptive magnitude is smaller at the geometric bound 0.00027219389842495325, but this is not evidence of a statistically resolved source difference. Eleven full-information point signs reversed relative to the source; none had an adjusted interval excluding zero. Two conditional MAR source-estimate deviations occur at the ambiguous low-missingness level.

Saved-output checks address 21 additional descriptive Chapter 6 targets without adding fits or new hypothesis tests. All 81 adjacent-covariate-count deletion variance-SE point differences are positive. Absolute C deletion bias exceeds absolute A bias in all twelve MAR cells, with both raw and truth-relative comparisons retained. Under 10\% MNAR missingness, full-information E means for two through five covariates are 0.208212, 0.207331, 0.206199 and 0.204763; the last is nearer 0.20 than 0.21. The recommendation-relevant subset has 54 positive pre-existing adjusted precision contrasts, but this does not establish an optimal ``more than 3\%, more than two covariates'' policy. Recovery targets retain biases, MCSEs, RMSEs and between-method differences without inventing an equivalence margin. Five direct-power or unequal-structure targets remain pending.

Precision is not rejection-rate power. The source's general power assertion has no unique quantified alternative, test or rejection rule from which to reconstruct a power comparison. Neither lower SE nor a confidence interval containing zero establishes power or adequacy of twenty items. The Chapter 5 sample-size and item-count claims likewise require appropriate nonzero alternatives and detection fractions, not only posterior standard deviations under null moderation.

The observed-data likelihood and observation kernels were checked separately. The Gaussian covariance construction agrees with Equation 6.5 for $q=2$--5. An exact conditional-variance example gives $2,3/2,12/11,279/286$ as progressively more related observations are supplied, supporting how co-twin information can reduce uncertainty about a missing value. It does not guarantee smaller fitted parameter SE in every dataset. Appendix G's binary kernel uses probabilities 0.001 and 0.999 rather than a deterministic threshold; its continuous observation precision 100 adds variance 0.01 on the standardized scale. These exact semantics are documented in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/chapter6-analytical-evidence.json}{\nolinkurl{handoff/chapter6-analytical-evidence.json}}.

\section{Response generation and Bayesian recovery}
Generated response properties support some descriptive patterns but do not establish parameter recovery or power. Across 100 datasets each, the floor and ceiling designs had raw reliability means 0.908030 and 0.907533, versus the source descriptor 0.90. Mean skewness was positive 0.534467 and negative $-0.543213$, with the expected sign in all 100 datasets of each design. The source's skewness endpoints concern three estimators on one unknown-seed illustration; our replicate means are not that same estimand. The twelve Chapter 2 power-design generation cells had reliability means rounding to 0.92. The four Chapter 5 60-item designs rounded to 0.90, whereas the 20-item mean 0.738196 rounded to 0.74 against the reported 0.75. Neither favorable rounding nor the number of generated datasets supplies a posterior-fit result.

Changing the reliability formula did not explain the key descriptor gaps. Standardized alpha was 0.906973 for the floor design, 0.906451 for the ceiling design and 0.731098 for twenty items. Their paired standardized-minus-raw differences were $-0.001057$, $-0.001082$ and $-0.007099$ with MCSEs 0.0000570, 0.0000591 and 0.0001094. These compare formulas on the same 2,650 datasets; they are not two independent simulation samples. Source participant subset, aggregation and calibration conventions remain uncertain. All 19 conditions are retained below.

The fixed Chapter 2 floor posterior passes the stated operational diagnostic criteria, but supplies no repeated-dataset recovery result. Four adapted chains retained 28,000 draws each for the same seed-301 dataset with 500 twin pairs and 60 items. All fourteen monitored quantities passed the joint thresholds: rank-normalized split/folded $\widehat R<1.01$, bulk and tail effective sample sizes at least 400, and mean-MCSE/posterior-SD at most 0.05. The worst values were $\widehat R=1.001673$, bulk ESS 685.762, tail ESS 1407.532, and relative mean MCSE 0.038211. Diagnostics follow \citet{vehtari2021}. Trace and rank inspection still showed serial dependence and rank differences in variance components; passing these criteria is not proof of convergence or an independent end-to-end posterior validation.

The interaction posterior mean was $-0.123383$, with pooled 95\% HPD [$-0.684694$,0.430699]. All twelve inspected whole- and half-chain HPDs included zero. This stable non-exclusion concerns one generated null-interaction dataset; it does not establish a zero effect, unbiasedness, calibrated detection or the source's 100-dataset detection fraction. Equal-tailed quantile MCSE is not HPD-endpoint MCSE. The shorter original and centered diagnostics failed their joint criteria. Every 12,000-draw centered prefix is identical to the corresponding prefix of the longer run, so those draws are not pooled as independent evidence. Ceiling, nonzero-interaction, larger-sample and Chapter 5 designs require separate validation. \section{Further conditional consequences of the models}
Three additional analytical results clarify what the source models can support without establishing empirical performance. First, the Gaussian observed-moderator identification argument extends to disjoint MZ and DZ moderator supports if each support is not concentrated on three or fewer values and the same exact log-linear ACE functions apply across zygosities. A nonzero sum of at most four real exponentials has at most three distinct zeros, by an induction using Rolle's theorem. Equal conditional covariance functions at four support points must therefore agree everywhere. The ACE inversion then identifies the coefficients. This is a population result requiring calibrated items and available joint probabilities; it is not a guarantee of stable finite-sample extrapolation.

Second, Chapter 2's non-Gaussian latent model has an explicit fixed-parameter moment inverse. With $v_a>0$, $v_c\geq0$, $v_e>0$ and interaction $\beta$, write
\[
 \Theta=\mu+A+C+\sqrt{v_e\exp(\beta A)}Z,\quad
 R=v_e\exp(\beta^2v_a/2),\quad m_3=3v_a\beta R.
\]
The twin covariances identify $v_a,v_c$ as above, total variance gives $R=T-v_a-v_c$, and $\beta=m_3/(3v_aR)$, $\log v_e=\log R-\beta^2v_a/2$. A nonzero interaction gives a nonzero third central moment and therefore a non-Gaussian marginal trait. This inverse identifies latent distributions on a fixed scale; no result shows that finite binary response probabilities identify all these moments. Fixed-parameter moment existence also cannot be exchanged with prior averaging: the source inverse-gamma priors have infinite means.

Third, exact Rasch score likelihood can retain the item model under known calibration. For responses $y_j$, score $S=\sum_j y_j$, and fixed difficulties $b_j$,
\[
 p(y\mid\theta,b)=\frac{\exp(S\theta-\sum_jb_jy_j)}{\prod_j(1+\exp(\theta-b_j))}.
\]
Summing patterns with the same score replaces $\exp(-\sum b_jy_j)$ by a coefficient depending only on the score and difficulties. Their ratio is independent of every latent-model parameter; integrating the same correlated latent law therefore preserves the normalized posterior. This does not justify a rescaled Gaussian sum-score comparator. Unknown calibration or discarded item masks break the argument, and no computational acceleration or recovery improvement has been measured. These three results are our conditional ordinary derivations, not additional source experiments; complete hypotheses are in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/continuation-evidence-packet.json}{\nolinkurl{handoff/continuation-evidence-packet.json}}.

\section{Assumptions}
\subsection{Common calibration and observable joint probabilities}
The population arguments assume known finite positive logistic slopes, a fixed anchored trait scale, conditional independence and sufficient observable within-person and cross-twin joint probabilities. Unknown calibration, informative item selection or absent joint observations require separate identification arguments. Source-scale invariance is not assumed across arbitrary transformations.
\subsection{Gaussian shared-moderator reconstruction}
The Chapter 5 and Chapter 6 covariance arguments assume the stated Gaussian innovations, common ACE functions across zygosity and known genetic correlations. The continuous-support extension additionally assumes exact log-linear functions across the relevant support. Neither the mixed-data application nor latent-genetic moderation is silently treated as this same Gaussian model.
\subsection{Underreported generation choices}
Chapter 5's reconstructed Bernoulli moderator probability is 0.5, consistent with the stated roughly 11\% explained variance but not explicitly specified in the source. Chapter 2's fixed-versus-redrawn difficulty bank and original seeds are unresolved. The generation comparisons therefore test a declared reconstruction. No post-hoc choice of seeds, calibration or equivalence margin is used to force agreement.
\subsection{Comparison precision and uncertainty}
Rounding tolerance is half the source's last printed unit; for derived linear contrasts it is the sum of input bounds. Figure comparisons instead use declared coordinate-extraction bounds. A numerical match would round half up to the source value, close would differ by at most one last-place unit, and mismatch would exceed it, only for a comparable quantity. These labels differ from Monte Carlo uncertainty and reconstruction scope. Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals use independent generated datasets with paired method differences; Bonferroni families retain the original dependence and target grouping. The full plan was specified during production, after baseline information was known, rather than wholly preregistered.

\section{Ambiguities that were resolved}
The analytical checks distinguish literal printed formulas from implemented likelihoods; some source inconsistencies remain unresolved rather than being silently repaired.
\subsection{Environmental-variance ratio example (Chapter 2, p.21)}
The variance-ratio identity is $\Delta=\exp(\beta_1\sqrt{V_A})$, so $\beta_1=\log(\Delta)/\sqrt{V_A}$ for positive $V_A$. The neighboring 0.5 versus 0.2 baseline discrepancy does not change this identity; it does prevent treating both worked numbers as one common-baseline example.
\subsection{Item information (Chapter 3, p.37)}
For $p=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(a(\theta-b))$, information for $\theta$ is $a^2p(1-p)$. At $a=2,\theta=b$, it is 1, whereas the printed formula gives 0.25. Unit slope resolves the narrow formula, but the source plot implementation is unavailable. Additivity requires conditional independence; inverse square-root information is not an arbitrary posterior SD.
\subsection{Genetic shift convention (Chapter 3, pp.41--42)}
The reported 1.63 and 1.85 ratios agree with one genetic standard deviation rather than variance. This identifies the numerical convention without independently recovering the empirical interaction posterior.
\subsection{Category probabilities (Chapter 4, p.56; Appendix B)}
The literal printed category probabilities sum to 1.25 at zero thresholds, while the Appendix B cumulative-exponential probabilities are normalized and equal 1/3 there. The code uses effective transitions indexed by the second and third thresholds. The normalized implemented target and the erroneous printed equations have different verdicts.
\subsection{Heritability interval (Chapter 4, p.64)}
The reported heritability mean 0.54 lies outside the printed HPD interval [0.34,0.51]. The inconsistency is retained; no corrected posterior interval is inferred without the original draws.
\subsection{Sample size (Chapter 5, p.78; Table 5.1)}
The table and results use 8,000 pairs while one design sentence says 80,000. The declared reconstruction uses the table design and preserves this discrepancy; the source design cannot be silently declared exact.
\subsection{Separate-moderator appendix (Chapter 5, pp.74,84)}
The separate-moderator program is Appendix E according to the contents and later text; the Appendix F reference is a locator discrepancy. Neither its source code nor this correction supplies extension-power evidence.
\subsection{Low missingness (Chapter 6, pp.91--102)}
The primary reconstruction uses 2\% from the methods and a separate 3\% sensitivity from plot labels. Both are retained, without selecting the better numerical agreement. The discrepancy affects the low-missingness comparison.
\subsection{Application categories (Chapter 6, p.97; Table 6.1)}
The enumerated and tabulated pedagogical categories are six, despite text saying five. The Reformed-liberated/liberal label discrepancy is retained in the empirical source mapping, not treated as a new measured group.
\subsection{Covariate-count summaries (Chapter 7, p.104; Dutch summary, p.117)}
The simulation includes five covariates as well as two, three and four. Summaries omitting the fifth condition do not justify dropping it from the full grid.
\subsection{Shared-environment direction (Dutch summary, p.116)}
The primary Chapter 4 table reports positive shared-environment moderation; the Dutch summary has the opposite wording. The table is the numerical source, and the translation is not a separate experiment.
\subsection{Observation kernels (Chapter 6, p.97; Appendix G)}
Appendix G uses the 0.001/0.999 binary kernel and continuous residual variance 0.01. These are exact probability-model differences from deterministic observation, not evidence that the empirical fit is wrong.
\subsection{Identification and likelihood (Chapter 5, p.72; Appendix C)}

\section{What could not be established}
The dissertation's broad measurement-remedy, power and empirical conclusions are not settled by the completed narrow checks. Missing original data prevent empirical refits; incomplete repeated posterior experiments prevent source-count recovery and detection fractions; underreported procedures limit exact numerical comparison. Population injectivity does not establish a unique finite-sample optimum. The remaining core Bayesian source designs comprise 3,250 unique datasets and 3,450 dataset-method fits: 200 floor/ceiling datasets with two methods, 1,200 interaction-power/recovery datasets, 100 symmetric-comparator datasets, 1,000 sample-size datasets and 750 additional item-count datasets. These are target requirements, not completed fits. The 2,650 generation-only datasets omit the symmetric condition and the 100- and 250-item conditions; their count must not be interpreted as posterior completion. The following limitations retain the complete scientific scope of the evidence.
\paragraph{Limit 1.} The extended fixed Chapter 2 floor case passes its operational diagnostic, but it does not reproduce the 100-dataset Chapter 2 or 250-dataset Chapter 5 summaries. Other source designs and repeated-dataset recovery remain unestablished.
\paragraph{Limit 2.} Unavailable original empirical responses, calibration, linkage records, joint posterior draws and incompletely reported designs prevent the corresponding independent empirical and exact-design comparisons.
\paragraph{Limit 3.} Figure extraction and arithmetic based on published parameters reuse source evidence. They are not independent data generation or empirical fits.
\paragraph{Limit 4.} The ordinary mathematical arguments are conditional on explicit assumptions. They do not verify empirical calibration, observation mechanisms or support, and no formal proof certificate is supplied.
\paragraph{Limit 5.} The 36 primary Chapter 6 conditions and 12 additional conditions with 3\% missingness are distinct. The source describes 2\% in prose and 3\% in plots at the low level. Geometric extraction bounds are neither source Monte Carlo uncertainty nor equivalence margins.
\paragraph{Limit 6.} Eleven full-information MAR bias point estimates reverse the source sign; none excludes zero after adjustment. Both conditional MAR source-estimate deviations occur at the ambiguous low-missingness level.
\paragraph{Limit 7.} Shorter original and centered Bayesian diagnostics failed their criteria; the extended fixed case passes. This does not complete any 100- or 250-dataset source aggregate, detection fraction or empirical effect.
\paragraph{Limit 8.} Population identification and common-scale arguments are assumption-bound; they do not establish finite-sample likelihood uniqueness. Ordinary analytical support is distinct from formal certification.
\paragraph{Limit 9.} The 996 records include dependent components, parent claims and source-only arithmetic. They are not independent findings and do not define a replication success fraction.
\paragraph{Limit 10.} The source reliability formula, aggregation and calibration are incompletely specified. Rounding agreement of reconstructed means is not an exact-source verdict; standardization does not explain the floor, ceiling or twenty-item gaps.
\paragraph{Limit 11.} Qualitative constancy, slightness, closeness and adequacy have no justified equivalence margins. Descriptive Chapter 6 trends cannot establish those qualifications or a power advantage.
\paragraph{Limit 12.} The conditional proofs do not establish finite-sample maximum existence or uniqueness, nor observed-response identification of the non-Gaussian latent moments.
\paragraph{Limit 13.} All twelve whole- and half-chain interaction HPDs include zero for the fixed null-interaction dataset. Non-exclusion does not establish the null, unbiasedness or detection calibration. Quantile MCSE is not HPD-endpoint MCSE, and identical shorter prefixes are not pooled.

Its Gaussian missing-data design is fully fitted, but model guards are not a global optimum certificate. Generation-only comparisons and one-dataset posterior diagnostics do not stand in for the source's repeated recovery experiments. The full copyrighted dissertation and unlicensed original programs are locally inspected sources, not redistributed data. Conditional mathematical extensions add explicit assumptions and do not silently strengthen the dissertation's claims.
