\section{Introduction}
We partly replicated Schwabe's (2016) headline findings: all 108 reconstructed deletion-minus-full variance-SE contrasts favor full information after multiplicity adjustment, but all 216 literal source SE values fall outside conditional comparison bands. Unknown source Monte Carlo error and SE implementation prevent a definitive numerical replication verdict. The source's full-information power advantage is not established by our precision result. The joint Bayesian convergence gate failed; full Bayesian recovery and power grids and independent empirical effects are not established. Eleven full-information point-bias signs reverse relative to the source, but none excludes zero after adjustment. In the precision illustration, the published 0.00106356 becomes 0.00150235 in our reconstruction, with unresolved magnitude comparability; the MAR deletion heritability bias is 0.03315402 versus 0.03118039, both positive. Its extraction tolerance is 0.00027219389842495325. These point estimates do not establish a statistically different or equivalent effect.

The dissertation asks how measurement and missing covariates affect genetic and environmental inference. Its item response theory (IRT) models separate latent traits from observed item responses; its biometric models separate additive genetic variance (A), shared environment (C), and unique environment (E). It reports scale-induced interactions under sum scores, remedies using item-level models, empirical moderation of mathematics and liberalism, a logvariance moderation model, and gains from full-information treatment of incomplete covariates \citep[Chapters 1--7]{schwabe2016}. These are distinct scientific targets. Success on covariance algebra or a reconstructed simulation does not supply missing empirical observations or establish source-count Bayesian recovery.

Our coverage comprises all seven chapters and Appendices A--G, with 996 linked records and 26 principal claims. Records include parents, shared components, source-only calculations and blocked targets. They are not 996 independent experiments, and no replication success fraction is defined. The first-page table preserves all principal claims and two illustrations; its nonscalar rows represent conjunctions or propositions for which no scientific scalar aggregate exists.

\section{Missing covariates: precision and power}
Full information has lower reconstructed variance-component standard errors throughout the primary Chapter 6 grid. We used 1,000 independent datasets in each of 36 primary conditions: three missingness mechanisms, two to five covariates, and missing fractions 0.02, 0.06 and 0.10. Both full-information and twin-wise deletion methods were fitted to each dataset. Twelve additional conditions at 0.03 missingness are a separate sensitivity analysis. Thus 48,000 datasets produce 96,000 completed method fits, comprising 72,000 primary and 24,000 sensitivity fits. All pass the recorded numerical guards; no fits are excluded. A guard is not a certificate of a global maximum.

The primary precision comparison is deletion minus full-information mean variance-component SE. All 108 differences are positive, and all 108 Bonferroni-adjusted Student intervals exclude zero positively. The illustration (MCAR, two covariates, 2\% missingness, A variance) has difference 0.00150234874, paired Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) 0.00002230935, and adjusted interval [0.00142397666, 0.00158072082]. Its source point difference is 0.00106356032. All 216 literal plotted variance-SE values lie outside the conditional source-comparison bands. The source's SE implementation and Monte Carlo uncertainty were not recovered, so these discrepancies do not establish nonreplication of a fully comparable numerical estimand. The exploratory path-SE difference 0.00106818378 is a different estimand and cannot replace variance SE.

The missingness mechanisms are missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR), and missing not at random (MNAR). Under MAR, deletion bias directions agree with the source in all 36 component-condition cells: A and C are underestimated and residual heritability $h^2=A/(A+C+E)$ is overestimated. Adjusted intervals exclude zero in 10 of 12 A cells, all 12 C cells, and 9 of 12 heritability cells. A bias spans [-0.07283115,-0.00621756], C bias [-0.11495778,-0.00766858], and heritability bias [0.00141622,0.03118039]. Full-information bias ranges are respectively [-0.00319176,0.00133589], [-0.00261639,0.00101715], and [-0.00139029,0.00265364]; none excludes zero after adjustment. Those null-compatible intervals do not establish zero bias or equivalence.

Eleven full-information point-bias directions reverse relative to the source, but none excludes zero after adjustment. The two conditional MAR source-estimate deviations both occur at the ambiguous low missingness level. The heritability illustration at MAR, five covariates and 10\% missingness has bias 0.03118038817, MCSE 0.00304897252, and exploratory adjusted interval [0.02080654405,0.04155423228]; its source bias is 0.03315402096. Full condition-level signed estimates, source deviations and uncertainty are retained in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/production/chapter6-v1/terminal-analysis/signed-SE-contrasts.csv}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{signed-SE-contrasts.csv}}} and \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/production/chapter6-v1/terminal-analysis/signed-bias-contrasts.csv}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{signed-bias-contrasts.csv}}}.

\section{Conditional analytical results}
Common-scale population identification can hold even when item subsets change finite-data likelihood evidence. Under the assumptions below, two distinct conditionally independent calibrated binary observations identify a Gaussian trait's mean and variance; cross-twin joint probabilities identify the interior covariance. With $T=A+C+E$, $K_{MZ}=A+C$ and $K_{DZ}=A/2+C$, the inverse relations are
\[
 A=2(K_{MZ}-K_{DZ}),\qquad C=2K_{DZ}-K_{MZ},\qquad E=T-K_{MZ}.
\]
Both zygosities at two moderator levels identify the stipulated log-linear variance coefficients. Correct specification and injectivity yield a unique population expected-log-likelihood maximizer by the Kullback--Leibler argument. They do not establish finite-sample likelihood concavity or uniqueness.

An exact finite example shows why this distinction matters. On the same common scale, let P have $(A,C,E)=(1/4,1/4,1/2)$ and Q have $(1/2,1/8,3/8)$ at both moderator levels. Both have total variance 1 and DZ covariance 3/8, while MZ covariance rises from 1/2 to 5/8. Four distinct Rasch observations per twin have slope 1 and difficulty 0. In MZ families, one two-item subset is all ones in both twins; the other is all ones in one twin and all zeros in the other. Gaussian covariance differentiation \citep[Corollary 2]{voigtlaender2020} gives opposite strict likelihood orderings of P and Q for the two subsets. DZ factors cancel. The configuration has positive probability and retains the required support. This is not a claim about opposite global MLEs or significance decisions, and it does not assert that Schwabe intended exact finite-sample invariance.

The logvariance formulation has useful elementary properties without proving likelihood uniqueness. For $V(m)=\exp(b_0+b_1m)$, its derivative has the sign of $b_1$; at zero slope it is constant. For a squared path $(a+bm)^2$ with shared binary moderator, the reflection $b^*=-2a-b$ preserves both endpoints while keeping $a>0$. Distinct reflected slopes require $a+b\ne0$. Unrestricted global sign symmetry extends to continuous moderators, but with three distinct support values and a strictly positive intercept, equality of squared affine paths forces equality of the paths. An all-zero response dataset with a free common mean has a likelihood supremum approached as the mean tends to minus infinity, without a finite maximizer. This refutes universal finite-MLE existence, a stronger interpretation than the source wording; uniqueness when a maximum exists remains unresolved \citep[p.72; Appendix C]{schwabe2016}.

The Chapter 6 Gaussian covariance construction is an exact model identity. If $x=(x_1,x_2)$ has diagonal covariance blocks $S=W+B$ and cross-block B, and $y_j=\mu+\beta^T x_j+u_j$ with residuals independent of x, then $\operatorname{Var}(y_j)=A+C+E+\beta^TS\beta$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(y_1,y_2)=\rho A+C+\beta^TB\beta$. Conditioning on observed variables reduces missing-value conditional variance by a positive semidefinite Schur-complement term. Strict reduction requires nonzero residual conditional covariance. An exact example has successive conditional variances $2,3/2,12/11,279/286$. This known-parameter prediction result does not guarantee smaller estimated biometric SEs or higher rejection-rate power.

\section{Methods and comparison rules}
Our uncertainty calculations distinguish simulation error from source reconstruction error. MCSE is the across-dataset standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of valid replicates; paired contrasts use within-dataset differences. Two-MCSE bands are descriptive initial comparisons. Bonferroni-adjusted Student intervals control the specified familywise error at 0.05 \citep{dunn1961}: 108 primary variance-SE contrasts, 36 sensitivity contrasts, separate exploratory path-SE families of 108 and 36, 216 source-point comparisons per SE metric, and 72 MAR bias comparisons. Shared simulation and parameter dependence is retained; records are not independent trials. Source-comparison bands add the geometric extraction bound to our uncertainty band. They omit unknown source Monte Carlo error and are not full two-sample confidence intervals.

Numerical agreement and reconstruction scope answer different questions. A numerical match rounds half up to the printed source value; ``close'' differs by at most one unit of its last printed decimal; a larger comparable gap is a mismatch. Scope is exact only for the same input, estimator and procedure, limited for reconstructions, and unavailable for missing inputs. Figure coordinates lack printed decimal precision, so no ordinary rounding verdict is assigned. The pre-production extraction bound uses 0.25 PDF point per coordinate propagated through axis scaling and summed for contrasts. Earlier baseline outcomes were known; the complete plan was recorded after launch but before inspecting the new production effects, so it was not wholly preregistered. The ordering result and conditional bands do not establish source equivalence or direct detection power.

R 4.5.0 supplies statistical execution \citep{r2025}; OpenMx 2.21.13 fits the reconstructed Gaussian likelihood \citep{neale2016}. lavaan 0.6.19 provides an independent baseline covariance fit \citep{rosseel2012}. Their baseline variance estimates agree within approximately $5\times10^{-8}$, while their SEs are not identical. JAGS 4.3.2 with rjags 4-16 is used because the item-level non-Gaussian likelihood is not a covariance-only SEM \citep{rjags2024}; CODA supports historical diagnostics \citep{coda2006}, and posterior 1.6.1 supplies the rank-based diagnostics. These checks do not independently validate the full moderated posterior. The terminal audit recomputed the same saved trials. The Bayesian outcome audit used the same saved draws and did not independently reimplement ESS or MCSE. Exact algebra checks share the source model assumptions. These are independent checks of specified calculations, not independent end-to-end studies. Exact runtime records are in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/provenance/runtime-packages.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{runtime-packages.json}}} and \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/provenance/posterior-runtime-packages.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{posterior-runtime-packages.json}}}.

\section{Assumptions}
\subsection{Reconstructed covariate allocation}
Equal covariate coefficients are our reconstruction choice because the original driver and exact allocation are unavailable. The 2\% design is primary and 3\% is a separate sensitivity case; neither resolves the source's conflicting labels. The Gaussian latent model and observation mechanisms must be correctly specified for their statistical interpretation.
\subsection{Common calibrated observation scale}
Our population argument assumes known finite positive logistic slopes and finite intercepts on one anchored scalar scale, at least two distinct conditionally independent observations per subset, independent cross-twin measurement errors, and observable within-person and cross-twin joint probabilities. Subsets are prespecified or their selection model is included. Calibration alone does not identify unobserved joint moments.
\subsection{Gaussian components and moderator support}
The identification bridge assumes strictly positive Gaussian ACE components, common conditional means and variances across zygosity, independent families, genetic correlations 1 and 1/2, and positive joint support for both zygosities at each of two distinct shared observed moderator levels. Means are linear and component variances log-linear in that moderator. These are sufficient mathematical assumptions, not verified empirical properties. They exclude Chapter 2's non-Gaussian latent-genetic interaction family and broader ordinal or multidimensional measurement models.
\subsection{Conditional formulas and numerical checks}
Gaussian conditional formulas require nonsingular observed covariance blocks and fixed known parameters. Strict information gain requires nonzero conditional covariance. Numerical guard validity does not establish global optimality, missingness ignorability or population model validity. No formal proof-assistant bridge is supplied.

\section{Ambiguities that were resolved}
\subsection{Variance ratios versus standard-deviation ratios}
The source environmental-variance ratio over one genetic standard deviation is $\Delta=\exp(\beta_1\sqrt{V_A})$, hence $\beta_1=\log(\Delta)/\sqrt{V_A}$ for $V_A>0$. The SD ratio is $\sqrt\Delta$. At $V_A=0$ this inverse is undefined. The general identity is established, but the Chapter 2 example's change from environmental baseline 0.2 to the multiplication $0.5\times1.5=0.75$ is not repaired by it. Chapter 3's reported ratios 1.63 and 1.85 correspond to a one-SD shift despite text describing a variance shift. These are source arithmetic/interpretation issues, not independent empirical successes; see \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/narrow-analytical-evidence.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{narrow-analytical-evidence.json}}}.
\subsection{Information on the trait scale}
The printed Chapter 3 Eq.3.4 omits the squared discrimination factor. For Bernoulli probability $p=\operatorname{logistic}(a(\theta-b))$, the score is $a(Y-p)$ and information is $a^2p(1-p)$. At $a=2,\theta=b$, this is 1 rather than the printed 1/4. The formula is correct for unit slope or the item logit coordinate, not arbitrary common-trait slopes. Information adds under conditional independence and known calibration; inverse square-root information is a conventional information-based SE, not every finite-sample or posterior SD. The plot implementation is unavailable, so a printed discrepancy does not prove an implemented error.
\subsection{Ordinal probabilities versus the printed formulas}
Appendix B's cumulative-exponential weights normalize and give the stated adjacent-category logits. The literal Chapter 4 formulas instead produce probabilities $1/2,1/2,1/4$ at the zero setting, summing to $5/4$, while Appendix B gives three probabilities $1/3$. Its first cumulative term cancels and effective transitions use the second and third thresholds. This establishes implemented-kernel normalization and a separate printed-formula discrepancy, not empirical reproduction. Evidence and source locators are in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/narrow-analytical-evidence.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{narrow-analytical-evidence.json}}}.
\subsection{Binary and continuous observation kernels}
Appendix G uses probabilities 0.001 and 0.999 around a zero liability threshold, not a deterministic threshold. Marginally $P(X=1)=0.001+0.998\Phi(\mu/\sigma)$ and independent observation errors multiply binary cross-covariance by $0.998^2$. The continuous school-size observation has precision 100, hence residual variance 0.01 on its standardized scale. The phenotypic regression uses observed or imputed X, not its latent expectation. These semantics differ from the continuous joint-Gaussian simulation and do not establish posterior convergence; see \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/chapter6-analytical-evidence.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{chapter6-analytical-evidence.json}}}.
\subsection{Indexing and support of the source designs}
Appendix E contains the separate-moderator extension referred to as Appendix F on p.74. The Chapter 6 table lists six pedagogical categories despite prose saying five; its Reformed category label varies. The primary design includes five covariates despite shorter summary lists. Primary Chapter 4 positive AxC values are retained despite the opposite Dutch-summary wording. These are resolved indexing or source-selection decisions, not new experiments. All source conflicts, including unresolved ones, are retained in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/evidence-packet.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{evidence-packet.json}}}.

\section{What remains unresolved}
The principal unresolved causes are missing inputs, incomplete validated posterior targets, and source implementation ambiguity. For empirical claims, the public-source search found original model programs and a supplement but no matching responses, linkage or calibration files. Printed values and BayesTwin examples cannot substitute for those inputs. The required settling artifacts are the original datasets, calibration/linkage records, exact model versions and joint posterior draws.

For Bayesian recovery, short and source-length single-dataset diagnostics and a four-chain check were available, but the joint convergence gate failed. Mixing is the immediate demonstrated obstacle; the missing repeated-dataset aggregates remain a separate requirement. The settling evidence is a validated sampling procedure with joint diagnostics followed by the source-count 100/250-dataset designs. A favorable single coefficient or probability cannot settle that family. Historical diagnostic values and their limited scopes are retained in \href{https://github.com/alectionary/vibe-math/blob/master/formalization/schwabe-psychometric-genetics/handoff/historical-diagnostic-evidence.json}{\mbox{\nolinkurl{historical-diagnostic-evidence.json}}}.

For Chapter 6 numerical fidelity, the complete fixed grid settles our reconstruction's ordering, but not the original SE calculation. Variance versus path SE and 2\% versus 3\% are the leading identified ambiguities; an original plotting/fitting driver and its replicate-level SEs would distinguish them. More repetitions of the same reconstruction cannot recover that provenance. The source power assertion requires a rejection-rate experiment, not only smaller SEs.

For the broad scale-invariance and likelihood-uniqueness statements, population identification is conditionally established, but broader measurement and latent-genetic families are not covered. The all-zero example settles only nonexistence under a universal finite-MLE reading. A proof or counterexample under explicit nondegenerate support and parameter constraints is needed for at-most-one finite maximum. Actual empirical calibration and observation assumptions are independently unresolved.

Other printed inconsistencies remain explicit: Chapter 4's heritability mean 0.54 lies outside its printed HPD [0.34,0.51]; Chapter 5 prose says 80,000 pairs while its table/results say 8,000; the low-missingness labels disagree. We do not silently replace these numbers. They are source discrepancies, not demonstrated failures of independent fitted estimates.
\subsection{Scope limits of the evidence}
\textbf{L0.} Single-dataset posterior runs and short pilots do not reproduce the source 100-dataset Chapter 2 or 250-dataset Chapter 5 summaries. Adequate joint convergence and repeated-dataset uncertainty are not established.

\textbf{L1.} Unavailable original empirical responses, calibration and linkage records, joint posterior draws, and underreported designs prevent the corresponding independent empirical or exact-design comparisons.

\textbf{L2.} Figure geometry and arithmetic from printed parameters reuse source evidence. They do not reproduce the original data generation or empirical fits.

\textbf{L3.} Conditional ordinary mathematical arguments do not supply formal certification or verify empirical calibration, observation and support assumptions.

\textbf{L4.} The precision grid contains 36 primary conditions and 12 additional 3\% missingness sensitivity conditions. The source discrepancy between 2\% in prose and 3\% in plots is unresolved. Source-derived geometric bounds are neither original Monte Carlo uncertainty nor equivalence margins.

\textbf{L5.} The MAR A, C and heritability comparisons contain 11 full-information point-sign reversals; none excludes zero after adjustment. Both conditional MAR source-estimate deviations occur at ambiguous low missingness.

\textbf{L6.} The Chapter 2 IRT bias-remedy and power claims and the Chapter 5 Bayesian claims are not established by the bounded four-chain, one-dataset diagnostic, which fails its joint convergence gate. No source 100- or 250-dataset aggregate, power fraction or empirical effect is inferred from it.

\textbf{L7.} The scale-invariance and Chapter 5/6 analytical results are assumption-bound ordinary arguments, not Lean-certified proofs. Population identification is not finite-sample likelihood uniqueness.

\textbf{L8.} The whole-dissertation inventory contains 996 linked records, including shared components, source-only arithmetic and parents. They are not 996 independent findings, and no replication success fraction is defined.
