\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
We partly replicated Schwabe's (2016) finding that psychometric modelling improves behavioural-genetic inference: the reconstructed missing-covariate experiment supports the full-information precision ordering across all 108 comparisons, but does not establish direct power, empirical applications, or the complete Bayesian recovery claims. The published and reconstructed deletion-minus-full-information standard-error gains in the first illustrative cell are 0.00106356 and 0.00150235; the corresponding source and reconstructed heritability biases in the second illustration are 0.03315402 and 0.03118039. Source-figure extraction margins are respectively 0.00023667095377570213 and 0.00027219389842495325. The first magnitude is unresolved because the original standard-error implementation is unknown; the second point magnitude is smaller. Neither comparison is an equivalence test. Eleven full-information bias estimates reverse the source point sign, with no adjusted interval excluding zero.

The scientific question is whether modelling measurement and missingness changes conclusions about genetic and environmental variation. The 2016 University of Twente dissertation, \emph{Nature, Nurture and Item Response Theory: A Psychometric Approach to Behaviour Genetics}, combines item response theory (IRT), twin variance models, simulations, empirical applications and software \citep{schwabe2016}. IRT models the probability of each item response given a latent trait. The twin model separates additive genetic variance ($A$), shared environmental variance ($C$) and individual environmental variance ($E$); monozygotic and dizygotic genetic correlations are fixed at 1 and $1/2$. These model assumptions matter as much as numerical agreement.

The unit of scientific replication is a substantive proposition and its design, not an inventory row. Our coverage includes all seven chapters and Appendices A--G, with 996 linked original-claim records, 26 principal claims, two preselected numerical illustrations and 187 retained headline component comparisons. Parents share their constituent datasets, estimates and assumptions. The records therefore define neither 996 independent findings nor a replication success fraction. Translated summaries and repeated conclusions map to their original chapters; cited background and future proposals are not new completed experiments.

The dissertation supplied the models, designs, source tables and figures. Our work supplied original reconstruction code, generated data, fitted values, analytical arguments, uncertainty calculations and explicitly stated choices where source details were absent. Reading a number from a figure or evaluating a formula using printed parameters is source-based arithmetic, not an independent empirical refit.

\section{Missing covariates: precision, bias and power}\label{sec:ch6}
Full information improves precision in the reconstructed Chapter 6 design, while the source's broader power assertion is not established. The primary grid crosses missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR) and missing not at random (MNAR), two through five covariates, and missing fractions 0.02, 0.06 and 0.10. An additional 0.03 grid addresses the source's conflict between 2\% prose and 3\% plot labels. It does not silently replace the primary design. The comparison concerns residual ACE components, not phenotype variance inclusive of covariate explanation.

\subsection{The precision ordering is reproduced, its literal magnitude is unresolved}
All 108 signed variance-standard-error contrasts, deletion minus full information, are positive. Each of the 108 Bonferroni-adjusted Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals also lies above zero. Pairing preserves the fact that both methods fit the same generated dataset; the Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) is the standard deviation of replicate differences divided by the square root of the replicate count. Familywise adjustment is over the 108 primary contrasts, using the Bonferroni principle \citep{dunn1961}. This is evidence about the reconstructed grid's mean precision ordering.

All 216 literal plotted variance-standard-error points fall outside the conditional source-comparison bands. That diagnostic disagreement cannot by itself establish failure to reproduce the original estimand: original standard-error implementation and source Monte Carlo uncertainty are unavailable. A path standard error concerns a different parameter from a variance standard error. Its exploratory agreement must not replace the dissertation's stated variance estimand. The full signed values and conditional comparison records are retained in \file{production/chapter6-v1/terminal-analysis/signed-SE-contrasts.csv} and \file{production/chapter6-v1/terminal-analysis/source-comparisons.csv}.

For MCAR, two covariates and 2\% missingness, the source A-variance SE gain is 0.0010635603 and ours is 0.0015023487. Our paired MCSE is 0.0000223093; the 108-family adjusted interval is [0.0014239767, 0.0015807208]. Both point signs favour full information. The geometric source margin is 0.0002366710, but source-estimand comparability is unresolved, so the magnitude label remains not established. The conditional point difference of 0.0004387884 is not a new scientific contrast or evidence of equivalence.

\subsection{MAR bias directions agree without establishing full-information unbiasedness}
Deletion underestimates A and C and overestimates residual heritability in all 12 MAR cells for each component. Adjusted intervals exclude zero in the stated direction in 10 A cells, all 12 C cells and nine heritability cells. The point ranges are respectively [-0.07283115, -0.00621756], [-0.11495778, -0.00766858] and [0.00141622, 0.03118039]. The complete 36-cell directional finding is not 36 independent discoveries.

Full-information point biases are much nearer zero in this reconstruction, with ranges [-0.00319176, 0.00133589] for A, [-0.00261639, 0.00101715] for C and [-0.00139029, 0.00265364] for heritability. None of their adjusted intervals excludes zero. This does not establish zero bias or the source's qualitative claim of closeness: no scientific equivalence margin was specified. Eleven full-information point signs reverse their source signs; none of those reversals is supported by an adjusted exclusion of zero. Two conditional MAR deviations from source estimates occur at the ambiguous low missingness level.

For deletion with five covariates and 10\% MAR missingness, the generating residual heritability is 0.5. The source mean 0.53315402 gives signed bias 0.03315402; our mean 0.53118039 gives bias 0.03118039. Our point magnitude is smaller at the geometric margin 0.0002721939, and the direction is the same. Our MCSE is 0.00304897 and the exploratory 72-family adjusted interval is [0.02080654, 0.04155423]. The magnitude label is descriptive, not a test of a difference from the source. All 72 signed MAR bias comparisons are retained in \file{production/chapter6-v1/terminal-analysis/signed-bias-contrasts.csv}.

\subsection{Additional trends do not settle power or adequacy}
All 81 adjacent-covariate-count deletion variance-SE point differences in the saved assessment are positive. Absolute C bias exceeds absolute A bias in all 12 primary MAR cells, but no new cross-parameter interval or test was run. With 10\% MNAR missingness, full-information E means for two through five covariates are 0.20821236, 0.20733095, 0.20619935 and 0.20476346. The last is nearer 0.20 than 0.21, unlike the first three. The source's per-condition outputs are unavailable.

Twenty-one of 26 saved targets have complete descriptive assessments. Direct MCAR, MAR and MNAR power targets, unequal zygosity structure, and the broad power assertion remain pending. The recommendation-relevant subset has 54 positive adjusted precision contrasts out of 54, but this does not establish an optimal threshold of more than 3\% missingness and more than two covariates. A power comparison needs a specified nonzero alternative, test and rejection rule, followed by repeated-dataset rejection rates. Precision alone does not supply it. The signed assessment is \file{outputs/chapter6-saved-target-assessment.json}.

\section{Measurement scales and mathematical identification}\label{sec:theory}
Conditional population results support parts of the dissertation's mathematical argument, but do not establish universal item-subset invariance or a unique finite-sample likelihood maximum. These are ordinary mathematical derivations, not proof-assistant certificates. They neither verify source empirical assumptions nor supply missing posterior experiments.

\subsection{A common scale identifies a target, not identical sample evidence}
With known positive logistic calibrations, distinct locally independent item observations, positive Gaussian ACE components and both zygosities at two shared moderator levels, admissible item subsets identify the same population parameter on the common anchored scale. Within-person marginal and joint probabilities identify the mean and total latent variance. Cross-twin response probabilities then identify latent covariances, because their Gaussian covariance derivative is positive for positive-slope response functions. The covariance derivative identity is a form of Price's theorem; the evidence uses its bounded smooth-function setting \citep{voigtlaender2020}. Recover
\[
 A=2(K_{\rm MZ}-K_{\rm DZ}),\quad C=2K_{\rm DZ}-K_{\rm MZ},\quad E=T-K_{\rm MZ}.
\]
Two moderator values identify the log-linear variance and affine mean coefficients under the stated common functions. Unknown calibration or unobserved joint response moments break this argument.

Equal population targets do not imply equal likelihood evidence in a finite sample. An exact example holds total variance at 1 and compares $(A,C,E)=(1/4,1/4,1/2)$ with $(1/2,1/8,3/8)$. DZ covariance is $3/8$ at both points while MZ covariance rises from $1/2$ to $5/8$. Two admissible Rasch subsets with different observed concordance patterns yield opposite likelihood orderings of these parameter points. This establishes neither opposite unrestricted maximum-likelihood estimates nor opposite significance decisions. The dissertation itself qualifies scaling by common identification and acknowledges weak tail information (pp. 51, 107--108). See \file{handoff/scale-invariance-evidence.json}.

\subsection{Log-variance moderation removes one symmetry, not all inferential difficulties}
For a shared binary moderator $m\in\{0,1\}$ and path $a+bm$ with $a>0$, the reflected slope $b^*=-2a-b$ leaves both endpoint squared paths unchanged. The alternatives coalesce if $a+b=0$. With three distinct moderator values, equality of two squared affine paths implies equality up to a global sign; strict positive intercepts remove that global ambiguity. Thus the binary reflection need not extend to rich continuous support. Unrestricted global sign symmetry does extend. These are distinct interpretations of Appendix C.

Log-linear variance $V(m)=\exp(\beta_0+\beta_1m)$ has derivative $\beta_1V(m)$, establishing monotonicity for a fixed slope. It does not establish likelihood concavity. The Gaussian model with common component functions has within-twin variance $T=A+C+E$ and cross-twin covariance $A+C$ for MZ and $A/2+C$ for DZ. Source square-root scaling in Eq. 5.14 is essential to this calculation. The verified moments and symmetry arguments are in \file{handoff/chapter5-analytical-evidence.json}.

A separate sufficient identification condition uses common exact log-linear component functions and affine mean functions over moderator laws that, within each zygosity, are not concentrated on three or fewer values. Four distinct atoms suffice, and the two zygosity supports may be disjoint. It does not justify arbitrary smooth extrapolation across disjoint supports, binary supports, noisy separate-twin moderators or unknown calibration. Failure to satisfy a sufficient condition is not proof of nonidentification. The explicit support assumptions and result are retained in \file{provenance/continuous-moderator-identification-check.json}.

\subsection{Population identification does not prove one finite likelihood maximum}
The source links unique identification to one likelihood maximum (Chapter 5, p. 72). Under a free common mean, an all-zero response sample has likelihood increasing toward its supremum as that mean decreases, without attaining a finite maximum. This refutes universal finite-maximum existence, a stronger interpretation than at-most-one maximum when one exists. It is not a multiple-maximum example. Neither a proof nor a counterexample for the general at-most-one finite local or global maximum claim is supplied.

The Chapter 2 genetic moderation model also requires a distinct observation argument. Nonzero interaction gives non-Gaussian marginal traits. A conditional fixed-parameter latent-moment inversion can identify latent components under known genetic correlations, common component laws and independent innovations. It does not show that finitely many calibrated Rasch response patterns identify those latent moments. The Gaussian item argument cannot supply that missing bridge. Fixed-parameter moment existence also does not imply finite prior-predictive moments under inverse-gamma$(1,1)$ variance priors. See \file{provenance/non-gaussian-latent-moment-check.json}.

\section{Response generation and Bayesian feasibility}\label{sec:bayes}
The reconstructed generators and one completed posterior diagnostic provide limited evidence about feasibility, not the dissertation's repeated-dataset recovery or power claims. Chapter 2's 100-dataset and Chapter 5's 250-dataset posterior aggregates remain incomplete. Generation counts, retained draws and completed source-target experiments are different quantities.

\subsection{Generation matches some descriptors and leaves the source estimand uncertain}
The response experiment comprises 14 Chapter 2 cells with 100 datasets each and five Chapter 5 cells with 250 datasets each. The seed rule is $910000+1000c+r$, with condition $c$ and replicate $r$ fixed before generation. The initial pilot duplicates each cell's first seed and is not 19 extra observations. All 19 conditions, including unfavourable descriptor comparisons, are reported in \file{handoff/generator-summary-scientific-evidence.json}.

For Chapter 2 floor and ceiling designs, source reliability is approximately 0.90, whereas our raw-alpha means are 0.90802950 and 0.90753316 (MCSEs 0.00059652 and 0.00050631). All 12 power-design means round to the reported 0.92. Chapter 5's four 60-item cells round to 0.90, while the 20-item mean 0.73819631 rounds to 0.74 rather than the quoted 0.75. Source aggregation, calibration and participant subset are not determined sufficiently to turn these descriptors into exact-source verdicts.

For item covariance $V$, raw alpha is $J[1-\operatorname{tr}(V)/\sum V]/(J-1)$; standardized alpha is $J\bar r/[1+(J-1)\bar r]$. Reusing the same 2,650 generated datasets gives floor and ceiling standardized means 0.90697262 and 0.90645093. For 20 items, standardization lowers 0.73819631 to 0.73109750, a paired change of -0.00709880 with MCSE 0.00010938. This does not explain the descriptor gap. The paired calculation adds zero independent datasets and zero posterior fits; see \file{handoff/generator-alpha-sensitivity-scientific-evidence.json}.

The source floor skewness endpoints 0.630 and 0.632 and ceiling endpoints -0.435 and -0.434 refer to different estimators on an unknown illustrative sample. They are not across-replicate extrema. Our replicate skewness means and observed extrema cannot be substituted for those endpoints. Reconstructed positive floor and negative ceiling skew support qualitative directions, not the exact figure or an undefined qualification such as slight.

\subsection{One completed centered diagnostic supports only its fixed case}
The completed centered item-Bernoulli model uses one $N=500$, $J=60$ floor dataset, seed 301, unchanged priors and known calibration. Four chains each have 1,000 adaptation, 7,000 burn-in and 28,000 retained iterations. All 14 monitored quantities pass the declared joint operational criteria: rank-normalized split/folded $\widehat R<1.01$, bulk and tail effective sample sizes at least 400, and mean MCSE/posterior SD at most 0.05. These diagnostics follow the rank-based approach of \citet{vehtari2021}; they are finite diagnostics, not a proof of convergence.

The worst $\widehat R$ is 1.001673, minimum bulk effective sample size 685.762, minimum tail effective sample size 1407.532, and maximum relative mean MCSE 0.038211. Interaction mean is -0.12338265, with pooled 95\% highest-posterior-density (HPD) interval [-0.68469410, 0.43069918]. All 12 whole/half-chain interaction HPDs contain zero. This is non-exclusion on one dataset, not equality to zero, unbiasedness, a calibrated detection rate or replication of the source's aggregate. Equal-tailed quantile MCSE is not HPD endpoint MCSE.

Shorter original and centered diagnostics failed their joint criteria. The first 12,000 retained draws of each completed longer chain repeat the shorter centered run exactly; those prefixes must not be pooled or counted as independent evidence. The independently checked result still shares data, model, backend and some diagnostic-library computations. Variance components retain serial correlation and rank differences. The full summaries and limitations are in \file{handoff/jags-centered-extended-recovery-scientific-evidence.json}.

\subsection{An exact score representation did not complete the fixed workload}
For known Rasch difficulties, the likelihood of a response vector depends on the latent trait through its canonical score, up to a known parameter-independent factor. The exact calibrated score likelihood therefore preserves the physical latent model and priors. It is not the dissertation's Gaussian analysis of sum scores. Exact target preservation does not promise computational acceleration; see \file{provenance/rasch-sufficiency-derivation.json}.

The bounded score-model test requested the same four-chain, 28,000-retained-draw workload on the same fixed dataset, using JAGS 4.3.2. All four chains adapted, but each retained only 16,000 draws before the predetermined summed-process budget ended: 64,000 saved draws and zero completed fixed-length chains. Chain/cleanup time was 6419.751313 seconds; all-phase summed time was 6420.894927 seconds and elapsed wall time 1606.104006 seconds. These are distinct resource measures, not CPU seconds.

The completed item-Bernoulli reference used 4927.558112 summed chain-process seconds for four chains of 28,000 retained draws. The score implementation consumed more chain/cleanup time while retaining fewer draws. This is neither equal-work benchmarking nor proof that score methods cannot accelerate other implementations: starts, random streams and runtime epochs differ. Computational acceleration and posterior agreement are not established.

The full fixed-draw joint diagnostic and five-global MCSE comparisons are ineligible and were not run. Adaptation and finite checkpoints do not substitute for convergence evidence. Incomplete chains are not pooled with historical chains. This same-backend feasibility test contributes no new dataset, no completed source-count aggregate and no change to the original scientific verdicts. Exact outcome evidence is \file{handoff/score-a0-scientific-evidence.json} and \file{provenance/score-a0-terminal-outcome.json}.

\section{Empirical findings and software semantics}\label{sec:empirical}
Independent empirical replication is blocked by missing source responses, linkage and calibration inputs. The available source reports remain scientifically important, but source arithmetic cannot replace their refitting. In Chapter 3 mathematics, the published genetic-by-individual-environment slope is 2.0837 and the genetic-by-shared-environment slope is 2.6159; the source reports the former HPD excluding zero and the latter including zero (Table 3.2, p. 42). Our independent slopes are not established.

Chapter 4 reports a two-dimensional interpretation of 28 items and a nine-item social-liberalism subscale. Its IRT AxE slope is -2.81 and AxC slope 0.54 (Table 4.3, p. 63), both with reported HPDs excluding zero. The directions change in the sum-score analysis. Comparing absolute values across differently identified scales would conceal the substantive sign reversal; subtracting those unstandardized slopes would not create a justified scalar effect. Neither dimensional interpretation, scale selection nor interaction reversal was independently refitted. The published article and supplement are distinct source materials \citep{schwabeConservatism}; accessible plots and programs do not supply Virginia30K responses.

Chapter 5 reports shared-environment variance decreasing in high-SES families, with log-variance slope -3.0777 and an HPD excluding zero (Table 5.4, p. 82). Chapter 6 reports no detected coefficient among 13 measured covariates under its 99.6\% HPD criterion (Table 6.3, p. 100). These application results require the original linked NTR/Cito data; the simulated Gaussian missingness experiment cannot reproduce them. Nondetection does not imply all 13 population effects are zero.

Some software propositions can be evaluated without the empirical data. For positive-slope logistic items, conditional information is $a_j^2p_j(1-p_j)$; Eq. 3.4 omits the squared discrimination factor present in Eq. 3.3. Printed Chapter 4 probability equations and the normalized cumulative-exponential Appendix B implementation differ. The implemented probabilities sum to one, but that fact is not faithful reproduction of the literal printed formula. These analytical findings are detailed in \file{handoff/narrow-analytical-evidence.json}.

Appendix G has probabilistic observation links. The binary link is $P(X=1\mid L)=0.001+0.998\,1\{L\geq0\}$, not a deterministic threshold. For a nondegenerate Gaussian liability it gives $0.001+0.998\Phi(\mu/\sigma)$ and scales binary cross-covariance by $0.998^2$ under conditional independence. The continuous school-size observation has precision 100, adding variance 0.01 on its standardized scale. The regression uses observed or imputed covariates rather than simply their latent expectations. Thus the mixed-data application is not identical to the continuous joint-Gaussian simulation. See \file{handoff/chapter6-analytical-evidence.json}.

Chapter 7's software contribution is assessed separately from its empirical illustrations. BayesTwin version 1.0 is dated 2017; the available pre-defense revision is from 15 October 2015. Its example posterior data are simulated, not the dissertation's original empirical data \citep{bayestwin}. Version-aware software replay and probability-kernel checks establish only those specific implementation properties.

\section{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}
Our reconstruction needs explicit choices where the source does not uniquely specify an executable procedure. The following assumptions are supplied by the replication; source-stated Gaussian covariance structure and genetic correlations are model premises rather than newly inferred facts.
\subsection{Low-missingness design}
We use 2\% as the primary low-missingness level and 3\% as a separate sensitivity level because both appear in the source. Conclusions do not silently merge these designs.
\subsection{Response-generator calibration and reliability summaries}
We declare a fixed reconstructed calibration and reproducible seed rule. Raw and standardized reliability, participant subsets and across-dataset aggregation are alternative descriptors, not recovered historical choices. For Chapter 5, Bernoulli moderator probability 0.5 is consistent with approximately 11\% explained variance but is not explicitly supplied by the source.
\subsection{Sufficient conditions for identification}
Known finite positive logistic calibrations, distinct conditionally independent observations, accessible joint moments and appropriate shared moderator/zygosity support are added sufficient mathematical hypotheses. They have not been empirically verified for the original applications. The common scale cannot be replaced by arbitrary nonlinear transformations.
\subsection{Comparison margins and uncertainty}
Source-coordinate bounds use 0.25 PDF point transformed through the source axes and summed across a linear contrast. Those bounds predate the guarded production experiment, although the full plan was not wholly preregistered and earlier outcomes were known. They bound extraction, not scientific equivalence or source Monte Carlo error. Printed scalar slopes instead use half their last printed unit. No pooled margin is invented for multicomponent propositions or qualitative adequacy.

\section{Ambiguities that were resolved or retained}\label{sec:ambiguities}
The source contains distinctions that materially affect interpretation. An inconsistency is not automatically a replication failure; the following checks identify what can be resolved and what must stay uncertain.
\subsection{A 01: Chapter 2 p 21 Eq 2.16 worked example}
For Delta 1.5 prose multiplies .5$\times$1.5=.75 though environmental baseline was .2; slope/ratio formula and baseline example conflict. Use the variance-ratio formula and retain the conflicting baseline example; the arithmetic discrepancy does not establish a posterior recovery result.
\subsection{A 02: Chapter 3 p 37 Eq 3.4}
OPLM information formula omits discrimination squared although Eq 3.3 has item discrimination; plot implementation unavailable from dissertation. The logistic derivative requires squared discrimination in information. The literal printed equation and model-implied information are distinct; the unavailable plot implementation is not inferred.
\subsection{A 03: Chapter 3 p 41 versus p 42}
Text defines effect at $A=\sigma_A^2$, but reported ratios 1.63/1.85 agree with one standard deviation. The reported ratios agree with one standard deviation rather than one variance unit; these are arithmetic checks on source parameters, not empirical re-estimation.
\subsection{A 04: Chapter 4 p 56 Eqs 4.6--4.10 versus Appendix B}
Printed GPCM normalization/threshold equations differ from cumulative-exponential code. The Appendix B cumulative-exponential implementation normalizes; the literal displayed expression does not generally define the same probability kernel.
\subsection{A 05: Chapter 4 p 64}
IRT $h^2$ mean .54 is outside printed 95\% HPD [.34,.51]. The reported mean and HPD are retained without inferring a corrected posterior summary. A posterior mean can lie outside a highest-posterior-density interval; this alone does not establish an inconsistency.
\subsection{A 06: Chapter 5 p 78 versus Table 5.1}
Design prose says 80000 pairs but table and results say 8000. Preserve both; no silent replacement. The reconstruction uses the table/results value of 8,000 pairs and flags the 80,000 prose value. Neither choice recovers missing historical simulation records.
\subsection{A 07: Chapter 5 p 74 versus p 84}
