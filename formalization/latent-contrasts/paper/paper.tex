\begin{abstract}
Do the latent-mean contrasts in Jeffries (2024) sit on the printed scale
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), \(0\), and do the rejection rates in Tables~4.1 and~4.3
follow from that model? Jeffries defines three codes for four groups, states
that imbalance makes the set non-orthogonal, and reports multigroup and MIMIC
averages near those three numbers when the generating means are
\(1, 0.6, 0.8, 0.4\). The fitting was done in lavaan~0.6.17. Population
contrasts were obtained by fitting each candidate identification to the
population moments. A 400-replicate Monte Carlo then used the analysis string
from the public OSF project. On a population in which every loading is \(0.70\),
a marker loading of \(1\) produces \(-0.28\), \(-0.14\), and \(0\). On the
population lavaan builds from the generation string, the first loading is \(1\)
and the other three are \(0.70\), and the same marker model produces
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), and \(0\). The second population matches the printed
averages. At \(N=240\), a \(400\)-replicate screen put six of \(30\) rejection
rates outside an uncorrected two-standard-error band, and one of those six
outside Bonferroni and Holm: \(2.26\) against the printed \(5.18\). The same
ten cells at \(10{,}000\) replicates put that rate at \(5.01\). Three rates
remain outside the uncorrected band. None remain outside Bonferroni or Holm.
A binomial count of those three misses does not exceed the \(5\%\) expectation.
Redrawing the three cells with simsem~0.5.16 and the \texttt{exportData} seed
puts two of them inside that band and leaves one outside: \(6.46\) against
\(7.41\). The dissertation's percents are single Wald tests at \(\alpha=0.05\). The
correction applies only to this agreement check. The unbalanced power rates
come out \(49.35\), \(21.28\), and \(8.51\) against \(49.23\), \(21.08\), and
\(7.77\). Multigroup convergence in the smallest groups is \(89.82\%\) against
\(89.29\%\).
\end{abstract}

\section{What this replication did}
\label{sec:did}

This section answers what was fit, and in which program.
lavaan~0.6.17 is the estimator named in the dissertation, and it is the
program that produced every fitted number below. simsem~0.5.17, the installed
successor of the named simsem~0.5.16, was used to parse the generation string.
OpenMx and MplusAutomation are not installed. Mplus is not installed. The file
\path{simulation/mgcfa_osf.inp} mirrors the multigroup string and was not
executed. \texttt{exportData(program = "Mplus")} in the generation scripts
writes data files. The estimation scripts call \texttt{lavaan::sem}.

Lean is used only for the code identities already proved in
\path{LatentContrasts/Orthogonality.lean}: the products of the Table~2.2
codes, the population values of equations~(3.3)--(3.5), and the treatment
coding of those codes. The gap between \(-0.28\) and \(-0.40\) is a question
about which population and which identification lavaan is fitting. That
question is answered by the moment fits, not by a new proof.

The moment fits are \path{simulation/candidate_readings.R}. The short Monte
Carlo is \path{simulation/validate_gaps.R}, \(400\) replicates, seed \(13131\)
under R's L'Ecuyer generator, with one drawn seed per cell. The
\(10{,}000\)-replicate rerun is \path{simulation/validate_10k.R}. Each of its
ten cells has a fixed seed, set once, and the replicates are drawn in order on
one core. The agreement classification, including the Bonferroni and Holm
checks, is \path{simulation/score_gaps.R}. Package versions are in
\path{simulation/out/session_info.txt}: R~4.3.3, lavaan~0.6.17, simsem~0.5.17.
The miss-cell redraw is \path{simulation/env_miss.R}, simsem~0.5.16, seed
\(13131\). The dissertation names R~4.2.2.

Jeffries studies one factor with four indicators and four groups. The
true-difference means are \(\alpha=(1,0.6,0.8,0.4)\). The null means are all
\(1\). Total sample size is \(240\), \(480\), or \(960\). The group shares are
\(25/25/25/25\), \(35/35/15/15\), and \(45/45/5/5\).

\section{Where the gaps come from}
\label{sec:gaps}

The question is which population and which identification produce
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), and \(0\), and whether that same fit reproduces the
printed rejection rates.

The generation string in the public OSF script, and the repaired form of the
appendix string, is
\begin{lstlisting}
eta =~ NA*i1 + 0.7*i1 + 0.7*i2 + 0.7*i3 + 0.7*i4
eta ~~ .5*eta
i1 ~ 1*1
eta ~ c(1, .6, .8, .4)*1
\end{lstlisting}
with residual variances \(0.51\). lavaan~0.6.17 keeps a free loading on
\texttt{i1} and drops the second modifier \(0.7\). \texttt{simulateData} fills
the free loading with \(1\). A draw of \(20{,}000\) at factor mean \(1\) had
mean \(1.996\) on \texttt{i1} and \(1.693\) on \texttt{i2}. The targets are
\(2\) and \(1.7\). The variances were \(1.021\) and \(0.749\). The targets are
\(1.01\) and \(0.755\). simsem~0.5.17 rejects the page's token
\texttt{i1 \textasciitilde\ 1*} with the same parse error. The OSF generation
script already writes \texttt{1*1}. The dissertation page writes \texttt{1*}
for that one intercept. Figure~\ref{fig:intercept} is the page.

The analysis string in \path{osf/analysis/mgcfa_model.R} is a marker loading of
\(1\), free factor variances, four free means, and one intercept shared by all
four indicators and all four groups. It does not constrain the loadings equal
across groups, and it does not fix them at \(0.7\). The summary script sets the
printed standard-error column to the standard deviation of the estimates divided
by \(\sqrt{10{,}000}\).

\subsection{Monte Carlo noise}

The question is whether the remaining percent gaps are the size of a
\(400\)-replicate simulation. A gap is outside noise when it exceeds two
combined standard errors. For a percent, the combined standard error is
\[
100\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{10{,}000}+\frac{\tilde p(1-\tilde p)}{n}},
\]
with \(\hat p\) the printed rate and \(n\) our number of converged replicates.
For an average, the printed standard error is already the standard error of the
mean of \(10{,}000\) replicates, and ours is the standard deviation divided by
\(\sqrt{n}\).

All \(30\) averages in the \(N=240\) cells we ran, multigroup and free-intercept
MIMIC, true and null, are inside that band. The sampling standard deviations
match the printed standard errors times \(100\). In the multigroup
\(45/45/5/5\) true-difference cell the printed standard errors are all
\(0.0021\), so the implied standard deviations are \(0.210\). Ours were
\(0.210\), \(0.221\), and \(0.206\).

\subsection{Multiplicity}

The percent columns in Tables~4.1--4.4 are one Wald test on one dataset at
\(\alpha=0.05\). That is the design of those columns. This replication leaves
those tests at \(\alpha=0.05\). Multiplicity is a property of the agreement
check only: thirty differences between a printed rate and our rate.

The uncorrected check is a two-sided \(5\%\) test, \(|z|>1.96\). The
two-combined-standard-error band is that rule with \(2\) in place of \(1.96\).
On these thirty rates the two cutoffs select the same six rows.

Bonferroni divides \(0.05\) by \(30\). The one-sided normal quantile of the
remaining tail is \(2.94\). The two-sided quantile is \(3.14\). Holm sorts the
thirty two-sided \(p\)-values and compares the \(i\)th with
\(0.05/(31-i)\), then stops at the first \(p\)-value that fails its cutoff.
The smallest \(p\)-value is \(0.00016\), below \(0.05/30=0.00167\), so Holm
rejects that row. The next \(p\)-value is \(0.0076\), from the multigroup
\(35/15\) null contrast~2. Holm's second cutoff is \(0.05/29=0.0017\). The
step-down stops. Five of the six uncorrected misses are inside every corrected
rule above.

A binomial test asks whether six misses in thirty trials exceeds the count
expected at \(p=0.05\). The upper tail is \(0.0033\). Under independence the
expected count is \(1.5\). The thirty indicators are dependent. Each cell
contributes three contrasts computed on the same replicates, so a miss on one
contrast moves with the other two. Dependence of that kind widens the
distribution of the miss count relative to a \(\mathrm{Bin}(30,0.05)\) draw.
The binomial probability is the independent-trials figure. The ten cells use
separate seeds, so the dependence sits inside cells. At \(400\) replicates the
Table~4.3 contrast~2 rate, \(12.85\) against \(8.59\), is the uncorrected miss
in that cell, and it is inside the corrected band.

The multigroup null model at \(N=240\) and shares \(45/45/5/5\) converged in
\(389\) of \(400\) replicates, \(97.25\%\). Table~4.5 prints \(9{,}675\) and
\(95.75\%\) on that row. The count divided by \(10{,}000\) is \(96.75\).

\section{What we proved}

The codes, in group order \((1,2,3,4)\), are
\[
c_1=(-1,1,-1,1),\qquad
c_2=(-1,-1,1,1),\qquad
c_3=(1,-1,-1,1).
\]
The weighted product is \(\sum_{g=1}^{4} n_g c_{jg} c_{kg}\). For equal sizes
the three products are zero (\lean{}{107}). For sizes \((a,a,b,b)\) the
products of \(c_1\) with \(c_2\) and of \(c_2\) with \(c_3\) stay zero, and the
product of \(c_1\) with \(c_3\) equals \(2(b-a)\) (\lean{}{68}). The column sums
are zero (\lean{}{99}).

A defined contrast is the average of the means coded \(+1\), minus the average
of the means coded \(-1\). On \(\kappa=(1,3/5,4/5,2/5)\) the three contrasts
are \(-2/5\), \(-1/5\), and \(0\) (\lean{}{123}). The third is zero while
\(\kappa_1\neq\kappa_2\) (\lean{}{127}). Multiplying every mean by \(7/10\)
multiplies the contrasts by \(7/10\) (\lean{}{131}). That factor is the
every-loading-\(0.70\) marker reading. It is not the population lavaan builds
from \texttt{NA*i1}. The treatment-contrast form with coefficients \(-2/5\),
\(-1/5\), and \(0\) reproduces \(\kappa\) (\lean{}{159}).

The proofs are rational arithmetic. The \texttt{ring} proofs depend on
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The
\texttt{native\_decide} proofs also depend on \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool}.
There is no \texttt{sorry}.
