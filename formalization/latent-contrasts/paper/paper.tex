\title{Replication of the orthogonal latent-mean contrasts\\
in Jeffries (2024)}
\author{}
\date{28 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Do the latent-mean contrasts in Jeffries (2024) sit on the printed scale
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), \(0\), and do the rejection rates and the MIMIC
convergence counts follow from that model? Jeffries defines three codes
for four groups and reports multigroup and MIMIC averages near those
three numbers when the generating means are \(1\), \(0.6\), \(0.8\), and
\(0.4\), from \(10{,}000\) converged replicates in each cell.
Fitting the marker model to the population that lavaan~\cite{rosseel2012}
builds from the generation string returns exactly \(-0.40\), \(-0.20\),
and \(0\).
The unbalanced power rates at \(N=240\) come out \(49.35\), \(21.28\),
and \(8.51\) against the printed \(49.23\), \(21.08\), and \(7.77\).
Of thirty rejection-rate comparisons, one stays outside an uncorrected
two-standard-error band and none miss Bonferroni or Holm.
That rate is the MIMIC model, shares \(45/45/5/5\), null means,
contrast~2: \(6.51\) on the simsem~0.5.16~\cite{simsem} stream and
\(6.47\) on the hand-written stream, against the printed \(7.41\).
The combined standard error is \(0.36\) percentage points, and the
two-standard-error band around \(7.41\) runs from \(6.69\) to \(8.13\).
The MIMIC convergence gap closes with lavaan's BFGS optimizer.
Seven of the eight cells reach the printed \(10{,}000\) of \(10{,}000\),
and the simsem null cell with those shares reaches \(9{,}999\).
The named optimizer, \texttt{nlminb}, converges \(9{,}951\) to
\(9{,}988\) of the same replicates, so the printed count is consistent
with a different optimizer or setting.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question, and the headline}
\label{sec:headline}

This section answers whether the printed contrasts, the printed
rejection rates, and the MIMIC convergence counts come from the model
in the dissertation.
The contrasts do. The rates do, except one. The MIMIC convergence
counts close when lavaan uses BFGS.

Jeffries (2024), \url{https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/dissunl/216/},
studies one factor, four indicators, and four groups. The
true-difference means are \(1\), \(0.6\), \(0.8\), and \(0.4\). The null
means are all \(1\). Sample sizes are \(240\), \(480\), and \(960\), with
group shares \(25/25/25/25\), \(35/35/15/15\), and \(45/45/5/5\).
The fitting program named in the text is lavaan~0.6.17. The public
analysis scripts are at \url{https://osf.io/q9yu8/}.

The population fit of the marker model on the parsed generation string
is \(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), \(0\). The same marker model on a population
in which every loading is \(0.70\) is \(-0.28\), \(-0.14\), \(0\).
The printed tables match the first of those. At \(N=240\) and
\(10{,}000\) replicates, the unbalanced power rates match the page.
One rejection rate remains outside an uncorrected two-standard-error
band: the MIMIC model, shares \(45/45/5/5\), null means, contrast~2,
\(6.51\) on the simsem stream and \(6.47\) on the hand-written stream,
against the printed \(7.41\). A Bonferroni or Holm correction of the
thirty rate comparisons leaves no miss.
The MIMIC convergence gap closes under BFGS. Seven of the eight cells
reach the printed \(10{,}000\) of \(10{,}000\), and the simsem null cell
with shares \(45/45/5/5\) reaches \(9{,}999\). The optimizer the
dissertation names is \texttt{nlminb}. On these replicates nlminb
converges \(9{,}951\) to \(9{,}988\). The printed count of \(10{,}000\)
is what a different optimizer produces, and it is consistent with an
optimizer or a setting other than that named run.

The original work is the design, the codes, and the printed tables.
The calculations below are ours: the moment fits, the Monte Carlo, and
the code identities. The dissertation's percent columns are one Wald
test per dataset at \(\alpha=0.05\). That is the design of those
columns. Multiplicity is applied only to our check that a printed rate
and our rate agree.

\section{What gave us trouble, and how it was resolved}
\label{sec:trouble}

This section answers why an earlier replay sat on \(-0.28\) and why a
short Monte Carlo looked as if many rates had missed.

\subsection{The scale \(-0.28\) against \(-0.40\)}

The generation line is \texttt{eta =\textasciitilde\ NA*i1 + 0.7*i1 + 0.7*i2 + 0.7*i3 + 0.7*i4}.
In lavaan~0.6.17 that line keeps a free loading on the first indicator
and drops the duplicate \(0.7\). \texttt{simulateData} fills the free
loading with \(1\). The other three loadings stay at \(0.70\).
The factor variance in the syntax is \(0.50\) and the residual variances
are \(0.51\). A large sample from that string has mean about \(2.00\)
on the first indicator when the factor mean is \(1\), and means about
\(1.70\) on the other three, which is \(1+0.70\times 1\).

The marker model fixes the first loading at \(1\). On this population
the factor stays on the generating scale, and the three contrasts are
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), and \(0\). If every loading is generated at
\(0.70\) and the first is then fixed at \(1\), the same contrasts are
rescaled by \(0.70\), which is \(-0.28\), \(-0.14\), and \(0\).
The printed tables match the parsed string. The moment fits are
\srcat{simulation/candidate_readings.R}{103}, and the output is
\src{simulation/out/candidate_readings.csv}.

\subsection{The simsem~0.5.16 redraw, and the convergence rule}

The dissertation names simsem~0.5.16. For a lavaan script,
\texttt{sim()} in that version draws the rows with
\texttt{lavaan::simulateData}, after setting the generator to
L'Ecuyer-CMRG and the seed to the \texttt{exportData} seed.

\section{What is still outstanding}
\label{sec:open}

This section answers which comparisons remain after the
\(10{,}000\)-replicate runs and the BFGS refit.
One rejection rate does not match.
Three smaller differences are listed after it, and they are not
replication failures.
The remaining comparisons match, and Bonferroni and Holm leave zero
misses among the thirty rates.

The comparison that does not match is the MIMIC rejection rate for
contrast~2, null means, group shares \(45/45/5/5\).
The page prints \(7.41\).
The simsem stream gives \(6.51\) from \(9{,}999\) converged replicates.
The hand-written stream gives \(6.47\) from \(10{,}000\).

streams. The two-standard-error band around \(7.41\) runs from \(6.69\)

The standardized gaps are \(|z|=2.50\) (simsem) and \(|z|=2.62\) (hand).
The two-sided \(p\)-values are \(0.012\) and \(0.0088\).

On the simsem stream that count includes the two rates the

The nonnegative-variance subset of the same cell is \(0.0014\),

An earlier stream of the cell was \(0.0023\), \(|z|=2.40\).
The simsem stream is the one on the boundary. It is not a replication

Table~4.5, multigroup null, \(N=240\), condition~3, prints \(9{,}675\)

The count and our simulation agree.

That pair is not a replication failure of the convergence rate.

That page-versus-file difference is not a replication failure.

\section{What we tried on the outstanding gaps}
\label{sec:tried}

This section answers what was tried on the gaps, and what prompted each
tool.
R~\cite{rcore} and lavaan~\cite{rosseel2012} are the fitting tools in the dissertation and in the OSF
scripts. A structural-equation fit is a numerical optimization of a
covariance model, which is work for R. The Lean proofs check the code
identities and do not fit those matrices.
simsem~0.5.16~\cite{simsem} is the version the dissertation names, so the cells that
had missed were redrawn in that version.
OpenMx~\cite{neale2016} was brought in as an independent structural-equation estimator,
to test whether the lavaan \texttt{nlminb} shortfall was the optimizer
or the data.
BFGS was the alternate lavaan optimizer tried after that diagnosis.
The convergence counts close under BFGS.
The contrast-2 rejection rate does not.
The public record still has no \texttt{.dat} files and no
\texttt{sessionInfo} from the run that wrote the tables.

The dissertation names R~4.2.2, simsem~0.5.16, and lavaan~0.6.17, and
\texttt{nlminb} with a maximum of \(10{,}000\) iterations. The operating
system and \texttt{RNGkind} are absent from that description. The
default since R~3.6.0, and the default here, is Mersenne-Twister,
Inversion, and Rejection. \texttt{sim()} replaces that with
L'Ecuyer-CMRG and then uses the \texttt{exportData} seed.
simsem~0.5-16 was current from 2021-03-28 until 0.5-17 on 2025-04-02.
lavaan~0.6-17 was published on 2023-12-20. The ten-cell Monte Carlo
used R~4.3.3~\cite{rcore}. R~4.2.2 was not installed. The L'Ecuyer generator is the
one \texttt{sim()} sets in both of those R versions.

\texttt{exportData(program = "Mplus")} writes data files. The
estimation scripts call \texttt{lavaan::sem}. The page quotes a lavaan
warning and names \texttt{nlminb}. No Mplus output is on the OSF node.
Had Mplus been the estimator, its default would fix the first loading
at \(1\), and a default summary prints a convergence criterion of
\(0.00005\) with at most \(1{,}000\) iterations. A negative residual
variance is reported and the solution is still returned. The tables
come from the lavaan scripts above. Mplus is not installed.
\src{simulation/mgcfa_osf.inp} mirrors the multigroup string and was
not executed.

The OSF node \texttt{q9yu8} was created on 2025-04-18. The files now on
it were uploaded on 2025-09-20. They are the generation scripts, the
lavaan analysis scripts, and the summary CSVs. There is no
\texttt{sessionInfo}, no log, no \texttt{.Rhistory}, and no
\texttt{.dat} replicate. The generation scripts call
\texttt{set.seed(1313)} and pass seed \(13131\) to \texttt{exportData}.
That call is commented out. One uncommented call, in the \(N=480\)
condition-3 null script, names group sizes \((168,168,72,72)\), the
\(35/15\) split. The Monte Carlo here uses the printed shares.

GitHub, GitLab, and Zenodo searches, and a clone of the twenty public
repositories under \href{https://github.com/jjeffries13}{\texttt{jjeffries13}}, are logged in
\src{simulation/out/code_search.md}. Queries for the dissertation
title, \srcat{osf/analysis/mgcfa_model.R}{6}, and \texttt{set.seed(1313)} on that
account returned no dissertation script.
\aparepo\ reuses the loading idiom and seed

\subsection{OpenMx on the unconverged MIMIC replicates}

The nlminb counts were short of the printed \(10{,}000\).
The question is whether those replicates are bad datasets or an
optimizer setting.
OpenMx is the independent estimator used for that question.
BFGS is the lavaan optimizer tried because OpenMx left the same
datasets unconverged.
The script is \srcat{simulation/openmx_check.R}{172}.
It replays the hand stream from \srcat{simulation/validate_10k.R}{126}
and the simsem stream from \srcat{simulation/env_miss.R}{126}, keeps
every replicate lavaan did not converge, and keeps 25 converged
replicates in each of the eight cells as a control.
OpenMx~2.22.11~\cite{neale2016} then fits the same free-intercept
marker model. The CRAN build's default optimizer is SLSQP.
NPSOL is not compiled into it. CSOLNP was run as a second optimizer.

The indicator covariance of a failed replicate has a condition number
between about \(3.6\) and \(5.4\), and its smallest eigenvalue is
between about \(0.34\) and \(0.45\). The nlminb attempt did not return
a negative variance, and it did not raise the gradient warning.
Those replicates do not look like a singular sample or a Heywood case
in the data.
