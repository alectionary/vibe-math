\begin{abstract}
Do the latent-mean contrasts in Jeffries (2024) sit on the printed scale
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), \(0\), and do the rejection rates in Tables~4.1
and~4.3 follow from that model? Jeffries defines three codes for four
groups and reports multigroup and MIMIC averages near those three numbers
when the generating means are \(1\), \(0.6\), \(0.8\), and \(0.4\).
Fitting the marker model to the population that lavaan builds from the
generation string returns exactly \(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), and \(0\).
The unbalanced power rates at \(N=240\) come out \(49.35\), \(21.28\),
and \(8.51\) against the printed \(49.23\), \(21.08\), and \(7.77\).
Of thirty rejection-rate comparisons at \(10{,}000\) replicates, three
miss an uncorrected two-standard-error band and none miss Bonferroni or
Holm. A later draw with simsem~0.5.16 moves two of those three inside
the band and leaves one: \(6.46\) against \(7.41\).
\end{abstract}

\section{The question, and the headline}
\label{sec:headline}

This section answers whether the printed contrasts and the printed
rejection rates come from the model in the dissertation.
The contrasts do. Most of the rates do. One rate, one average, and the
MIMIC convergence counts are still open.

Jeffries (2024), \url{https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/dissunl/216/},
studies one factor, four indicators, and four groups. The
true-difference means are \(1\), \(0.6\), \(0.8\), and \(0.4\). The null
means are all \(1\). Sample sizes are \(240\), \(480\), and \(960\), with
group shares \(25/25/25/25\), \(35/35/15/15\), and \(45/45/5/5\).
The fitting program named in the text is lavaan~0.6.17. The public
analysis scripts are at \url{https://osf.io/q9yu8/}.

The population fit of the marker model on the parsed generation string
is \(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), \(0\). The same marker model on a population
in which every loading is \(0.70\) is \(-0.28\), \(-0.14\), \(0\).
The printed tables match the first of those. At \(N=240\) and
\(10{,}000\) replicates, the unbalanced power rates match the page,
and a Bonferroni or Holm correction of the thirty rate comparisons
leaves no miss. The rate that remains outside an uncorrected band is
the MIMIC model, shares \(45/45/5/5\), null means, contrast~2.

The original work is the design, the codes, and the printed tables.
The calculations below are ours: the moment fits, the Monte Carlo, and
the code identities. The dissertation's percent columns are one Wald
test per dataset at \(\alpha=0.05\). That is the design of those
columns. Multiplicity is applied only to our check that a printed rate
and our rate agree.

largest \(|z|\) among those three rates is \(1.85\). The averages in

normal test per rate. The uncorrected cutoff is \(|z|>1.96\), and the
two-standard-error band used in the first screen is the same rule with
\(2\) in place of \(1.96\). Bonferroni on thirty tests uses
\(z=\Phi^{-1}(1-0.05/30)=2.94\), and the two-sided threshold is
\(z=\Phi^{-1}(1-0.05/60)=3.14\). Holm sorts the thirty two-sided
\(p\)-values and stops at the first failure. At \(10{,}000\) replicates
the smallest of those \(p\)-values is \(0.0083\), above
\(0.05/30=0.00167\), so Holm stops immediately.

A binomial test of three misses in thirty trials at \(p=0.05\) has
upper tail \(0.19\). That figure treats the thirty indicators as
independent. The three contrasts in a cell share replicates, so the
count is dependent inside each cell and the binomial probability is the
independent-trials calculation. The expected count under independence
is \(1.5\). Three misses is an ordinary count for that band.

\section{What gave us trouble, and how it was resolved}
\label{sec:trouble}

This section answers why an earlier replay sat on \(-0.28\) and why a
short Monte Carlo looked as if many rates had missed.

\subsection{The scale \(-0.28\) against \(-0.40\)}

The generation line is \texttt{eta =\textasciitilde\ NA*i1 + 0.7*i1 + 0.7*i2 + 0.7*i3 + 0.7*i4}.
In lavaan~0.6.17 that line keeps a free loading on the first indicator
and drops the duplicate \(0.7\). \texttt{simulateData} fills the free
loading with \(1\). The other three loadings stay at \(0.70\).
The factor variance in the syntax is \(0.50\) and the residual variances
are \(0.51\). A large sample from that string has mean about \(2.00\)
on the first indicator when the factor mean is \(1\), and means about
\(1.70\) on the other three, which is \(1+0.70\times 1\).

The marker model fixes the first loading at \(1\). On this population
the factor stays on the generating scale, and the three contrasts are
\(-0.40\), \(-0.20\), and \(0\). If every loading is generated at
\(0.70\) and the first is then fixed at \(1\), the same contrasts are
rescaled by \(0.70\), which is \(-0.28\), \(-0.14\), and \(0\).
The printed tables match the parsed string. The moment fits are
\path{simulation/candidate_readings.R}, and the output is
\path{simulation/out/candidate_readings.csv}.

\section{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

This section answers which choices were supplied here rather than read
off the page.

\subsection{Random-number stream}

The \(400\)-replicate screen used L'Ecuyer-CMRG, \texttt{set.seed(13131)},
and one drawn seed per cell. The \(10{,}000\)-replicate run sets a
separate fixed seed in each cell. The simsem redraw uses seed \(13131\)
inside \texttt{sim()}. These are not Jeffries's replicates.

\subsection{Package versions}

The ten-cell Monte Carlo used R~4.3.3 and simsem~0.5.17. The miss-cell
redraw installed simsem~0.5-16 and kept lavaan~0.6.17. R~4.2.2 was not
installed. Versions are in \path{simulation/out/session_info.txt} and
\path{simulation/out/env_session.txt}.

\subsection{Convergence}

A replicate counts as converged when \texttt{lavInspect(fit, "converged")}
is true and the three contrast standard errors are finite. That flag is
the one the OSF script reads from the summary header. In the
simsem~0.5-16 redraw the two rules selected the same replicates.

\subsection{The first intercept}

The Monte Carlo generates from \texttt{i1 \textasciitilde\ 1*1}, the
token in the OSF script and in the null model on the page.

\subsection{The MIMIC intercept}

The MIMIC Monte Carlo frees the latent intercept. The posted line, which
fixes it at \(0\), does not recover \(-0.40\).
