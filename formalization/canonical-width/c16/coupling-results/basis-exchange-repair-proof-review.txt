Independent review of the exact basis-mixture exchange repair

Scope and assumptions

Let E be a finite set of physical binary vectors of rank r. Let q_j be an
exact target in [0,1] with sum r, and let x be the incidence marginal of a
finite distribution on bases B of E. Every retained weight w_B is positive
and their sum is one. Repeated copies of the same basis are first merged.
All statements below are exact. An approximate target, negative weight or
incomplete collection of exchange edges does not support the no-path proof.

Local augmentation lemma

Put an edge i -> j whenever some positive-weight B contains i, excludes j,
and B-i+j is a basis. A simple path from a surplus coordinate s (x_s>q_s)
to a deficit coordinate t (x_t<q_t) has a chosen original basis B_e for
each edge. Let m_B count how many path edges use B. Set

  delta = min(x_s-q_s, q_t-x_t, min_B w_B/m_B).

Subtract m_B*delta from each original B and add delta to each exchanged
basis B_e-i_e+j_e, merging duplicates. Each new basis is independently
rank checked. The weights remain nonnegative and have total one. The
incidence changes telescope along the path, leaving only -delta at s and
+delta at t. The positive marginal error decreases by delta and the L1
error by 2*delta. The multiplicity divisor is required because an original
basis can witness more than one edge. It is conservative if new bases
coincide with old ones, which does not invalidate the update.

The supplementary literal boundary has binary vectors [1,3,2,3,1], original
bases B=(0,2) of weight 1/3 and C=(1,4) of weight 2/3, and path
0->1->2->3 witnessed by [B,C,B]. Endpoint discrepancies are both 1/4.
The valid delta is 1/6; ignoring multiplicity would use 1/4 and subtract
the original B weight to -1/6. None of the three new bases equals B, so
this remains a negative final weight even after incoming weights are
added. The earlier four-vector control separately checks the conservative
bound in the case where a newly generated basis equals an original one.

Complete-graph no-path lemma

Let S contain every surplus coordinate, D every deficit, and R be all
vertices reachable from S in the complete positive-basis exchange graph.
If no S-to-D path exists and x differs from q, then D lies in C=E\R.
No surplus lies in C, so q(C)>x(C).

Fix any positive B. For j in C\B, its fundamental circuit in B+j has no
element i in B intersect R: such an i would give an outgoing edge i->j.
Thus j lies in span(B intersect C). Vectors already in B intersect C do
too. Consequently span(B intersect C)=span(C). Since B is independent,
|B intersect C|=rank(C) for every positive B. Averaging gives

  x(C)=rank(C)<q(C).

This is an exact violated matroid rank inequality. The implementation
also directly computes rank(C) and checks |B intersect C|=rank(C) for
every retained positive B before exporting a cut. This is an additional
check of the final certificate, independent of the search heuristic.
The source inequality is sum_{j in C} T(anchor,j) <= rank(C)*p_anchor.

Restricted graphs may propose valid paths. Failure to find a path in
only the 8 or 32 largest-weight bases proves nothing. The lazy successor
correctly falls back to the complete graph before its rank-cut branch.
Both versions perform the same direct rank and target checks at that
branch. An incomplete-graph control explicitly manufactures a purported
closed set at a feasible target; direct rank rejects its supposed cut.

Termination is separate

Every accepted step is valid and strictly decreases the exact error.
No positive lower bound on the step size has been established. Therefore
these observations do not prove finite or polynomial global termination.
Timeout and iteration-limit outcomes remain inconclusive. Search paths
are not needed to accept a terminal certificate.

Independent terminal mixture proof for anchor40 at point12

For selected X anchor x, split all 720 Z vectors by the evaluation x.z.
The zero pool is a rank127 hyperplane. The candidate supplies a probability
distribution on its physical rank127 bases and a separate probability
distribution on the unit-evaluation vectors. Every product component
B union {u} has rank128: u has evaluation one while every vector in
span(B) has evaluation zero. The product probabilities are nonnegative,
sum to one, and give the specified zero and unit marginals.

The fresh direct terminal consumer reconstructs the physical source,
checks every one of 956 zero bases with a low-pivot rank routine, checks
96 unit probabilities, and reproduces all 720 exact conditional targets
q_j=T(anchor,j)/p_anchor. This represents 91,776 implicit full128 bases;
there is no need to expand their product list. The target has zero and
unit masses 127 and 1. No discovery trajectory is used. Five mutations
are rejected: negative basis weight, short basis, negative unit mass,
changed exact target and wrong point binding.

This proves singleton present-conditioned opposite-basis membership.
The existing generic consumer separately checks the rotation and sector
maps to transfer the singleton conclusion to all phases. Neither this
terminal mixture nor the exchange lemma establishes a joint profile law,
a complete compatible X/Z frame, a selected cover case, or an answer to
the source width problem. Cover405 and partial88 remain unchanged.

Finite independent controls

The independent local consumer constructs every exchange edge by literal
rank testing rather than by the producer's fundamental-circuit routine.
It checks 2,944 states over four small binary matroids, including loops
and parallel elements, and 21,732 literal edges. Results: 2,642 exact
path steps, 250 directly verified closed-set rank cuts and 52 matched
states. Controls support the implementation; the general proofs above
are what justify the lemmas beyond these finite examples.
