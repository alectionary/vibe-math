# Exact local matroid gate after count allocation

The accepted direct Z allocation at point 15 satisfies all 3,600 retained
moment equations and every pattern's boxes, forced/deleted coordinates,
cardinality and owner counts. This is an allocation in a necessary count
relaxation. It is not yet a mixture of the declared physical full bases.

For a positive pattern mass `lambda[m]`, each required owner bin is already a
simplex: its nonnegative allocations sum to `lambda[m]`, and a forced owner
fixes that simplex to one vertex. The remaining issue is the zero-pool
matroid. Jointly contract its forced zero elements `F0`. For free zero
indices `E`, put `q[j] = y[m,j]/lambda[m]`. The exact condition is

```
q >= 0,
sum(q) = rank(E after contraction),
sum(q[j] for j in A) <= rank(F0 union A) - rank(F0)   for every A subset E.
```

Together with the owner simplexes, this characterizes the per-pattern
sector basis hull. Matching allocations on both sectors at one common
pattern law would prove auxiliary two-sided membership, while still
relaxing mutual pairings between unanchored selected vectors.

The first bounded screen contracts `F0` exactly, groups parallel quotient
vectors, and checks loops, parallel classes, three-point lines and flats
generated by four-circuits. Every exported inequality is checked again by
direct physical rank. On the first Z allocation it found 24 violations in
1.77 seconds. These reject that allocation only. Their geometry remains
valid in a successor allocation LP, independent of the old target values.
The screen is not a complete rank separation algorithm.

The next approved gate is at most five direct-allocation successor solves
with accepted rank rows, exact target/bucket/box recovery after each solve,
and independent rank replay before reuse. Stop on no new small-flat rows
or an infeasibility candidate. Numerical infeasibility alone proves
nothing, and even an exact fixed-law obstruction would not exclude all
possible pattern laws.

If the cheap rows are exhausted, two exact terminal routes remain:

* Recover a nonnegative mixture of physical bases for each quotient
  allocation. The existing singleton exchange-repair lemma applies to a
  general represented matroid: a positive-basis exchange changes only two
  marginals. Multiple uses of the same basis require a shared mass budget.
  Exact equality of all marginals is the membership certificate; strict
  decrease alone supplies no termination guarantee.
* Produce a directly verified set `A` with `q(A) > rank(A)`. Reachability
  in a complete positive-basis exchange graph can supply such a set. A
  heuristic or incomplete graph may propose it, but the physical rank and
  rational violation must be checked independently before acceptance.

A possible bounded acceleration is an exchange-flow repair. Give each
valid exchange from a positive basis `B` a capacity whose sum over all
chosen exchanges of `B` is at most its mixture weight. Aggregate these
capacities on directed element edges, solve a rational surplus-to-deficit
flow, then distribute each used edge among its contributing bases. Split
the corresponding basis mass into exchanged bases simultaneously. Flow
conservation cancels intermediate marginal changes; the shared capacity
budget prevents overspending. A full flow repairs the allocation, and a
partial flow gives a valid new mixture. Failure to route all mass is only
a failure of this chosen capacity construction, not a rank obstruction.
This acceleration is a design, not an implemented or benchmarked method.

General linear-matroid rank separation is a submodular minimization task.
There is no justified reduction here from arbitrary binary-matroid rank
to an ordinary graph min-cut. The exchange-flow graph above serves a
different, limited purpose and does not establish such a reduction.
