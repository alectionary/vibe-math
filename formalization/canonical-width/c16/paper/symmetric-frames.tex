\begin{abstract}
Lee (arXiv:2610.06820) introduced the canonical width $f$ of a quantum code: the smallest maximum weight of a symplectic (canonical) basis of logical operators. For the $[[1088,128,16]]$ self-adjoint lifted-product code $\CLP$ over $\F_4[C_{16}]$, $f\in\{16,18\}$, and deciding which value holds is open. Earlier work reduced the question to a finite one: does the anticommutation graph between the $720$ weight-$16$ $X$-type and $720$ weight-$16$ $Z$-type logical classes contain an induced matching of size $128$? We determine the full automorphism group of this problem: it has order $32$, generated by the cyclic shift and the $X\leftrightarrow Z$ exchange, and contains exactly three involutions. We show that no width-$16$ frame is fixed by any of them. Hence every width-$16$ canonical frame, if one exists, has trivial stabilizer, and the minimum width among frames with any symmetry of the problem is $18$. The proofs combine an exact linear-algebra search with rank pruning, independent SAT encodings, and planted positive controls. We also show that the largest known partial frames ($88$ and $84$ pairs) are far from any frame, and we estimate the remaining unrestricted search at $10^{11}$--$10^{12}$ nodes. Every computational claim is labelled with the strength of its evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Logical operators of minimum weight in a stabilizer code govern its distance; a \emph{basis} of logical operators that can be measured or manipulated together must in addition satisfy the canonical (symplectic) commutation relations. Lee~\cite{lee} defines the canonical width $f(C)$ as the smallest $w$ such that $C$ has a canonical basis of logical Pauli operators of weight at most $w$, and places it in a hierarchy $d \le f_{\mathrm{span}} \le f \le f_{\mathrm{reg}} \le f_{\mathrm{rep}}$. For the self-adjoint lifted-product code $\CLP$ ($n=1088$, $k=128$, $d=16$), the source establishes $f_{\mathrm{span}}=16$ and $f_{\mathrm{reg}}=18$ (regular frames for the $C_{16}$ action), so that $f\in\{16,18\}$. Whether $f=16$ is open.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Items marked \emph{prior} are due to earlier work in the same research repository (\S\ref{sec:prior}); the rest are new here.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{(prior)} Reduction to a finite combinatorial problem: $f=16$ iff the anticommutation graph of the weight-16 pools has an induced matching of size 128 (Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}).
\item \textbf{The symmetry group.} The full automorphism group of the finite problem, including all linear maps of the logical space, is $C_{16}\times C_2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}).
\item \textbf{No symmetric frame.} No width-16 frame is fixed by any of the three involutions $T^8$, $s$, $sT^8$ of this group (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). In particular no frame is invariant under any nontrivial cyclic shift, strengthening the source's $f_{\mathrm{reg}}=18$ (full $C_{16}$-regularity) and the earlier quarter-turn exclusion.
\item \textbf{Method.} An exact search for frames based on the residual identity $\sum x_iz_i^{\mathsf T}=I$ with rank pruning and a per-group look-ahead (\S\ref{sec:method}), validated by planted frames, SAT/DRAT agreement and reproduced node counts.
\item \textbf{Near-miss rigidity.} The best known partial frames (88 and 84 pairs, \emph{prior}) share at most a handful of pairs with any frame (\S\ref{sec:nearmiss}).
\item \textbf{The remaining case.} A size estimate for the unrestricted search and a record of reformulations that do \emph{not} help (\S\ref{sec:remaining}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Evidence labels.} We distinguish \evidence{proof} (a written proof), \evidence{certified} (a computation whose output is an independently checkable certificate, e.g.\ DRAT), and \evidence{computation} (an exhaustive search by a program, cross-checked as stated but not certified).

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The code}
$\CLP$ is the self-adjoint lifted product of the $3\times5$ matrix over $\F_4[C_{16}]$ with coefficient array $((1,1,1,1,1),(1,1,\omega,0,\omega^2),(1,\omega^2,1,\omega,0))$ and exponent array $((0,0,0,0,0),(0,6,12,9,3),(0,12,6,2,14))$~\cite[eq.~(50)]{lee}, expanded to binary by the trace representation $\rho(\omega)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. It has $480$ independent checks of weight at most $9$ per sector, $n=1088$, $k=128$, and both CSS distances equal $16$.

\subsection{Canonical frames and width}
\begin{definition}
A \emph{canonical frame} of a CSS code is a family $(\bar X_i,\bar Z_i)_{i=1}^k$ of logical operators with $\bar X_i\bar Z_j=(-1)^{\delta_{ij}}\bar Z_j\bar X_i$ and all other pairs commuting. Its width is the largest operator weight; $f(C)$ is the minimum width over frames.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Logical coordinates and the cyclic action}
Fix the known width-18 frame as a basis of the logical space; logical classes then have coordinates in $\F_2^{128}$, arranged as $8$ blocks (``seeds'') of $16$ bits. The cyclic group $C_{16}$ acts by the shift $T$, which rotates every block by one position. Writing $R=\F_2[t]/(t^{16}+1)=\F_2[t]/((t+1)^{16})$, a local ring, the logical space is the free module $R^8$.

\section{Reduction to an induced-matching problem (prior work)}\label{sec:prior}

\begin{proposition}[pools; \evidence{computation}, prior]\label{prop:pools}
The nontrivial pure logical classes of weight at most $17$ are exactly $720$ $X$-classes and $720$ $Z$-classes, all of weight $16$, forming $45$ free $T$-orbits in each sector. Any canonical frame of width at most $17$ yields a pure-sector frame drawn from these pools.
\end{proposition}

Let $x_1,\dots,x_{720}$ and $z_1,\dots,z_{720}$ be their coordinates, indexed so that $T$ acts by $16a+t\mapsto 16a+(t+1 \bmod 16)$, and let $G_{ij}=\langle x_i,z_j\rangle\in\F_2$.

\begin{proposition}[\evidence{proof}]\label{prop:reduction}
$f(\CLP)=16$ iff there are $S,T\subseteq[720]$ with $|S|=|T|=128$ and $G[S,T]$ a permutation matrix, i.e.\ iff the bipartite anticommutation graph has an induced matching of size $128$. Since $\operatorname{rank}_{\F_2}G\le 128$, no induced matching is larger.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A permutation submatrix $G[S,T]$ means $X_SZ_T^{\mathsf T}$ is a permutation matrix, so $X_S$ is invertible and the pairs form a canonical frame; the converse is Proposition~\ref{prop:pools}. An induced matching of size $m$ gives an identity $m\times m$ submatrix, so $m\le\operatorname{rank}G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\evidence{computation}]\label{lem:sym}
$G$ is symmetric and $T$-invariant: $G_{ij}=G_{ji}$ and $G_{\tau i,\tau j}=G_{ij}$ for the shift $\tau$ on indices. Every pool orbit is a free cyclic $R$-module of rank one.
\end{lemma}

Other prior results we use or compare against: the nonexistence of frames containing $\ge 13$ seed-4 $X$ monomials (via a scan of all $3^{16}$ partner assignments); exclusion of quarter-turn ($T^4$) invariant frames by DRAT-certified SAT; explicit partial frames with $84$ and $88$ pairs; and a series of moment and semidefinite relaxations that are feasible at $128$. \pending{exact citations to repository commits and documents}

\section{The symmetry group}\label{sec:aut}

\begin{theorem}[\evidence{computation} with a certificate-style argument]\label{thm:aut}
Let $\mathcal G$ be the group of pairs of bijections $(\pi,\pi')$ of $[720]$ with $G_{\pi i,\pi' j}=G_{ij}$, extended by the maps exchanging the two sides (valid since $G$ is symmetric). Then $\mathcal G=\langle T\rangle\times\langle s\rangle\cong C_{16}\times C_2$, where $s(i,j)=(j,i)$. In particular every linear symmetry of the logical space preserving both pools induces an element of $\mathcal G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Argument]
Colour refinement of the anticommutation graph (1440 vertices) stabilizes at the $90$ $T$-orbits ($45$ classes of size $32$ when the sides may be exchanged). After individualizing one vertex $v$, refinement reaches the discrete partition. Since refinement is equivariant, any automorphism fixing $v$ fixes every vertex, so $|\mathcal G|\le|\text{colour class of }v|=32$; and $T$, $s$ are automorphisms. The refinement is reproduced by \texttt{explore/gram\_wl.py}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
$\mathcal G$ has exactly three involutions: $T^8$, $s$ and $sT^8$. Every nontrivial subgroup of $\mathcal G$ contains one of them, and every subgroup of $\langle T\rangle$ other than $\{1\}$ contains $T^8$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[\evidence{proof}]\label{lem:classes}
Let $F$ be a frame with vertex sets $(S,T)$. Since a frame is determined by $(S,T)$:
(i) $F$ is $T^8$-fixed iff $\tau S=S$ and $\tau T=T$;
(ii) $F$ is $s$-fixed iff $S=T$;
(iii) $F$ is $sT^8$-fixed iff $T=\tau S$.
\end{lemma}

\section{No symmetric frame}\label{sec:main}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
No width-$16$ canonical frame of $\CLP$ is fixed by $T^8$, by $s$ or by $sT^8$. Consequently every width-$16$ frame, if one exists, has trivial stabilizer in $\mathcal G$ and lies in a free orbit of $32$ frames.
\end{theorem}

\section{Method: exact search on the residual identity}\label{sec:method}

A choice of pairs $(x_i,z_i)$ forms a frame iff $\sum_i x_i z_i^{\mathsf T}=I$ over $\F_2$ with $128$ terms. The search state is a partial frame $P$ and the residual $R=I-\sum_{P}x_iz_i^{\mathsf T}$, the projection onto $C=\{z:z\in P\}^\perp$ along $\operatorname{span}\{x:x\in P\}$.

\begin{lemma}[branching; \evidence{proof}]
If $R_{ck}=1$, every completion contains a remaining pair with $x_c=z_k=1$. Branching over these pairs, and excluding earlier siblings in later subtrees, visits every completion exactly once.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[rank pruning; \evidence{proof}]
Let $X'$ ($Z'$) be the unused pool vectors orthogonal to all chosen $Z$ ($X$) vectors and belonging to some still-admissible pair. A completion exists only if $\operatorname{span}X'=C$ and $\operatorname{span}Z'=D$, both of dimension $128-|P|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[look-ahead splits by group; \evidence{proof}]
For a candidate item with $X$-group $a$ and $Z$-group $b$, the child passes the rank test iff $\operatorname{rank}(X'\cap b^\perp)\ge \mathit{need}-g$ and $\operatorname{rank}(Z'\cap a^\perp)\ge\mathit{need}-g$; the pairing between these spans is then automatically perfect. Hence the look-ahead costs one span computation per group rather than per item.
\end{lemma}

Restricted classes are searched with \emph{items} equal to orbits of pairs under the involution. Validation: the general implementation reproduces the original node counts exactly ($15{,}088$ on $T^4$); planted random $T$-invariant frames appended to the pools are found in every class; the quarter $\{0,1\}$ enumeration returns exactly $3^{16}=43{,}046{,}721$ frames. \pending{independent reimplementation / code review}

\section{Near-miss partial frames are far from frames}\label{sec:nearmiss}

\begin{proposition}[\evidence{computation}]
Neither the $88$-pair nor the $84$-pair partial frame extends by a single pair. A greedy minimal subset of $7$ of the $88$ pairs already admits no completion, and every sampled subset of $8$ or more pairs of the largest rank-compatible subsets ($40$ resp.\ $41$ pairs) admits no completion.
\end{proposition}
The completion search recovers planted frames after removing up to $60$ of their pairs. The interpretation is that combinatorially large partial frames are far, in shared pairs, from linear-algebraically consistent ones: rank-consistent partial frames typically die within $3$--$8$ further choices.

\section{The remaining problem}\label{sec:remaining}

Knuth estimates for the unrestricted search give roughly $2\times10^{11}$--$2\times10^{12}$ nodes with a heavy tail, i.e.\ $10^4$--$10^6$ core-hours at a few thousand nodes per second. The following reformulations were tested and do \emph{not} reduce it materially: parity checks $\#\{e\in F: z_e^{\mathsf T}Lx_e=1\}\equiv\operatorname{tr}L$ (smallest odd-trace check found involves $\ge1515$ pairs); small cocircuits of the pool matroids (unit functionals, $66$--$88$ vectors, are near-minimal); the orbit-aggregated rank-inequality LP (optimum exactly $128$); root normalization by a forced vector (no net gain); and orbits forced by rank (none). \pending{orderly generation with the 32-element group}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{question}
Is $f(\CLP)=16$? Equivalently, does the anticommutation graph contain an asymmetric induced matching of size $128$?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
What is the induced matching number of the anticommutation graph? It lies in $[88,128]$. Is it far from the rank bound?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Why do rank-consistent partial frames die after few choices while combinatorial partial frames reach $88$? Can the gap be turned into an obstruction?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Is there a symmetrization principle for canonical frames of lifted-product codes over cyclic $2$-group lifts? (A naive residue descent fails in an enlarged pool \pending{cite}.) The local-ring structure $R=\F_2[t]/(t+1)^{16}$, with the self-dual $T^8$-fixed submodule $(1+t^8)R^8$, is a natural place to look.
\end{question}

\begin{question}
Define the \emph{symmetric width} $f_{\mathrm{sym}}$ as the minimum width over frames with nontrivial stabilizer in the code's automorphism group. Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, with the source's regular width-18 frame, gives $f_{\mathrm{sym}}(\CLP)=18$. Is $f=f_{\mathrm{sym}}$ here? When do the two differ, e.g.\ for $C^{\mathrm{LP}}_{20,10}$?
\end{question}
