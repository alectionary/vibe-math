\title{No Symmetric Width-16 Canonical Frame for the Lifted-Product Code $\CLP$}
\author{}
\date{Draft, 3 November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Lee asks for the canonical width $f$ of the $[[1088,128,16]]$ self-adjoint lifted-product code $\CLP$, for which $f\in\{16,18\}$ is known. The question reduces to whether the anticommutation graph between the $720$ weight-$16$ $X$-type and $720$ weight-$16$ $Z$-type logical classes has an induced matching of size $128$. We determine the full automorphism group of this finite problem: it is $C_{16}\times C_2$, generated by the cyclic shift and the $X\leftrightarrow Z$ exchange, and it has exactly three involutions; this computation and all written proofs are checked in Lean~4. We show, by exhaustive computation validated with planted positive controls and a second, differently pruned search, that no width-$16$ canonical frame is fixed by any of them. Each search writes a proof certificate (its full search tree), and a checker written by a different AI model from a specification alone, which regenerates the problem data itself, accepts all three certificates. Every width-$16$ frame, if one exists, therefore has trivial stabilizer, and the minimum width among frames with a nontrivial symmetry is $18$. We also explain the block structure of the problem (the $3^{16}$ frames of one quarter, and why no frame splits along the coordinate blocks), show that the largest known partial frames ($88$ and $84$ pairs) are far from any frame, and size the remaining unrestricted search. The source question itself remains open.
\end{abstract}

Lee~\cite{lee} defines the canonical width $f(C)$ of a quantum code as the smallest $w$ for which $C$ has a canonical (symplectic) basis of logical Pauli operators of weight at most $w$. The source places it in the hierarchy $d\le f_{\mathrm{span}}\le f\le f_{\mathrm{reg}}\le f_{\mathrm{rep}}$ and states $f_{\mathrm{span}}=16$ and $f_{\mathrm{reg}}=18$ for $\CLP$ with its $C_{16}$ action. Earlier work in this repository showed $f\in\{16,18\}$ and reduced the question to a finite matching problem (\S\ref{sec:prior}).

\begin{theorem}[main result]\label{thm:main}
No width-$16$ canonical frame of $\CLP$ is fixed by a nontrivial element of the automorphism group $C_{16}\times C_2$ of the frame problem. Every width-$16$ frame, if one exists, lies in a free orbit of $32$ frames. In particular the minimum width over frames with a nontrivial symmetry is $18$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is computational: Theorem~\ref{thm:aut} identifies the group, Lemma~\ref{lem:classes} reduces the claim to three involution classes, and Table~\ref{tab:evidence} gives the evidence for each. The $T^8$ case subsumes the source's $f_{\mathrm{reg}}=18$ and the earlier exclusion of $T^4$-invariant frames~\cite{structural}, since every nontrivial subgroup of $C_{16}$ contains $T^8$.

\paragraph{Evidence labels.} \evidence{proof}: written proof. \evidence{certified}: computation that outputs an independently checkable certificate (a DRAT proof, or a search-tree certificate accepted by an independently written checker, \S\ref{sec:method}). \evidence{computation}: exhaustive search by a program, cross-checked as stated, not certified. Every written proof has a Lean version (\S\ref{sec:lean}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} New here: Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for the classes $T^8$, $s$ and $sT^8$; the linear-algebra search of \S\ref{sec:method} with its validation; the structural results on blocks (Lemma~\ref{lem:G} and \S\ref{sec:evidence}: $Z=\pi(X)$, the $3^{16}$ count, no block splitting); the near-miss analysis of \S\ref{sec:nearmiss}; the negative results of \S\ref{sec:remaining}; the Lean formalization of \S\ref{sec:lean}, including Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}; an independent implementation by a second agent, and proof certificates for the three classes accepted by an independently written checker (\S\ref{sec:method}). Cited from earlier repository work~\cite{investigation,enumeration,structural}: the pools and the reduction, the $T^4$ exclusion, the partial frames and the relaxation experiments.

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{The code.} $\CLP$ is the self-adjoint lifted product~\cite{pk} of the $3\times5$ matrix over $\F_4[C_{16}]$ with coefficient array $((1,1,1,1,1),(1,1,\omega,0,\omega^2),(1,\omega^2,1,\omega,0))$ and exponent array $((0,0,0,0,0),(0,6,12,9,3),(0,12,6,2,14))$~\cite[eq.~(50)]{lee}, expanded to binary by the trace representation $\rho(\omega)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\rho(\omega^2)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. It has $480$ independent checks of weight at most $9$ in each sector, $n=1088$ and $k=128$; both CSS distances are $16$~\cite{enumeration}. The checks are rebuilt independently by \prior{verify_partial_frame.py}{\texttt{verify\_partial\_frame.py}}.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{canonical frame} of a CSS code is a family $(\bar X_i,\bar Z_i)_{i=1}^{k}$ of logical operators in which $\bar X_i$ and $\bar Z_j$ anticommute iff $i=j$ and all other pairs commute. Its width is its largest operator weight, and $f(C)$ is the minimum width.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Coordinates and the cyclic action.} Fix the width-18 frame as a basis of the logical space. Logical classes then have coordinates in $\F_2^{128}$, arranged as eight blocks (seeds) of $16$ bits, and the shift $T$ rotates each block by one position. With $R=\F_2[t]/(t^{16}+1)=\F_2[t]/((t+1)^{16})$, a local ring, the logical space is the free module $R^8$.

\section{Reduction to an induced-matching problem (earlier work)}\label{sec:prior}

\begin{proposition}[\evidence{computation}; \cite{enumeration}]\label{prop:pools}
The nontrivial pure logical classes of weight at most $17$ are exactly $720$ $X$-classes and $720$ $Z$-classes, all of weight $16$, forming $45$ free $T$-orbits per sector. Any canonical frame of width at most $17$ yields a pure-sector frame drawn from these pools.
\end{proposition}

The pool coordinates are \prior{pools/xcoords.txt}{\texttt{pools/xcoords.txt}} and \prior{pools/zcoords.txt}{\texttt{pools/zcoords.txt}}; index $16a+t$ is $T^t$ applied to the $a$-th orbit representative. Write $G_{ij}=\langle x_i,z_j\rangle\in\F_2$.

\begin{proposition}[\evidence{proof}]\label{prop:reduction}
$f(\CLP)=16$ iff there are $S,T\subseteq\{1,\dots,720\}$ with $|S|=|T|=128$ and $G[S,T]$ a permutation matrix, i.e.\ iff the bipartite anticommutation graph has an induced matching of size $128$. No induced matching is larger, since its size is at most $\operatorname{rank}_{\F_2}G\le128$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $G[S,T]$ is a permutation matrix then $X_SZ_T^{\mathsf T}$ is invertible and the matched pairs form a canonical frame; the converse is Proposition~\ref{prop:pools}. An induced matching of size $m$ is an $m\times m$ identity submatrix of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\evidence{computation}]\label{lem:G}
$G$ is symmetric and $T$-invariant, and every pool orbit spans a free rank-one $R$-module. Moreover $z_i=\pi(x_i)$ for every index $i$, where $\pi$ exchanges blocks $2\leftrightarrow4$ and $3\leftrightarrow5$; so $G_{ij}=x_i^{\mathsf T}\pi x_j$ and a frame is a pair of bases from one pool, dual for this symmetric form. (Symmetry is asserted by \oursf{satcheck/sat_symmetric.py} and checked in Lean; the module statement is computed in \S\ref{sec:remaining}; $z_i=\pi(x_i)$ is checked by \oursf{theory/check_pi.py}.)
\end{lemma}

Earlier results compared against below: frames containing at least $13$ seed-4 $X$ monomials do not exist, by a scan of all $3^{16}$ partner assignments~\cite{investigation}; $T^4$-invariant frames do not exist (DRAT-certified)~\cite{structural}; explicit partial frames with $84$ and $88$ pairs exist (\prior{partial-frame-84.json}{\texttt{partial-frame-84.json}}, \prior{partial-frame-88.json}{\texttt{partial-frame-88.json}}); the basic degree-two moment relaxation is feasible at $128$~\cite{structural}; and an aggregate residue-descent argument fails in enlarged pools~\cite{inverse4h}.

\section{The symmetry group}\label{sec:aut}

\begin{theorem}[\evidence{computation}, checked in Lean]\label{thm:aut}
Let $\mathcal G$ be the group of pairs of bijections $(\pi,\pi')$ of the index set with $G_{\pi i,\pi' j}=G_{ij}$, together with their compositions with the side exchange $s:(i,j)\mapsto(j,i)$, which preserves frames because $G$ is symmetric. Then $\mathcal G=\langle T\rangle\times\langle s\rangle\cong C_{16}\times C_2$. Every linear map of the logical space preserving both pools induces an element of $\mathcal G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Colour refinement~\cite{mckay} of the anticommutation graph ($1440$ vertices) stabilizes at the $90$ $T$-orbits, or at $45$ classes of size $32$ when the two sides may be exchanged. After one vertex $v$ is individualized, refinement reaches the discrete partition. Refinement is equivariant, so an automorphism fixing $v$ fixes every vertex; hence $|\mathcal G|$ is at most the size of the colour class of $v$, which is $32$. Both $T$ and $s$ are automorphisms. The computation is \oursf{explore/gram_wl.py}; coordinate permutations alone are bounded in the same way by \oursf{explore/auto_wl.py}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
$\mathcal G$ has exactly three involutions, $T^8$, $s$ and $sT^8$, and every nontrivial subgroup of $\mathcal G$ contains one of them.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[\evidence{proof}]\label{lem:classes}
A frame is determined by its vertex sets $(S,T)$. Writing $\tau$ for $T^8$ on indices: (i) the frame is $T^8$-fixed iff $\tau S=S$ and $\tau T=T$; (ii) it is $s$-fixed iff $S=T$; (iii) it is $sT^8$-fixed iff $T=\tau S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The image of a frame under an element of $\mathcal G$ is the frame on the image vertex sets, and the induced matching on given vertex sets is unique.
\end{proof}

\section{Method: exact search on the residual identity}\label{sec:method}

A family of $128$ pairs $(x_i,z_i)$ is a frame iff $\sum_i x_iz_i^{\mathsf T}=I$ over $\F_2$. The search state is a partial frame $P$ together with the residual $R=I-\sum_{P}x_iz_i^{\mathsf T}$, the projection onto $C=\{z_i:i\in P\}^{\perp}$ along $\operatorname{span}\{x_i:i\in P\}$.

\begin{lemma}[branching; \evidence{proof}]
If $R_{ck}=1$, every completion contains a remaining pair with $x_c=z_k=1$. Branching over those pairs, with earlier siblings excluded from later subtrees, visits each completion exactly once.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[rank pruning; \evidence{proof}]
Let $X'$ ($Z'$) be the unused pool vectors orthogonal to all chosen $Z$ ($X$) vectors that belong to a still-admissible pair. A completion exists only if $\operatorname{span}X'=C$ and $\operatorname{span}Z'=D$, both of dimension $128-|P|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[look-ahead; \evidence{proof}]
For an item with $X$-group $a$ and $Z$-group $b$ of size $g$, the child passes the rank test iff $\operatorname{rank}(X'\cap b^{\perp})\ge \mathit{need}-g$ and $\operatorname{rank}(Z'\cap a^{\perp})\ge\mathit{need}-g$. The pairing between these spans is then perfect automatically, so the look-ahead costs one span computation per group rather than per item.
\end{lemma}

\section{Near-miss partial frames are far from frames}\label{sec:nearmiss}

\begin{proposition}[\evidence{computation}]
Neither the $88$-pair nor the $84$-pair partial frame extends by a single pair. Some $7$ of the $88$ pairs already admit no completion. Every sampled subset of $8$ or more pairs of the largest rank-compatible subsets ($40$ and $41$ pairs) admits no completion.
\end{proposition}
The driver is \oursf{la/nearmiss.py}, with fixed pairs passed to \texttt{la\_gen -p}. As a control, planted frames are recovered after up to $60$ of their pairs are removed. Large combinatorial partial frames are therefore far, in shared pairs, from rank-consistent ones; rank-consistent partial frames typically fail within $3$ to $8$ further choices.

\section{The remaining problem}\label{sec:remaining}

Knuth's estimator~\cite{knuth} puts the unrestricted search at roughly $2\times10^{11}$--$2\times10^{12}$ nodes, with a heavy tail. At a few thousand nodes per second this is $10^4$--$10^6$ core-hours, comparable to large SAT-based proofs~\cite{pythagorean,schur5}.

The following were tested and do not reduce the search materially:
\begin{itemize}
\item parity checks $\#\{e\in F:z_e^{\mathsf T}Lx_e=1\}\equiv\operatorname{tr}L$ (the smallest odd-trace check found involves at least $1515$ pairs; \oursf{explore/parity_checks.py});
\item small cocircuits of the pool matroids (unit functionals, which meet $66$--$88$ pool vectors, are near-minimal; \oursf{explore/cocircuits.py});
\item the orbit-aggregated rank-inequality LP, whose optimum is exactly $128$ (\oursf{explore/rank_lp.py});
\item root normalization by a forced vector, with no net gain;
\item orbits forced by rank, of which there are none;
\item branching first on the blocks $2,4,6$ where local frames fail, or on the other blocks: Knuth estimates are $1$--$5$ times the default fewest-candidates rule (\ours{order-exp/RESULTS.md}{\texttt{order-exp/RESULTS.md}}).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Structured pieces a frame cannot contain.} \evidence{proof; computation} If a frame contains $d$ pairs spanning $(U,U')$, the remaining pairs form a frame of $(U'^{\perp},U^{\perp})$. Using this: no frame contains a whole $X$-orbit paired one-to-one with a whole $Z$-orbit ($167$ of the $177$ such orbit pairs fail by rank, the other $10$ by complete search); $41$ of the $45$ orbits can never appear whole in a frame on either side, and orbits $9,10,11,33$ remain undecided after time-limited searches. The $88$-pair partial frame contains two of the excluded orbit pairs, which explains why it cannot be completed. None of this bounds how many vectors a frame takes from each orbit, so it is not an obstruction. Details: \ours{theory/THEORY-NOTES-2.md}{\texttt{theory/THEORY-NOTES-2.md}}.

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{question}[source question~\cite{lee}]
Is $f(\CLP)=16$? Equivalently, does the anticommutation graph contain an asymmetric induced matching of size $128$?
\end{question}

Follow-up directions raised by this work (not source-posed):
\begin{itemize}
\item What is the induced matching number of the anticommutation graph? It lies between $88$ and $128$.
\item Why do rank-consistent partial frames fail after few choices while combinatorial ones reach $88$? Can this gap be turned into an obstruction?
\item Is there a small $k$ with $|F\cap X_a|\le k$ for every frame $F$ and orbit $a$? With $\sum_a|F\cap X_a|=128$ over $45$ orbits, a bound $k<3$ would exclude frames.
\item Is there a symmetrization principle for canonical frames of lifted-product codes over cyclic $2$-group lifts? A naive residue descent fails~\cite{inverse4h}. The local ring $R$ and its self-dual $T^8$-fixed submodule $(1+t^8)R^8$ are natural tools.
\item Let the \emph{symmetric width} be the minimum width over frames with nontrivial stabilizer. It equals $18$ here. When does it differ from $f$, for instance for $C^{\mathrm{LP}}_{20,10}$?
\end{itemize}
