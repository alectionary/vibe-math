\begin{abstract}
We construct a fifteen-qubit CSS code with one encoded qubit and a
$(7,0)$-absorbing set on which every supported binary $Z$-error is corrected
by zero-initialized syndrome-based Gallager-B at initialization or after one
iteration. Both the hard-decision syndrome and outgoing extrinsic-message
parities accept the result. The actual decoding Tanner graph is connected,
its fifteen checks are distinct, and the stabilizer space admits no direct
product decomposition across any nontrivial partition of the physical qubits.
Thus the successful absorbing support is not an isolated stabilizer-state
factor. An explicit overlap spanning tree certifies the last assertion.
The complete finite construction, graph connectivity, recovery modulo the
whole opposite stabilizer span and unbounded stopped-execution properties
are checked in Lean, and independently reconstructed from literal matrices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and motivating question}
Morris, Pllaha and Kelley ask in Section VI of
\emph{Absorbing Sets in Quantum LDPC Codes}, arXiv:2307.14532v2,
May 10, 2024, whether every absorbing set is a trapping set for their
syndrome-based Gallager-B decoder \cite{mpk}. Their Definition 5 compares
even and odd induced check neighbors. Definition 3 describes persistent
syndrome mismatch or nonconvergence for some error supported on the set.
The simplex matrix already supplies an absorbing component with no such
failure; a disconnected CSS embedding can also make all its supported
errors correctable modulo stabilizers \cite{previous}. This motivates a
stronger, project-derived question: can both properties hold in a connected
code without an isolated stabilizer-state factor?

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is a binary CSS pair $(H_X,H_Z)$ on fifteen physical qubits with
$\operatorname{rank}H_X=\operatorname{rank}H_Z=7$ and the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The actual $H_X$ Tanner graph, including all fifteen distinct check
vertices and all fifteen variable vertices, is connected.
\item The first seven variables form a $(7,0)$-absorbing set $A$.
\item Every $Z$-error supported on $A$ is accepted at initialization or
within one full Gallager-B iteration, under both hard-decision and outgoing
extrinsic-edge syndrome tests. Its residual belongs to $\row H_Z$.
\item The binary stabilizer space has no product decomposition across any
nonempty proper partition of the fifteen physical coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
In particular the code encodes one qubit, and $A$ is not trapping for the
specified stopped decoder.
\end{theorem}
The matrices and decoder are given in Sections~\ref{sec:matrix}
and~\ref{sec:decode}; connectivity and the product obstruction are proved
in Section~\ref{sec:coupling}. Appendix~\ref{app:lean} gives the exact formal
model, complete central proof bodies and current and immutable proof links.
The complete source comparison and access ledger accompanies the artifact.

The construction is asymmetric: the matrix detecting $Z$-errors is $H_X$,
while successful recovery is measured modulo the entire span of $H_Z$.
It permits redundant checks and four-cycles; the exterior auxiliary variable $C$ has degree one in the decoding graph. It asserts neither correction
of both Pauli sectors nor a high-girth or degree-three counterexample.
These qualifications determine the mathematical statement.

\section{Literal CSS matrices}\label{sec:matrix}
Let $H$ be the $7\times7$ matrix with $H_{a,v}=a\cdot v$, for nonzero
$a,v\in\mathbb F_2^3$ in integer order $1,\ldots,7$, least significant
binary digit first. Its rows, as integer bit masks, are
\[
 (85,102,51,120,45,30,75).
\]
Let $G$ consist of rows $1,2,4$ of $H$, set
$K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}G\\\mathbf1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$u=H\mathbf e_1^{\mathsf T}$ and $v=G\mathbf e_1^{\mathsf T}$.
Order the physical coordinates as $A_1,\ldots,A_7,B_1,\ldots,B_7,C$.
The actual matrices are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
H_X=\begin{pmatrix}
 H&u\mathbf e_1&0\\
 0&H&0\\
 0&\mathbf e_1&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H_Z=\begin{pmatrix}
 K&0&0\\
 v\mathbf e_1&G&v
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf e_1$ is a row vector of length seven; block zero sizes are
determined by the displayed matrices. $H_X$ has fifteen rows and $H_Z$
has seven rows. For completely unambiguous reproduction, their row masks
with coordinate $A_1$ as the least significant bit are
\begin{align*}
H_X:\quad &(213,102,179,120,173,30,203,\\[-2pt]
           &\hspace{15mm}10880,13056,6528,15360,5760,3840,9600,16512),\\
H_Z:\quad &(85,102,120,127,27265,13056,15360).
\end{align*}
They can also be obtained from the earlier two-block CSS pair, adjoining
an $X$-stabilized qubit $C$, by CNOTs $A_1\to B_1$ and $C\to B_1$.
The exact binary gate transformations are checked by
\texttt{two\_CNOT\_X\_bridge} and \texttt{two\_CNOT\_Z\_bridge}; our claims
are checked directly on the resulting matrices rather than inferred from
an unspecified equivalence.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:css}
The matrices in \eqref{eq:pair} commute as CSS checks and both have rank
seven. All fifteen $X$ rows are distinct. The set $A$ is $(7,0)$-absorbing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The dot product of each of the fifteen displayed $X$ rows with each of
the seven $Z$ rows is zero. A basis for the $X$ rows consists of masks
\[
 (213,102,120,10880,13056,15360,16512).
\]
Each actual $X$ row is a combination of these seven actual rows, and all
$128$ combinations of the basis are distinct. The seven $Z$ rows likewise
have $128$ distinct combinations. These are finite proofs of both ranks;
they do not infer independence from merely having seven generators.
The distinctness test compares all pairs of actual $X$ rows.

The first seven checks restrict on $A$ to $H$, with induced degree four;
the remaining checks have no neighbor in $A$. Each variable of $A$ has
four even induced neighbors and no odd neighbor. Thus $A$ has seven
variables and zero odd checks, and satisfies the strict absorbing inequality.
All assertions are checked on the literal data by the declarations in the
proof map of Appendix~\ref{app:lean}.
\end{proof}
The CSS dimension formula now gives $k=15-7-7=1$. Explicitly,
$Z$ mask $32641$ commutes with every $X$ check and is outside $\row H_Z$.
$X$ mask $16256$ commutes with every $Z$ check and anticommutes with that
$Z$ word. These witnesses are checked in the same model.

\section{Decoder and all supported errors}\label{sec:decode}
All messages are binary. Variable-to-check messages start at zero. A check
sends its input syndrome bit plus the XOR of its other incoming messages.
A variable sends a strict majority of its other incoming check messages,
with a tie returning zero; its hard decision uses all its check neighbors.
These are the zero-initialized, extrinsic, zero-tie rules of Section II-B
of \cite{mpk}. We test both the syndrome of the hard word and the parity
of the outgoing variable messages at each check.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recovery}
For every error $e\in\mathbb F_2^7$ on $A$, the first hard output is
$(\mathbf e_p,0,0)$ if $p=\sum_{i=1}^7 e_i i\ne0$ in $\mathbb F_2^3$,
and zero otherwise. Its syndrome and the first outgoing-edge syndrome
both equal the input syndrome. Its residual is in $\row H_Z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first seven input syndrome bits are $a\cdot p$; the other eight are
zero. At $A_i$, the initial one-vote count is zero for $p=0$, four for
$p=i$, and two otherwise. This follows by counting solutions of the two
binary equations $a\cdot i=a\cdot p=1$. Consequently the hard output on
$A$ is as stated. The target $B_1$ has nine neighbors with at most four
one-votes, so its hard output is zero. Every other exterior variable
receives only zeros.

The extra check involving $B_1$ and $C$ is decisive for the extrinsic test:
even after excluding a zero vote, $B_1$ sees at most four ones among eight
votes and sends zero. Every exterior outgoing message is therefore zero.
On $A$, variable $p$ sends one on every incident edge. When $i\ne p$,
excluding a zero from two ones and two zeros sends one; excluding a one
sends zero. A check with syndrome one receives exactly one one from $A$,
and a check with syndrome zero receives either zero or four ones. Its
outgoing parity is therefore the input syndrome in both cases.

For the actual matrices, complete enumeration of $e\in\mathrm{BitVec}(7)$
proves the hard-output formula, both syndrome equalities, and the existence
of seven binary coefficients expressing $e+\hat e$ in the \emph{whole}
$H_Z$ span. These are the Lean theorems \texttt{first\_output\_formula},
\texttt{first\_hard\_matches}, \texttt{first\_edge\_matches} and
\texttt{complete\_quantum\_recovery}. The independent verifier reconstructs
all $128$ inputs with literal lists and independently enumerates the
complete stabilizer spans. Thus the quantum conclusion does not confuse a
zero residual syndrome with stabilizer equivalence.
\end{proof}
Zero syndromes are accepted at initialization; otherwise Lemma~\ref{lem:recovery}
gives acceptance at the first step. A halted decoder is extended by its
constant terminal output. For every input, every check is then correct and
every variable constant at all times $t\ge1$. The structural Lean proofs
\texttt{all\_checks\_eventually\_correct} and
\texttt{all\_variables\_converge} imply \texttt{selected\_not\_trapping}.
This is an infinite-time stopped-execution statement, not a finite
observation of unhalted message dynamics.

\section{Connectivity and intrinsic physical coupling}\label{sec:coupling}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:connected}
The actual thirty-vertex bipartite decoding graph is connected.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every physical variable shares an actual $X$ check with $B_1$. Every check
has at least one variable neighbor. Hence every variable has a walk of
length at most two to $B_1$, and every check has a walk of length at most
three. Reverse and concatenate these walks to connect any ordered pair
of graph vertices. \texttt{decoding\_graph\_connected} constructs these
walks with the literal adjacency relation.
\end{proof}
Connectivity of a chosen redundant check representation alone does not
exclude a product stabilizer code. We therefore prove a representation
independent obstruction. Write
$S=(\row H_X)\times(\row H_Z)$ for the binary stabilizer space. For a cut
$Q$, let $r_Q$ restrict both binary components to $Q$, zeroing the other
coordinates. A direct product across $Q\mid Q^c$ requires $r_Q(S)\subseteq S$.
We use this necessary closure property as the exact binary meaning of a
physical product split in the formal theorem.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cut}
For every nonempty proper $Q\subset\{0,\ldots,14\}$, there are actual
$X$ and $Z$ rows whose restrictions to $Q$ anticommute. In particular
$r_Q(S)\not\subseteq S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Table~\ref{tab:tree} gives fourteen pairs of rows whose support intersections
are exactly the two coordinates in the first column. Those pairs form a
spanning tree on all fifteen physical coordinates. A proper nonempty cut
must cross an edge: if no edge crossed, equality of cut membership would
propagate from vertex zero along the tree to every vertex, making the cut
empty or full. At a crossed pair, the restricted $X/Z$ dot product is one.
If the projected $X$ row belonged to $\row H_X$, it would commute with
every $Z$ row, a contradiction. Thus projecting the corresponding pure
$X$ stabilizer exits $S$.

Lean checks each row-pair projection identity and proves the tree-cut
argument by explicit Boolean propagation. \texttt{cut\_anticommutation}
and \texttt{no\_physical\_product\_cut} quantify over all fifteen-bit cuts.
No assumption about a preferred independent generating set is used.
\end{proof}

The product obstruction concerns the given physical tensor factors; it does
not assert an obstruction to global Clifford decoding.

\section{Source interpretation and earlier ingredients}
Hamming syndrome representatives and the simplex/Steane CSS ingredients
are classical \cite{hamming,steane}. Local anticommutation of restricted
stabilizers is also a standard tool in stabilizer entanglement
\cite{fattal}. A modern statement for stabilizer subspaces is Lemma 1 of
Makuta and Augusiak \cite{makuta}. We use the binary obstruction directly
and claim no new general entanglement criterion. The concrete contribution
is its coexistence with absorption and complete successful Gallager-B
recovery in a connected actual decoding graph.

The inspected source's dynamical Definition 3 and quantum logical failure
must be distinguished. Here both dynamical success and recovery modulo
actual opposite $Z$ stabilizers hold. The source also discusses logical
miscorrections and uses a row-space expression involving its $H_A$ in
Theorem 5. Our asymmetric pair does not settle an interpretation that
requires that very decoded matrix to generate the error-type stabilizers,
or requires $H_X=H_Z$. We assert the explicit theorem above rather than
certify every possible reading of the author's blanket question.
The positive-$b$ result in the source has different hypotheses; our example
has $b=0$. The zero-tie and stopping conventions are essential specifications.

The exact question inspected is arXiv v2, Sections II-B and VI and
Definitions 3 and 5. Journal metadata gives March 3, 2026 publication, but
the journal full text remained subscription restricted. The accompanying
source ledger records this version gap and the scope of the prior-art
comparison.

\section{Conclusion}
A connected ambient decoding graph and absence of every physical product
factor do not force this $(7,0)$-absorbing support to fail Gallager-B.
Every supported $Z$-error is recovered modulo the entire opposite
stabilizer span. Symmetric CSS pairs, prescribed girth or degree conditions,
and both-sector recovery remain separate, unassessed variants.
A requirement that $A$ carry a nontrivial $Z$ logical is incompatible with
correcting every supported $Z$-error: identity and that logical have the
same syndrome. The concrete theorem \texttt{no\_supported\_Z\_logical}
checks this consequence for the present pair.
