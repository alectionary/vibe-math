# Prior art for dyadic decoding objectives

Reviewed October 23, 2026. The comparison sought is conditional: which graph objective predicts performance for a specified dyadic ensemble, channel and decoder? Existing work already connects short cycles and absorbing/trapping sets to decoding failures. Merely observing such a connection again is not a new result.

## Source question and existing source experiments

Anthony Gómez-Fonseca, Gretchen L. Matthews, Kirsten D. Morris and Tefjol Pllaha, *Combinatorial Analysis of Dyadic and Quasi-Dyadic Codes*, [arXiv:2605.01942v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01942v1), submitted May 3, 2026. Section 7 asks which optimization objectives best correlate with performance; the preceding sentence asks for absorbing-set extensions and dyadic invariants predicting the error floor. This is an open research direction, not a universal conjecture with a binary truth value.

Section 6 already reports BP gains from short-cycle optimization. Figure 1(a) compares signature-weight-four dyadics at dimension five, including 288 versus 96 four-cycles. Figure 1(b) compares three `3 × 8` layouts at dimension three with girth four but different four-cycle counts. Figure 1(c) compares two constructions at dimension four and also minimizes eight-cycles within one construction. These are the authors' experiments, not ours. They do not provide a held-out, rank-matched comparison of cycle and absorbing-set predictors. Relevant source Sections 5–7 were read in full in the primary HTML; the earlier completeness investigation separately checked source definitions and appendix data.

## What to measure

Start with simple-cycle counts at each length and absorbing spectra by size, syndrome weight, elementary status and connectedness. Keep zero-syndrome/codeword supports separate. In regular degree-three ensembles, ACE at a fixed cycle length is constant: a length-`2k` cycle has ACE `k(3-2)=k`, so it supplies no extra within-length ranking. This follows directly from ACE's definition, not an experimental discovery. For irregular extensions, retain ACE/EMD distributions.

Compare out-of-sample prediction first. Then test whether optimizing the winning objective produces improvement on independent code populations and noise. The second question requires an intervention, not merely a regression. Use exact low-weight failure enumeration or importance sampling before making error-floor claims; ordinary Monte Carlo at moderate error fractions does not resolve rare-event asymptotics. For quantum CSS stages record both checks, their commutation, dimension and stabilizer-equivalent scoring. A raw count of `(a,0)` sets can mix benign stabilizers with harmful logical supports.

The [pilot protocol](PROTOCOL.md) fixes a modest classical single-permutation-block experiment. It addresses a concrete subcase of the source direction. It does not replace the source's CSS experiments or settle which objective is best for all P11 constructions. No mathematical novelty, first experiment, or universal predictor claim is made.
