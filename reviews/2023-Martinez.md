Meraiah Martinez · 2023 · Mathematics

## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorems 3.0.4, 3.1.1, 3.1.2: Dyadic matrices form a ring identified with the stated multivariate quotient; over F2 the square is zero or identity according to signature-row parity. The analogous n-adic family for n ≥ 3 is not generally a commutative multiplication-closed ring. (PDF pp. [31](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=31), [32](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=32), [33](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=33), [34](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=34), [35](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=35)).

- Theorems 4.0.1, 4.0.7, 5.1.3, 5.2.4–5.2.5: Even-weight dyadic check matrices produce dual-containing binary codes, with further conditions giving self-duality. Other results give permanent-based minimum-distance upper bounds and quasi-n-adic distance bounds. (PDF pp. [36](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=36), [38](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=38), [40](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=40), [50](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=50), [52](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=52), [60](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=60), [61](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=61)).

- Theorems 6.1.1, 6.1.3, 6.2.1, 7.0.4, 7.0.6: The thesis gives cycle/girth and stopping-distance statements, and describes how a base cycle’s permutation type determines lifted cycles; the 2-regular case has a specific generator-lift equivalence. (PDF pp. [65](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=65), [70](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=70), [72](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=72), [78](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=78), [79](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=79), [80](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=80), [81](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=81), [82](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=82)).

## Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

- Field characteristic, dyadic versus n-adic structure, block weights/dimensions and parity conditions are essential. The binary square/dual-containing conclusions cannot be transferred automatically to arbitrary n or arbitrary fields. (PDF pp. [31](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=31), [32](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=32), [35](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=35), [36](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=36), [40](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=40), [52](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=52), [60](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=60), [65](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=65)).

## Questions, conjectures and remaining work

- The graph-lift chapter proposes a more precise account of the general cycle-code relationship, including projected closed walks and cut-set dual codes, as future work. (PDF pp. [79](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=79)).

- Theorem 7.0.6: The 2-regular special-case result is proved; it must not be generalized to arbitrary graph lifts without further argument. (PDF pp. [81](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=81), [82](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=82)).

## Scope and interpretation notes

- Structural dual containment, distance upper bounds and decoding performance are different kinds of statements. A bound or a construction alone does not prove robust empirical decoding performance. (PDF pp. [36](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=36), [52](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=52), [60](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=60), [72](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1128&context=mathstudent#page=72)).
