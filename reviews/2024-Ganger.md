## Mathematical setup and definitions

- Projective spaces, spreads/maximal partial spreads, transversals, groupoid actions and geproci sets are the structural definitions. Geproci means that a general projection is a complete intersection. (PDF pp. [5](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=5), [6](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=6), [7](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=7), [8](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=8), [9](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=9), [10](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=10), [11](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=11), [12](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=12), [13](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=13)).

## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorem 2.2: The canonical spread in projective three-space has exactly two transversals for each finite base field, proved by separate characteristic-two and odd-characteristic arguments. (PDF pp. [14](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=14), [15](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=15), [16](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=16), [17](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=17), [18](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=18), [19](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=19)).

- Theorems 3.4 and 4.2: For the classified full/maximal partial spreads over F3, the associated groups are identified according to the existence of transversals and the type of spread. (PDF pp. [31](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=31), [32](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=32), [33](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=33)).

- Theorem 7.3: The characteristic-zero orbit construction is shown projectively equivalent to the previously known standard geproci construction, linking the two perspectives. (PDF pp. [43](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=43), [44](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=44), [45](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=45), [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=46), [47](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=47), [48](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=48), [49](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=49)).

## Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

- Finite-field extension constructions, the canonical spread, and the specific F3 classification hypotheses must be preserved. The characteristic-zero orbit argument uses selected roots of unity and prescribed fibers; it is not a theorem about every spread. (PDF pp. [14](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=14), [31](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=31), [33](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=33), [43](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=43), [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=dissunl#page=46)).
