Boolean Rank via Monomial Ideals and Neural Ideals

Juliann Marie Geraci · 2026 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Connects Boolean matrix factorization and biclique covers with monomial ideals, regularity, neural codes, and order-theoretic morphisms.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Boolean rank, isolated sets, isolation ideals, neural-code morphisms, intersection completion, and monomial rank supply the precise mathematical objects. (PDF pp. 13, 14, 24, 30, 65, 89)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 2.21: For a bipartite graph, regularity of its edge-ideal quotient is bounded above by Boolean rank of its adjacency matrix. The argument translates biclique decompositions into bounds on regularity. (PDF pp. 21, 22)
  • Theorem 2.41: The isolation-ideal quotient has regularity equal to the isolation number, also equal to one plus the isolation-complex dimension; this gives another Boolean-rank lower bound. (PDF pp. 30, 31, 32)
  • Theorems 3.52, 4.19, 4.22: The code theory gives a canonical-form description of intersection completion, maximal-rank results for intersection-complete codes, and a monomial-rank lower bound on Boolean rank. (PDF pp. 65, 66, 88, 89, 90, 91)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The first results concern finite Boolean matrices and their associated bipartite graphs; regularity is taken in explicitly defined graded polynomial-ring quotients. Neural-ring constructions use binary codes and their specified morphisms. These hypotheses cannot be replaced with arbitrary real matrices or arbitrary functions between codes. (PDF pp. 13, 20, 21, 30, 31, 40, 41)

Prior results used

  • §1.1; Proposition 2.42: The author explicitly distinguishes propositions recalled from literature from lemmas/theorems developed in collaboration. Stanley–Reisner theory and the Artinian top-degree characterization of regularity are essential dependencies. (PDF pp. 11, 12, 31)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Worked matrix/code examples illustrate exact algebraic constructions. They do not constitute a Lean, Coq, or Isabelle verification of the general results. (PDF pp. 30, 33, 36, 38)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Chapter 5: Questions 5.1 and 5.2 ask about gaps between Ferrers and biclique covering invariants and the largest canonical form of an intersection-complete code. Chapter 5 also proposes studying invariants of matrix representations of code morphisms. (PDF pp. 92, 93, 94)
  • §5.4: The proposed equality relating Boolean rank before and after reduction to a count of nonsimple redundancies is explicitly conjectural. (PDF pp. 94)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Question 5.1 includes a fixed-number-of-vertices parenthetical. Literal unboundedness on a fixed finite vertex set is impossible, so the intended asymptotic quantifiers need clarification before formalization. (PDF pp. 93)
  • A neural code is a discrete mathematical model; these results do not by themselves validate a biological model. (PDF pp. 3, 4)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize the finite equivalence between a Boolean factorization and a biclique cover before tackling regularity or neural ideals.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
