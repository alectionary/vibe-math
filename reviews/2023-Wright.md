## Mathematical setup and definitions

- Gordian distance is crossing-change distance. Complete Alexander neighbors are defined by realizing every Alexander polynomial among distance-one neighbors. Knot determinants and algebraic versus ordinary unknotting numbers must be distinguished. (PDF pp. [8](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=8), [9](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=9), [24](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=24), [25](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=25), [26](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=26)).

## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorem 1.1: A knot of unknotting number one with determinant greater than one, composite or congruent to one modulo four, is not a complete Alexander neighbor. (PDF pp. [8](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=8), [27](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=27), [28](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=28), [29](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=29), [30](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=30)).

- Theorem 1.2; Corollary 1.3: The thesis characterizes the breadth-two Alexander polynomials in the cited Kawauchi family via a nonsquare arithmetic condition, and derives exclusions from complete Alexander neighborhood. (PDF pp. [9](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=9), [31](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=31), [32](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=32), [33](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=33), [34](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=34)).

- Theorem 1.4; Chapter 4: Determinant/linking-form obstructions rule out unknotting number one for five explicitly named knots. (PDF pp. [9](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=9), [40](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=40), [41](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=41), [42](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=42), [43](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=43), [44](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=44), [45](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=45), [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=46), [47](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=47), [48](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=48)).

## Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

- The hypotheses distinguish ordinary unknotting number one from algebraic unknotting number one; breadth, determinant arithmetic and normalized Alexander-polynomial conventions restrict the results. (PDF pp. [8](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=8), [9](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=9), [24](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=24), [37](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=37), [38](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=38)).

## Questions, conjectures and remaining work

- Question 3.2 asks whether a complete Alexander neighbor can have nontrivial Alexander polynomial. Conjectures 3.8–3.12 concern extending polynomial realizability from ordinary to algebraic unknotting hypotheses and stronger breadth-two exclusions. (PDF pp. [26](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=26), [36](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=36), [37](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=37), [38](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=38)).

- Lemmas 3.11 and 3.13: Lemmas prove implications between these conjectures. A proved implication is not a proof of either conjecture’s premise. (PDF pp. [37](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=37), [38](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=38), [39](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1123&context=mathstudent#page=39)).

## Scope and interpretation notes

- The broad algebraic-unknotting extensions are labeled conjectural; do not substitute them for Theorem 1.1’s ordinary unknotting hypothesis.
