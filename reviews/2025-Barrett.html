A Zariski-Nagata Theorem for Smooth Toric Surfaces

Jordan Vincent Barrett · 2025 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Seeks a toric analogue of Zariski–Nagata: describing symbolic powers by vanishing-order conditions in Cox rings, with criteria for smooth toric surfaces.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Symbolic powers, point ideals, smooth fans, toric varieties, Cox rings and the generic-point ideal I(1) are the precise objects. (PDF pp. 19, 20, 21, 35, 36, 37, 40)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 5.2.5: The Generic Point Criterion reduces the toric symbolic-power equality for all subvarieties of a smooth toric surface to equality of ordinary and symbolic powers of its generic-point ideal. (PDF pp. 51, 52)
  • Theorem 6.0.2; Corollary 6.0.7: The Quadrant Criterion supplies sufficient smoothness conditions; a corollary establishes Toric Zariski–Nagata for smooth complete toric surfaces with at most five rays. (PDF pp. 54, 55, 56, 57, 58, 59)
  • Theorem 6.0.10: Normality of the relevant generic-point quotient is a sufficient condition for the symbolic-power equality in the stated smooth complete surface setting. (PDF pp. 61)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The setting uses the specified complex toric/Cox-ring construction, smoothness, and, where required, completeness. Fan/ray constraints and normality are substantive hypotheses, not conclusions automatically true for every toric variety. (PDF pp. 35, 36, 37, 51, 54, 61)

Prior results used

  • Classical affine/projective Zariski–Nagata, fan classification, and results on normal toric rings/strong F-regular type are imported dependencies. (PDF pp. 21, 27, 35, 57, 61)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • The final examples include Macaulay2-style calculations for particular fans/ideals. These illustrate cases; the general results have conventional proofs, not proof-assistant certificates. (PDF pp. 62, 63, 64)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The closing discussion leaves universality across toric surfaces unresolved, and suggests further normality criteria, higher-dimensional analogues, and behavior under star subdivision. (PDF pp. 64)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • The title is broader than the strongest unconditional family proved: at-most-five-ray complete surfaces and other surfaces meeting sufficient criteria. Do not infer a theorem for every smooth toric surface. (PDF pp. 57, 61, 64)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: a low-dimensional fan and explicit generic-point ideal example before any general symbolic-power theorem.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
