# Making Sandwiches: A Novel Invariant in D-module Theory
David Lieberman · 2024 · Mathematics

[Source record](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/mathstudent/124/) · [Original PDF](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent)

## Mathematical setup and definitions

- Differential-operator rings, filtered-module dimension, Bernstein’s inequality, linear simplicity and sandwich Bernstein–Sato polynomials are explicitly defined. Sandwich equations use composition on both sides of multiplication by a power of f. ## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorem 4.7: A finite-dimensional linearly simple filtered algebra satisfies the stated Bernstein-type lower bound on dimension of a nonzero filtered module. (PDF pp. [57](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=57), [58](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=58)).

- Theorems 4.17 and 4.19: Veronese differential-operator rings have a quantified linear-simplicity result for the specified filtration. (PDF pp. [69](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=69), [70](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=70), [71](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=71), [72](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=72)).

- Theorems 6.17 and 6.24: A suitable sandwich polynomial with no nonnegative integer roots implies linear simplicity under the singular-locus/localization hypotheses. The Segre-ring application proves Bernstein’s inequality for the stated filtered modules and existence of ordinary Bernstein–Sato polynomials for all its elements. (PDF pp. [99](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=99), [100](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=100), [101](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=101), [108](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=108), [109](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=109)).

- Questions 3.27/4.5 ask about the general scope of Bernstein’s inequality; Question 6.2 asks for sandwich functional equations. (PDF pp. [108](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=108), [109](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=109)).

## Scope and interpretation notes

- Ordinary and sandwich Bernstein–Sato polynomials are distinct invariants. Existence of one does not automatically establish the precise other polynomial, and a conditional theorem cannot be applied before its root/localization hypotheses are checked. (PDF pp. [77](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=77), [78](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=78), [99](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=99), [108](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1130&context=mathstudent#page=108)).
