# Approximation via Degree Reduction of Nonlinearities with Applications to Turbulent Flows, Flame Fronts, and Magnetohydrodynamics
Matthew Enlow · 2024 · Mathematics

[Source record](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/dissunl/106/) · [Original PDF](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl)

Studies modified (“calmed”) dissipative PDEs, proving regularity/well-posedness and conditional convergence back to original fluid, flame-front and MHD models.

## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorems 1.2.3–1.2.7 and Chapter 3: The calmed Navier–Stokes systems admit the specified global solution/regularity results. Convergence to an original Navier–Stokes solution is proved on an interval where that sufficiently regular original solution exists. (PDF pp. [15](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=15), [16](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=16), [42](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=42), [43](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=43), [44](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=44), [45](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=45), [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=46), [47](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=47), [48](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=48), [49](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=49)).

- Theorems 1.2.8 and 1.2.10: For the rotational calmed system, an energy identity and global attractor follow under additional structural and forcing assumptions. (PDF pp. [17](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=17), [18](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=18), [62](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=62), [63](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=63), [64](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=64), [65](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=65)).

- Theorems 1.2.18–1.2.28: Parallel global calmed-system results and short-time/conditional convergence results are obtained for the 2D MHD–Boussinesq model with calmed Ohmic heating and the calmed Kuramoto–Sivashinsky systems. (PDF pp. [20](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=20), [21](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=21), [23](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=23), [24](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=24)).

## Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

- Theorems distinguish initial-data spaces, forcing regularity, domain/boundary conditions, fixed calming parameter, and which of the calming-function conditions apply. Convergence assumes an original solution with stated regularity on the comparison interval. ## Scope and interpretation notes

- Global well-posedness of a modified equation does not solve global well-posedness of the original equation. Statements about regular original solutions remain conditional. (PDF pp. [16](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=16), [21](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=21), [23](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=23), [24](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=24), [84](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=84)).

- Theorem 1.2.5’s uniqueness wording and later discussion of weak uniqueness need careful class-by-class reconciliation; this review does not treat the thesis as proving uniqueness for every weak-solution notion mentioned. (PDF pp. [15](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=15), [42](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=42), [66](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=66), [83](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=dissunl#page=83)).
