Online Prediction of Streaming Data

Aleena Chanda · 2025 · Statistics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Develops hash/sketch-based and Gaussian-process streaming predictors. It combines Bayesian derivations, conditional asymptotic results, and empirical comparisons for difficult prediction settings.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Hash-based predictors, estimated empirical distribution functions, Gaussian-process priors with random bias, Shtarkov predictors and cumulative absolute loss are precisely formulated methods/criteria. (PDF pp. 3, 28, 46, 68, 70, 73, 82)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 1–2: Posterior predictive Student-t distributions are derived for two specified Gaussian-process/random-bias setups. (PDF pp. 28, 29, 30, 31, 46)
  • Theorems 3–6: The sketch chapter presents collision/count bounds and empirical-distribution approximation/convergence results. Theorem 6 explicitly assumes IID observations and an appropriate growth regime for sketch parameters. (PDF pp. 68, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74)
  • Theorems 7–8: The Shtarkov comparison includes asymptotic statements under regularity hypotheses such as Fisher-information control and uniform limit assumptions; those hypotheses are stated rather than established for every data stream. (PDF pp. 85, 86, 87)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Bayesian posterior formulas are conditional on the adopted likelihood/prior and covariance choices. Consistency uses IID sampling and specified sketch growth. M-open motivation does not eliminate those assumptions from the theorems. (PDF pp. 3, 28, 46, 73, 85, 86)

Prior results used

  • Count-Min sketch bounds, Glivenko–Cantelli and prior Shtarkov/Bayesian theory are inputs or adapted results. (PDF pp. 68, 69, 73, 85)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Reported predictive rankings are comparisons on chosen data under cumulative absolute error and sensitivity experiments. They are not a universal optimality theorem for model-free or nonstationary streams. (PDF pp. 3, 146, 147, 148)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The thesis proposes turnstile-stream extensions, multidimensional prediction, broader empirical data classes, and possible recommender-system connections. It also identifies conceptual uncertainty about typical M-open data and bias–variance reasoning in that setting. (PDF pp. 16, 69, 138, 149, 150)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • The M-open discussion and IID consistency theorem have different scopes; do not advertise the latter as a theorem about arbitrary M-open streams. “Formalized” in the regret discussion means mathematically defined, not machine checked. (PDF pp. 3, 73, 82)
  • The displayed probability inequalities in Theorems 3–4 warrant checking against their proofs before direct transcription into a formal system; this review does not certify their exact typography. (PDF pp. 68, 69)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: a finite Count-Min collision-probability bound, then a separately scoped IID convergence statement.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
