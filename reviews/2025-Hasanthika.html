Multivariate Mixture Regression Models with Known Group Membership and Informative Priors

Pahalapathirage Dona Kalani Hasanthika · 2025 · Statistics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Builds hierarchical multivariate mixture-regression methods for Gaussian and count responses with known group labels and informative priors, using algebraic derivations and empirical validation.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Likelihoods, latent/indicator variables, known group membership, regression coefficients, hierarchical priors and Gaussian/Poisson-overdispersion response models are explicitly specified. (PDF pp. 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 107, 108, 109, 188, 189, 263, 264)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • §2.5.2 and earlier model derivations: The text derives likelihood/conditional-expectation and parameter-update formulas for successive mixture models. These are conventional conditional algebraic derivations, not theorem-labeled distribution-free guarantees. (PDF pp. 24, 25, 26, 114, 115, 116, 117, 118)
  • Appendices A–E; Chapter 4: Method-of-moments and robust-regression derivations, plus expanded derivations for the proposed Gaussian and count models, are placed in Appendices A–E. The summary explains the hybrid Bayesian-EM and moment/Newton estimation steps. (PDF pp. 324, 325, 328, 340, 353, 356, 363, 405, 412)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Known group information, a finite mixture/model structure, the chosen conditional Gaussian or overdispersed-count laws, design matrices and hierarchical priors are modeling assumptions. Distributional and independence/factorization assumptions belong to each likelihood specification. (PDF pp. 22, 24, 107, 108, 109, 188, 189, 263, 264)
  • Moment-based approximation and numerical root-finding are estimation choices; convergence or calibration across all possible data distributions is not implied by writing down the update equations. (PDF pp. 324, 325)

Prior results used

  • EM, Bayes’ rule, generalized mixed-model methods and empirical-Bayes variance moderation draw on prior literature; limma/edgeR/DESeq2 are benchmark comparators. (PDF pp. 23, 26, 324)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Simulations and an application evaluate sensitivity, ROC behavior and false-discovery rates. Apparent FDR control in selected settings is empirical, not a proven universal frequentist FDR guarantee. (PDF pp. 165, 166, 174, 175, 176, 184, 185, 186, 311, 312, 319, 320, 321, 322)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The count-model discussion identifies anomalous n = 100 two-group results requiring diagnosis, further real-data validation, and extensions to time-course, single-cell and multi-omic applications. (PDF pp. 322, 323)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • The known group-label variable and inferred discriminating-feature indicators have different roles; do not call all latent quantities observed. High sensitivity and low FDR are different goals, and the author explicitly reports trade-offs. (PDF pp. 188, 322)
  • No theorem/proof-assistant inventory can be inferred from the absence of numbered theorem headings: this dissertation contains extensive mathematical derivations and proof appendices. (PDF pp. 8, 9, 324)
