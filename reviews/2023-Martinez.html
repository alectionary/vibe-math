On Dyadic Parity Check Codes and Their Generalizations

Meraiah Martinez · 2023 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Studies algebraic parity-check structure and graph invariants of dyadic, n-adic and quasi-dyadic error-correcting codes, including graph lifts and possible quantum applications.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Dyadic/n-adic matrices, signature rows, quasi-dyadic block arrays, codes over a quotient polynomial ring, Tanner girth, stopping sets and graph lifts are defined mathematical objects. (PDF pp. 2, 28, 32, 35, 50, 65, 72, 79)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 3.0.4, 3.1.1, 3.1.2: Dyadic matrices form a ring identified with the stated multivariate quotient; over F2 the square is zero or identity according to signature-row parity. The analogous n-adic family for n ≥ 3 is not generally a commutative multiplication-closed ring. (PDF pp. 31, 32, 33, 34, 35)
  • Theorems 4.0.1, 4.0.7, 5.1.3, 5.2.4–5.2.5: Even-weight dyadic check matrices produce dual-containing binary codes, with further conditions giving self-duality. Other results give permanent-based minimum-distance upper bounds and quasi-n-adic distance bounds. (PDF pp. 36, 38, 40, 50, 52, 60, 61)
  • Theorems 6.1.1, 6.1.3, 6.2.1, 7.0.4, 7.0.6: The thesis gives cycle/girth and stopping-distance statements, and describes how a base cycle’s permutation type determines lifted cycles; the 2-regular case has a specific generator-lift equivalence. (PDF pp. 65, 70, 72, 78, 79, 80, 81, 82)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Field characteristic, dyadic versus n-adic structure, block weights/dimensions and parity conditions are essential. The binary square/dual-containing conclusions cannot be transferred automatically to arbitrary n or arbitrary fields. (PDF pp. 31, 32, 35, 36, 40, 52, 60, 65)

Prior results used

  • Classical coding theory, duality, earlier quasi-cyclic permanent bounds and graph-cover/permutation theory supply prior mathematics. (PDF pp. 2, 50, 52, 79)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Exact matrix and graph constructions illustrate the theory. Potential quantum-code usefulness and practical representational advantages are application directions, not universal performance theorems. (PDF pp. 2, 36, 40, 79)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The graph-lift chapter proposes a more precise account of the general cycle-code relationship, including projected closed walks and cut-set dual codes, as future work. (PDF pp. 79)
  • Theorem 7.0.6: The 2-regular special-case result is proved; it must not be generalized to arbitrary graph lifts without further argument. (PDF pp. 81, 82)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Structural dual containment, distance upper bounds and decoding performance are different kinds of statements. A bound or a construction alone does not prove robust empirical decoding performance. (PDF pp. 36, 52, 60, 72)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: prove the finite F2 dyadic-square identity and derive dual containment; it has fewer dependencies than the infinite/analytic results elsewhere in the collection.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
