Operator Theoretic Methods for Coupled PDE in General Geometries

Yuhao Mu · 2026 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Develops pressure elimination and operator representations for coupled fluid–elasticity PDEs on bounded Lipschitz geometries, with analytical well-posedness and finite-element applications.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • The model specifies fluid/solid domains, transmission conditions, an energy Hilbert space, unbounded generators and their domains, and a locally strong solution class. (PDF pp. 16, 17, 18, 32, 33, 34, 35, 103, 104)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 3; §2.4: The linear Stokes–Lamé generator produces a contraction C0 semigroup in the stated energy space on bounded Lipschitz domain pairs. Dissipativity and resolvent maximality drive the proof. (PDF pp. 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42, 43)
  • Theorems 9, 11, 15, 17, 22, 24: The nonlinear resolvent system is solvable as stated; truncated systems admit nonlinear semigroups. For the untruncated system, local strong existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence are proved in the specified solution class. (PDF pp. 75, 76, 82, 83, 104, 105, 106, 112, 113, 117, 118)
  • Chapter 4: Chapter 4 establishes finite-element error/convergence analysis using the mixed formulation and a discrete inf-sup condition. (PDF pp. 126, 127, 128, 129, 130, 131, 132, 133)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Bounded Lipschitz geometry does not remove the domain/interface hypotheses, constitutive parameters, function-space conditions, or prescribed boundary conditions. Local nonlinear strong solutions require data in the generator domain. FEM bounds require the specified meshes, spaces, and stability conditions. (PDF pp. 16, 17, 18, 36, 104, 129)

Prior results used

  • de Rham pressure recovery, Lumer–Phillips, Babuška–Brezzi, and nonlinear variational/semigroup theorems are imported analytic tools. (PDF pp. 27, 47, 82, 106)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Introduction; Chapters 5–6: Chapters 5–6 implement numerical experiments. The introduction explicitly separates those experiments from the justification of the analytical results. (PDF pp. 14)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The introduction presents global well-posedness of the full untruncated nonlinear system in general geometry as a larger unresolved goal, including the 2D and small-data 3D directions. (PDF pp. 14, 15)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Global well-posedness for a truncated equation and local well-posedness for the full equation are different claims. Neither establishes unrestricted global Navier–Stokes/fluid–structure regularity. (PDF pp. 13, 14, 15, 104)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: an abstract contraction-semigroup energy estimate under explicit hypotheses; full pressure elimination requires a large functional-analysis library.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
