Prefix-rewriting: The Falsification by Fellow Traveler Property and Practical Computation

Ash DeClerk · 2023 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Relates geometric word-problem properties to autostackable structures and proves correctness/computability results for prefix-constrained Knuth–Bendix completion.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Convergent prefix-rewriting systems, synchronously regular languages, autostackability/geodesic autostackability, compatible well-founded orderings, critical pairs and fair derivations are explicitly defined. (PDF pp. 17, 18, 48, 49, 50, 51, 52, 55)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 2.3.1–2.3.3 and 2.4.2: A weight-nonincreasing synchronously regular convergent prefix-rewriting system yields an autostackable structure. A group/generating-set pair with the falsification-by-fellow-traveler property is geodesically autostackable. (PDF pp. 21, 22, 23, 24, 30, 32, 34, 35)
  • Theorems 3.3.5 and 3.3.7: The inference rules preserve the presented equivalence relation; a finite fair derivation yields a bounded convergent prefix-rewriting system. Termination of rewriting and local confluence are proved under the defined conditions. (PDF pp. 53, 54, 55, 56, 57)
  • Theorems 3.4.3, 3.4.8, 3.4.9: Specified bounded regular orderings ensure computability of inference steps and regularity of languages; regular-split shortlex constructions provide examples. (PDF pp. 60, 61, 62, 64, 65, 66, 67)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Finite alphabets/presentations and well-founded compatible orders matter. “Fair” includes no remaining equations at the end and a complete critical-pair treatment. Correctness of a completed derivation does not prove the completion search terminates on all inputs. (PDF pp. 48, 50, 52, 55, 60)

Prior results used

  • Regular-language closure, rewriting/confluence theory and earlier relationships among automatic/geometric structures provide dependencies. (PDF pp. 21, 32, 50, 57)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • A Python implementation finds an autostackable structure for a Coxeter-group example; Appendix A lists the rules. This is an implemented proof-search/completion procedure whose correctness is conventionally proved, not evidence of a Lean-style kernel certificate. (PDF pp. 71, 72, 73)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The abstract notes that FFTP groups were not known to be automatic or to admit finite convergent rewriting systems. The thesis establishes autostackability, not those stronger properties. (PDF pp. 2)
  • No dedicated additional open-conjecture list was identified. Guaranteed termination/success on every group presentation is not established by the conditional completion theorem. (PDF pp. 55, 71)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • “A computer can prove convergence” here refers to the completion procedure together with mathematical correctness arguments. It should not be conflated with proof-assistant formalization. (PDF pp. 10, 55, 71)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: mechanize one inference rule’s soundness, then the finite fair-derivation correctness theorem with termination hypotheses explicit.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
