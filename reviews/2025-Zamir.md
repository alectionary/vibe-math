## Results proved or derived in the dissertation

- Theorem 5.1: General simple points impose the expected number of conditions in every degree over an infinite field. (PDF pp. [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=46), [47](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=47), [48](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=48)).

- Theorems 7.1 and 7.4: General double points in P(1,2,3) have expected Hilbert function in every degree, with no exceptions, under the stated characteristic exclusions. A weighted Terracini induction is a key ingredient. (PDF pp. [58](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=58), [59](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=59), [60](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=60), [61](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=61), [62](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=62)).

- Theorems 8.16 and 8.20: For P(1,b,c), a sufficiently high degree gives expected double-point interpolation; the stated bound is d ≥ 10c, with a stronger alternative under an additional numerical condition. The thesis also characterizes P(1,2,3) as the exception-free plane in its family. (PDF pp. [89](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=89), [90](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=90), [91](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=91), [92](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=92), [93](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=93), [94](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=94)).

## Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

- “General” refers to the algebraic-geometric parameter-space condition, not an arbitrary chosen point set. Weights and field conditions matter: Theorem 5.1 assumes an infinite field and Theorem 7.1 excludes characteristics 2, 3, and 5. Later statements inherit their chapter setup. (PDF pp. [10](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=10), [46](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=46), [58](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=58), [60](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=60), [89](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=89)).

## Questions, conjectures and remaining work

- Chapter 9: The final chapter lists explicit questions about generators of general-point ideals in dimension at least three, boundedness of the defect D(X,d), sharper/extended weighted interpolation statements, and associated Veronese/secant geometry. (PDF pp. [95](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=95), [96](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1386&context=dissunl#page=96)).

## Scope and interpretation notes

- The no-exceptions theorem is not asserted for all weighted planes; much of the dissertation analyzes exceptions and high-degree thresholds.
