Extremal Problems in Graph Saturation and Covering

Adam Volk · 2022 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Investigates extremal subgraph counts in saturated or covered graphs, characterizes tree-saturating spiders, and maximizes independent sets for selected covering constraints.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Generalized saturation counts, star/clique/path graphs, graph covering, tree-saturating trees/spiders and counts of fixed-size independent sets define several distinct optimization problems. (PDF pp. 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 78, 96, 104, 106)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 3–5 and 9: The thesis proves an asymptotic cherry-count result for clique-saturated graphs, exact optimization over a candidate family for stars in star-saturated graphs, and existence bounds for multipartite saturated constructions. (PDF pp. 13, 14, 23, 24, 32, 33, 36, 43, 44)
  • Theorem 15; Proposition 25: A spider is tree-saturating exactly in the classified types. Necessary conditions for more general tree-saturating trees are not the same as a full classification. (PDF pp. 78, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83, 84, 85)
  • Theorems 6–7: For path-covered and matching-covered graphs, fixed-size independent-set counts are bounded by specified extremal candidate families. (PDF pp. 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The graph is finite/simple under the dissertation’s conventions; star parameters, n thresholds, forbidden subgraph, covering type and independent-set size vary by result. An asymptotic estimate is distinct from an exact value at every n. (PDF pp. 16, 17, 18, 23, 32, 43, 104, 106)

Prior results used

  • Previously known clique-saturation extremal graphs, graph-coloring/regular-subgraph tools, and independent-set extremal theorems are recalled with attributions. Results by Chakraborti–Loh, Alon and others should not all be counted as new. (PDF pp. 12, 22, 61, 62, 96, 99)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Explicit graph constructions, extremal counterexamples and finite parameter calculations accompany counting proofs. No machine-checked proof artifact was identified. (PDF pp. 23, 32, 43, 47, 62)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Conjecture 1; Questions 1–6; Chapter 5: Conjecture 1 proposes a threshold separating the optimal matching-covered configurations. Questions ask about exact cherry/path minimizers, optimization of construction bounds, weak-saturation variants and a full classification of tree-saturating trees. (PDF pp. 47, 68, 112, 113, 114, 115)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Theorem 15 classifies spiders, not all trees. Some asymptotic subgraph-count results and all finite-n optimization questions remain separate from that classification. (PDF pp. 78, 85, 113, 114)
  • Future-work questions sometimes restate a broader problem after a restricted case was answered earlier; retain their parameter restrictions when labeling them unresolved. (PDF pp. 36, 40, 41, 113)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize the star-count identity as a sum of degree binomial coefficients, or check one finite extremal construction before the asymptotic theory.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
