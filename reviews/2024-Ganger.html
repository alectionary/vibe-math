Spreads and Transversals and Their Connection to Geproci Sets

Allison Joan Ganger · 2024 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Connects finite-projective-space spreads and their transversals with associated groupoids/groups and geproci point sets, then relates finite-characteristic constructions to characteristic zero.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Projective spaces, spreads/maximal partial spreads, transversals, groupoid actions and geproci sets are the structural definitions. Geproci means that a general projection is a complete intersection. (PDF pp. 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 2.2: The canonical spread in projective three-space has exactly two transversals for each finite base field, proved by separate characteristic-two and odd-characteristic arguments. (PDF pp. 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19)
  • Theorems 3.4 and 4.2: For the classified full/maximal partial spreads over F3, the associated groups are identified according to the existence of transversals and the type of spread. (PDF pp. 31, 32, 33)
  • Theorem 7.3: The characteristic-zero orbit construction is shown projectively equivalent to the previously known standard geproci construction, linking the two perspectives. (PDF pp. 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Finite-field extension constructions, the canonical spread, and the specific F3 classification hypotheses must be preserved. The characteristic-zero orbit argument uses selected roots of unity and prescribed fibers; it is not a theorem about every spread. (PDF pp. 14, 31, 33, 43, 46)

Prior results used

  • Projective geometry, finite-field theory, prior classifications of spreads, and the earlier standard geproci construction/root-system examples are dependencies or comparison targets. (PDF pp. 7, 31, 33, 46, 49)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Appendix A: Appendix A supplies Python code for explicit groupoid/group computations, including higher-dimensional examples. Exact finite computations and code are useful evidence but are not a kernel-checked formalization of the whole theory. (PDF pp. 42, 50, 51, 52)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Remark 6.4: Remark 6.4 asks whether the identified group relationship for the canonical spread extends to higher dimensions, motivated by a computed order-seven example. (PDF pp. 42)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Do not generalize the two-transversal statement from the canonical spread to all spreads; the dissertation explicitly studies spreads without transversals as well. (PDF pp. 2, 14, 31)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: verify one finite-field spread/group action by exhaustive finite reasoning, then prove the small characteristic case.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
