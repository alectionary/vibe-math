Cohomological Support Varieties Along Ring Maps

Ryan Watson · 2026 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Studies the behavior of cohomological support varieties under restriction and extension of scalars along local ring maps, and develops a computational construction.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Differential graded algebras/modules, filtered complexes, Koszul complexes, cohomological operators, and support varieties are the framework. (PDF pp. 12, 13, 14, 34, 35, 36, 37)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 1.0.1 and 3.3.9: Support varieties of suitable modules transform by inverse image under the map induced by a local homomorphism. The proof factors the map and uses the weakly regular and surjective cases. (PDF pp. 10, 50, 51, 52, 53, 54, 55)
  • Theorem 3.4.4 and corollaries: A local map of finite flat dimension gives the stated dimension inequality for supports after derived extension of scalars; complete-intersection descent/localization consequences follow. (PDF pp. 57, 58, 59, 60, 61, 62)
  • Chapters 4–5: Chapter 4 develops the resolution/construction needed to compute supports, extending Eisenbud–Shamash methods; Chapter 5 recasts results in derived-category language. (PDF pp. 11, 63, 64, 65, 66, 93, 94)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Rings are commutative Noetherian and local in the principal comparison results. The affine-variety theorem assumes algebraically closed residue fields and finite generation over both rings. The dimension inequality additionally assumes finite flat dimension; the weakly regular base-change theorem uses its own flatness hypotheses. (PDF pp. 10, 34, 37, 38, 54, 60)

Prior results used

  • Avramov/Pollitz support theory, Cohen/regular factorizations, and classical Eisenbud–Shamash resolution constructions are prior mathematics, not new claims of this dissertation. (PDF pp. 3, 11, 34, 35)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • The introduction describes implementation in the Macaulay2 package ThickSubcategories as under development. This is computer algebra, with neither an independently rerun package nor a proof-assistant certificate established here. (PDF pp. 3, 11)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Remark 3.2.5: Remark 3.2.5 suggests a nonflat generalization using derived tensor products; this is a possible extension, not a theorem proved in the stated nonflat generality. (PDF pp. 40, 41)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Theorems in Chapter 5 restate earlier results at another level of generality; do not count each restatement as a separate discovery. References to broader conjectures in the introduction do not mean those conjectures are solved. (PDF pp. 10, 93, 94)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: start with a finite Koszul-complex calculation or an explicit example of extension/restriction of scalars; the full result requires substantial derived/homological algebra.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
