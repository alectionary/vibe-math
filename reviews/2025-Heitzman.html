On Kernels and Antiderivatives of Nonlocal Derivatives

Alex John Heitzman · 2025 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Analyzes integral nonlocal derivatives, constructs antiderivatives from discrete approximations, and proves maximum-principle/kernel results for specified weights.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • D_k f(x) is the integral of [f(x) − f(z)]k(x,z). The weight function is distinguished from the operator’s nullspace. Piecewise-vanishing oscillation and weak nonlocal derivatives supply solution frameworks. (PDF pp. 6, 8, 9, 89, 90)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Chapter 2; Theorem 15: The antiderivative results construct solutions to D_k f = g through compactness/convergence of approximations, with uniqueness and almost-uniform/Lp convergence in the stated settings. (PDF pp. 8, 49, 50, 51, 52)
  • Theorems 24–25: A singular-weight result gives a weak antiderivative for the one-sided 1/(t−s) weight when the forcing is Hölder continuous and vanishes at the initial point; weak derivatives are unique almost everywhere under their hypotheses. (PDF pp. 89, 90, 91)
  • Chapter 3; Theorem 35 and discussion: The maximum-principle arguments constrain subsolutions and nullspaces using positivity, propagation/fattening, and integrability conditions. (PDF pp. 110, 111)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Results have different hypotheses: nonnegative/one-sided weights, positive and finite weight integrals, regularity/PVO assumptions, bounds by an auxiliary function, and normalization at zero. Singular weights require a separate weak formulation. Maximum principles use further connectivity/propagation and measure assumptions. (PDF pp. 16, 17, 51, 89, 90, 110, 111)

Prior results used

  • The work builds on compactness ideas related to Arzelà–Ascoli, maximum principles, and previous nonlocal Poincaré/analysis results; these inputs are not additional new theorems merely because they appear in exposition. (PDF pp. 8, 9)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • The discrete approximations are part of an existence/convergence proof. An approximation algorithm is not, by itself, a machine-checked proof. (PDF pp. 8, 51, 65, 66)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • No dedicated unresolved-conjecture section was identified. The dissertation develops several solution regimes; conditions not covered by those theorems should be recorded as outside scope rather than declared open problems. (PDF pp. 5, 6, 9, 111)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • “Kernel” can refer to an integral weight or an operator nullspace; the author deliberately calls the former a weight function. A uniqueness theorem cannot be detached from its initial normalization or solution class. (PDF pp. 6, 50, 51)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: prove that constant functions lie in the nullspace when the defining integral exists, followed by a finite weighted-graph maximum principle.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
