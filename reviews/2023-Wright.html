Gordian Distance and Complete Alexander Neighbors

Ana Wright · 2023 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Uses Alexander polynomials and linking-form obstructions to constrain which knots can be complete Alexander neighbors and to obstruct unknotting number one.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Gordian distance is crossing-change distance. Complete Alexander neighbors are defined by realizing every Alexander polynomial among distance-one neighbors. Knot determinants and algebraic versus ordinary unknotting numbers must be distinguished. (PDF pp. 8, 9, 24, 25, 26)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 1.1: A knot of unknotting number one with determinant greater than one, composite or congruent to one modulo four, is not a complete Alexander neighbor. (PDF pp. 8, 27, 28, 29, 30)
  • Theorem 1.2; Corollary 1.3: The thesis characterizes the breadth-two Alexander polynomials in the cited Kawauchi family via a nonsquare arithmetic condition, and derives exclusions from complete Alexander neighborhood. (PDF pp. 9, 31, 32, 33, 34)
  • Theorem 1.4; Chapter 4: Determinant/linking-form obstructions rule out unknotting number one for five explicitly named knots. (PDF pp. 9, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The hypotheses distinguish ordinary unknotting number one from algebraic unknotting number one; breadth, determinant arithmetic and normalized Alexander-polynomial conventions restrict the results. (PDF pp. 8, 9, 24, 37, 38)

Prior results used

  • Reidemeister theory, Kawauchi’s polynomial/Gordian-distance results, surgery/Alexander-matrix descriptions, and linking-form obstructions supply prior inputs. (PDF pp. 13, 26, 33, 35, 36)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Finite knot-table exclusions and arithmetic tests complement the theoretical obstructions. Reported counts and KnotInfo unknown-status statements are historical to the dissertation, not verified current database counts. (PDF pp. 9, 34, 35)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Question 3.2 asks whether a complete Alexander neighbor can have nontrivial Alexander polynomial. Conjectures 3.8–3.12 concern extending polynomial realizability from ordinary to algebraic unknotting hypotheses and stronger breadth-two exclusions. (PDF pp. 26, 36, 37, 38)
  • Lemmas 3.11 and 3.13: Lemmas prove implications between these conjectures. A proved implication is not a proof of either conjecture’s premise. (PDF pp. 37, 38, 39)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • The broad algebraic-unknotting extensions are labeled conjectural; do not substitute them for Theorem 1.1’s ordinary unknotting hypothesis. (PDF pp. 8, 37, 38)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize one determinant/congruence obstruction as a conditional arithmetic lemma; full knot-topology formalization is a larger dependency project.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
