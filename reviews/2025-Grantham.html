Changepoint Detection as Model Selection: A General Framework

Michael A. Grantham · 2025 · Statistics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Presents Iteratively Reweighted Fused Lasso (IRFL) as a flexible changepoint method, with optimization formulations, classical dynamic-programming proofs, derivations, and empirical evidence.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Segment cost functions, penalized changepoint objectives, generalized/fused lasso, adaptive weights, BIC selection and a recovery-distance metric define the framework. (PDF pp. 43, 44, 75, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80, 81)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Propositions 1–4: The background algorithm chapter proves the optimal-partition recursion, the PELT pruning implication under a cost inequality, and CPOP/segment-neighborhood recursions. These support the described classical algorithms. (PDF pp. 44, 45, 46, 47, 49, 50, 51, 52)
  • Algorithm 1; §4.3; Appendix A.6: The new method is specified by weighted generalized-lasso subproblems, BIC-based selection, and an update/stopping rule. Appendix A supplies further optimization/criterion derivations; a method definition is not itself a convergence theorem. (PDF pp. 77, 78, 79, 80, 81, 211, 212)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • A design matrix, contrast matrix, stabilizing epsilon, tuning/selection criterion, and stopping tolerance are chosen. Segment models and simulation scenarios impose their own noise, trend, seasonality and autocorrelation structure; PELT correctness uses its stated cost inequality. (PDF pp. 44, 46, 76, 77, 79, 154)

Prior results used

  • Optimal partitioning, PELT, CPOP, generalized lasso and adaptive/reweighted lasso are prior methods; the cited pruning/recursion results should not be credited as novel IRFL guarantees. (PDF pp. 43, 45, 46, 47, 49, 81)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • IRFL’s recovery/performance claims are supported principally by simulation scenarios and applications to CO2 data and images. They are empirical findings under those designs. (PDF pp. 76, 150, 151, 152, 153, 154)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • No numbered new-method open-conjecture list was identified. A proof of general IRFL iterate convergence, exact global ℓ0 recovery, or universal support consistency was not identified; these are review-identified proof obligations, not claimed author-stated open problems. (PDF pp. 79, 80, 81, 151)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Equation (4.5) is presented as an approximation/heuristic after a stationarity argument. It is not a demonstrated global minimizer guarantee. Classical exact dynamic-programming guarantees should not be transferred to IRFL. (PDF pp. 43, 75, 76, 80, 81)
  • Alignment of estimated changepoints with historical events is an application-level interpretation, not a causal mathematical proof. (PDF pp. 3, 152, 153, 154)
