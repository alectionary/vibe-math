Analysis of Graph-based Decoders for Quantum Low Density Parity Check Codes

Kirsten Morris · 2025 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Analyzes combinatorial causes of failure in graph-based quantum LDPC decoding and the effect of redundant checks in CSS-code constructions.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • CSS/stabilizer codes, Tanner graphs, absorbing/trapping/failure-inducing sets and Gallager-style syndrome decoding are mathematical and algorithmic definitions. (PDF pp. 3, 33, 44, 45, 54, 73)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 3.2.1–3.2.2, 3.2.6–3.2.7: Absorbing sets with odd-degree checks induce failure for the decoder being analyzed. All-even-check cases require an additional rowspace/stabilizer distinction; acyclic and cyclic structures give further trapping results. (PDF pp. 44, 45, 46, 48, 50)
  • Theorems 4.1.2, 4.1.6 and §4.2: Symmetric stabilizers are absorbing sets, and several partition/path/tree configurations yield failure-inducing sets under explicit neighborhood conditions. (PDF pp. 54, 56, 58, 60, 62, 64, 65)
  • Theorems 5.0.1 and 5.1.4: For the specified maximally redundant check construction, the decoder returns a syndrome-matching error pattern within two iterations. Syndrome agreement is not necessarily recovery of the actual error or logical state. (PDF pp. 73, 74, 79, 80)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Failure results depend on a particular update rule, initialization, graph structure, error pattern, and, in the quantum setting, rowspace/stabilizer equivalence. A statement about this decoder is not universal over quantum decoding algorithms. (PDF pp. 44, 45, 56, 58, 73, 79)

Prior results used

  • Theorem 2.2.11: Classical LDPC and quantum stabilizer/CSS constructions are prior theory. The hypergraph-product theorem in the background chapter is explicitly cited. (PDF pp. 3, 33)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Hamming-derived CSS experiments support improved decoding in the tested constructions; performance plots do not prove improved error correction for every code/noise model. (PDF pp. 3, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83, 84)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The conclusion identifies failure-inducing sets outside the absorbing-set class and relaxation of the strong graphical/two-iteration convergence condition as further work. (PDF pp. 85)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • “Decodes within two iterations” must be read alongside the paper’s distinction between matched syndrome and correct estimated error. This distinction is explicit in Theorem 5.0.1. (PDF pp. 73, 79)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize a finite Tanner graph, the exact update rule, and one absorbing-set failure theorem.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
