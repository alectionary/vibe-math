Asteroidal Sets and Dominating Targets in Graphs

Oleksiy Al-saadi · 2024 · Computing

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Analyzes how asteroidal sets constrain dominating pairs/targets in graphs, with structural and recognition-algorithm consequences.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Asteroidal sets/number, dominating pairs and targets, hereditary dominating-pair (HDP) graphs, strict dominating pairs, polar targets and golden triples are defined structural objects. (PDF pp. 14, 15, 16, 17, 24, 47, 53, 54, 65, 66)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 16, 26, 28: A strict dominating-pair graph must satisfy the stated diameter/asteroidal-number alternative; the converse is explicitly not asserted. Further theorems constrain how asteroidal sets meet dominating paths. (PDF pp. 29, 35, 37)
  • Theorem 29: Chordal HDP graphs are characterized by the specified absence of asteroidal triples with 3-spread, giving the stated recognition-time bound. (PDF pp. 38, 39)
  • Theorems 33, 56, 57: Additional results give a diameter bound under partition conditions on an asteroidal set, and show that a graph with dominating-target number three and a golden triple has a polar target of size three. (PDF pp. 43, 44, 65, 66)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • HDP/chordality, diameter constraints, prescribed dominating pairs/targets, and separation/spread/golden-triple conditions vary by theorem. The result for dominating-target number three is not the arbitrary-k statement. (PDF pp. 29, 39, 43, 65, 66, 67)

Prior results used

  • Earlier forbidden-subgraph characterizations and asteroidal-triple enumeration algorithms are dependencies in the recognition analysis, not newly proved from scratch by the runtime corollary. (PDF pp. 38, 39)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Graph drawings witness examples/counterexamples; the runtime guarantee is argued from structural characterization and known enumeration costs. No proof-assistant artifact was identified. (PDF pp. 29, 39, 43)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Conjecture 57.1: Conjecture 57.1 proposes the polar-target generalization for arbitrary k ≥ 2 under a distant asteroidal dominating-target hypothesis. (PDF pp. 67)
  • The conclusion identifies polynomial-time recognition for general HDP graphs and further polar-target existence criteria as unresolved directions. (PDF pp. 71)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • A recognition theorem for chordal HDP graphs does not establish polynomial recognition for all HDP graphs. The exponent stated in the dissertation depends on its cited algorithmic ingredients. (PDF pp. 39, 71)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: encode finite graphs and prove a small dominating-pair/asteroidal-set lemma before the more involved polar-target construction.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
