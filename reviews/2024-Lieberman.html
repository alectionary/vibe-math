Making Sandwiches: A Novel Invariant in D-module Theory

David Lieberman · 2024 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Develops sandwich Bernstein–Sato functional equations and uses them, together with linear simplicity, to prove dimension/finiteness consequences for differential-operator rings.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Differential-operator rings, filtered-module dimension, Bernstein’s inequality, linear simplicity and sandwich Bernstein–Sato polynomials are explicitly defined. Sandwich equations use composition on both sides of multiplication by a power of f. (PDF pp. 49, 51, 56, 57, 77, 78, 79, 80)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 4.7: A finite-dimensional linearly simple filtered algebra satisfies the stated Bernstein-type lower bound on dimension of a nonzero filtered module. (PDF pp. 57, 58)
  • Theorems 4.17 and 4.19: Veronese differential-operator rings have a quantified linear-simplicity result for the specified filtration. (PDF pp. 69, 70, 71, 72)
  • Theorems 6.17 and 6.24: A suitable sandwich polynomial with no nonnegative integer roots implies linear simplicity under the singular-locus/localization hypotheses. The Segre-ring application proves Bernstein’s inequality for the stated filtered modules and existence of ordinary Bernstein–Sato polynomials for all its elements. (PDF pp. 99, 100, 101, 108, 109)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The sandwich chapter works over a characteristic-zero field. Finiteness, grading, nonzero-module, filtration-compatibility, regular-element and localization hypotheses are essential where invoked. (PDF pp. 57, 77, 99, 108, 109)

Prior results used

  • Classical Weyl-algebra/Bernstein results, Kashiwara’s polynomial theorem and the cited links between linear simplicity and Bernstein inequalities form the background. Cited numbered results may be reproduced with proof and still be prior work. (PDF pp. 20, 49, 75, 76, 99)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Explicit differential-operator functional equations and divisibility/root calculations are conventional symbolic proofs. This review did not independently recompute or formally verify them. (PDF pp. 77, 78, 79, 108, 109)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Questions 3.27/4.5 ask about the general scope of Bernstein’s inequality; Question 6.2 asks for sandwich functional equations. Later sections establish sufficient criteria and families, rather than an unrestricted affirmative answer for every algebra and element. (PDF pp. 51, 56, 78, 99, 108, 109)
  • Remark 6.23: The Segre example bounds the possible sandwich polynomial without selecting between all displayed multiplicity alternatives; the dimension conclusion does not require resolving that ambiguity. (PDF pp. 108, 109)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Ordinary and sandwich Bernstein–Sato polynomials are distinct invariants. Existence of one does not automatically establish the precise other polynomial, and a conditional theorem cannot be applied before its root/localization hypotheses are checked. (PDF pp. 77, 78, 99, 108)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize the one-variable Weyl commutator and the sandwich equation for f = x before the general filtration argument.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
