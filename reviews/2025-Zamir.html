Interpolation in Weighted Projective Spaces

Shahriyar Roshan Zamir · 2025 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Studies Hilbert functions and interpolation by general simple/double points in weighted projective spaces, proving weighted analogues and exception results.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Weighted projective spaces, general points, symbolic powers for multiple points, Hilbert functions, the AH property, and secant varieties organize the problem. (PDF pp. 10, 36, 37, 38, 46, 87)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 5.1: General simple points impose the expected number of conditions in every degree over an infinite field. (PDF pp. 46, 47, 48)
  • Theorems 7.1 and 7.4: General double points in P(1,2,3) have expected Hilbert function in every degree, with no exceptions, under the stated characteristic exclusions. A weighted Terracini induction is a key ingredient. (PDF pp. 58, 59, 60, 61, 62)
  • Theorems 8.16 and 8.20: For P(1,b,c), a sufficiently high degree gives expected double-point interpolation; the stated bound is d ≥ 10c, with a stronger alternative under an additional numerical condition. The thesis also characterizes P(1,2,3) as the exception-free plane in its family. (PDF pp. 89, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • “General” refers to the algebraic-geometric parameter-space condition, not an arbitrary chosen point set. Weights and field conditions matter: Theorem 5.1 assumes an infinite field and Theorem 7.1 excludes characteristics 2, 3, and 5. Later statements inherit their chapter setup. (PDF pp. 10, 46, 58, 60, 89)

Prior results used

  • The classical Alexander–Hirschowitz theorem and Terracini/secant-variety methods are prior results, generalized or adapted here. (PDF pp. 14, 17, 60, 79, 87)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Finite base cases and worked Hilbert-function examples support induction. Calculated examples are not proof-assistant verification, and generic conclusions must be justified by the parameter-space arguments. (PDF pp. 46, 58, 59, 60)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Chapter 9: The final chapter lists explicit questions about generators of general-point ideals in dimension at least three, boundedness of the defect D(X,d), sharper/extended weighted interpolation statements, and associated Veronese/secant geometry. (PDF pp. 95, 96)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • The no-exceptions theorem is not asserted for all weighted planes; much of the dissertation analyzes exceptions and high-degree thresholds. (PDF pp. 58, 89, 93, 95)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: compute and prove a small weighted monomial-count/Hilbert-function base case with the characteristic parameter explicit.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
