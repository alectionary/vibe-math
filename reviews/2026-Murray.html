Bass Numbers of Veronese Submodules and Lifting the Frobenius Trace

Taylor Jeffrey Murray · 2026 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Two algebraic projects: transfer of Bass-number finiteness through Veronese functors, and a characteristic-two obstruction to lifting Frobenius trace on symmetric determinantal rings.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • Graded injective resolutions, Bass numbers, Veronese modules, local cohomology, differential operators, and Frobenius trace are defined and developed. (PDF pp. 16, 30, 39, 57, 64, 66, 90, 99)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorems 10–12; Corollary 10: Under the graded-generation hypotheses, finite Bass numbers pass from M over R to the Veronese module over the Veronese subring. The thesis gives formulas away from the homogeneous maximal ideal and applications to local cohomology. (PDF pp. 66, 67, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74)
  • Theorem 8: A graded Matlis-duality statement relates graded Betti numbers to Bass numbers, furnishing a tool for the finiteness argument. (PDF pp. 57, 58)
  • Theorems 16, 18, 19: For symmetric determinantal rings in the characteristic-two setting, the Frobenius trace does not lift to the indicated differential operator over the integral ring. The proof uses a nonzero local-cohomology obstruction and explicit differential operators. (PDF pp. 101, 102, 106, 133, 134, 135)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • The Veronese results require a positively graded finitely generated algebra with generator degrees coprime to the chosen positive integer, and a graded module with finite Bass numbers. The trace project is in characteristic two, with generic symmetric matrices of size at least three. (PDF pp. 3, 66, 67, 101)

Prior results used

  • Injective decomposition, graded Matlis duality, finiteness theorems of Lyubeznik/Huneke–Sharp, and Jeffries–Singh obstruction results are recalled or used as dependencies. (PDF pp. 16, 57, 90, 100, 101)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Explicit differential-operator and determinant calculations occur inside conventional algebraic proofs. They are not evidence of proof-assistant verification. (PDF pp. 106, 133, 134)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • The Smith–Van den Bergh simplicity conjecture is discussed as background. A lifting question is also discussed together with known negative results; it must not be presented simply as an unanswered question. (PDF pp. 99, 100)
  • Question 1: Question 1 about Veronese Bass numbers is answered under the dissertation’s hypotheses by Theorems 10–12, rather than left open. (PDF pp. 66, 67, 74)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Theorem 16 uses p in its statement while the immediately preceding text specifies p = 2. The review preserves that characteristic-two scope; an all-primes reading would overstate the contribution. (PDF pp. 100, 101)
  • The Bass-number formula excludes the homogeneous maximal ideal; finiteness is proved there without the same displayed formula. (PDF pp. 67, 74)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: a small Veronese grading example or a characteristic-two determinant identity, keeping the p = 2 parameter explicit.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
