Partitions of R^n with Maximal Seclusion and their Applications to Reproducible Computation

Jason Vander Woude · 2023 · Mathematics

Source record · Local PDF · Original PDF

Constructs optimally secluded cube partitions and proves lower bounds and generalizations, then applies the geometry to reproducible/randomized computation and learning.

Review scope. Principal-result and evidence map, not an independent proof-correctness audit or a claim-by-claim complete classification. PDF pages are 1-based viewer pages including cover sheets. Open status is as described in the dissertation, not checked against later literature.

Proof-assistant formalization

No proof-assistant verification identified in the reviewed PDF. PDF-only evidence search and document review; no external formalization repositories audited. Mathematical formalism, computer algebra, and algorithmic proofs are not counted as kernel-checked proofs.

Mathematical setup and definitions

  • A (k, epsilon)-secluded partition limits how many cells any specified l-infinity ball can meet. Axis alignment, unit-cube versus diameter/volume restrictions, open/closed neighborhoods, and pseudodeterministic algorithms are separate definitions. (PDF pp. 3, 38, 39, 40, 101, 152, 153, 173, 361)

Results proved or derived in the dissertation

  • Theorem 4.2.18; Theorem 5.1.1; Corollary 9.8.5: Explicit efficiently computable axis-aligned unit-cube partitions achieve k = d + 1 and epsilon = 1/(2d). The lower bound on k and the exact epsilon optimum for that cube class are proved separately. (PDF pp. 45, 46, 102, 123, 124, 125, 333, 334, 335, 336, 337, 338, 339, 340)
  • Theorems 6.2.1 and 7.1.9: For broader bounded-volume partitions, the work proves k ≥ d + 1 and a logarithmic-over-dimension epsilon bound. These are weaker than exact cube-class optimality, and have different hypotheses. (PDF pp. 152, 153, 158, 173, 184, 190)
  • Theorems 8.0.7–8.0.8, 9.7.4, 10.3.4, 10.3.10: Further proved results include neighborhood KKM/Sperner variants, equivalences for cube partitions, efficient rounding applications, and lower bounds for pseudodeterministic coin/threshold estimation. (PDF pp. 223, 224, 230, 239, 321, 361, 370)

Hypotheses and modeling assumptions

  • Each optimum is relative to an object class and norm/normalization. General partitions require diameter/outer-measure or local-finiteness conditions as stated. Computational lower bounds depend on the algorithm and learning models, not every possible notion of reproducibility. (PDF pp. 40, 152, 153, 173, 190, 361, 365, 370)

Prior results used

  • Classical Lebesgue covering/KKM/Sperner results, Brunn–Minkowski, and Zaks’s theorem are prior inputs; the thesis explicitly offers alternative proofs or new quantitative variants in several places. (PDF pp. 123, 124, 145, 146, 147, 180, 317)

Computational and empirical evidence

  • Finite clique computations motivate conjectures about alternative partitions; examples and efficient algorithms do not settle their asymptotic conjectures. (PDF pp. 107, 110, 115, 118)

Questions, conjectures and remaining work

  • Conjectures 4.3.1, 4.4.1, 7.3.1–7.3.3, 9.2.13, 9.6.7, 9.7.9, 10.2.17: Conjectures include stronger optimal epsilon bounds for arbitrary diameter-bounded cells, clique behavior/bifurcation for particular constructions, extension of cube enclosures, additional equivalences, and randomized-rounding limitations. (PDF pp. 107, 110, 216, 217, 218, 266, 320, 324, 360)
  • Question 8.3.2 asks about the range of the neighborhood-color functions; improving quantitative lower/upper bounds remains a direction. (PDF pp. 248, 252)

Scope and interpretation notes

  • Exact epsilon optimality for axis-aligned cubes is proved, while the analogous broad diameter-bounded claim is conjectural. Theorem 10.3.4’s headline wording must be read with its precise k < d + 1 statement, not as excluding k = d + 1. (PDF pp. 102, 216, 333, 361)

Possible first formalization task

Reviewer suggestion: formalize a low-dimensional secluded partition and its finite intersection bound; preserve open versus closed ball conventions.

A suggestion for new work, not an existing formalization.
